# Addresses, senders and names come from the install

Status: accepted. Follows ADR 0004 (switches and tabs are the install's).

## What changed, and why

The operator's host, its two senders and its name were written into the code of the parts: meet's page and its call links, the inbox's push host and notification subject, forms' sender, contact address, footer and consent, and the installed app's name. A second company's install would have sent signing mail as the operator's domain (which its account cannot), linked a guest to the operator's space and called its people the operator's people. Each now comes from the declaration.

- **One origin.** Every link the parts make (a call link, a guest's door, a signing link, a notification's subject) is on the install's space host (`Install.url()`). Forms' signing core and meet's wiring derive it from the install, so `APP_ORIGIN` and `MEET_APP_ORIGIN` are gone from the Worker's settings and from forms' and meet's environments: no setting can disagree with the host the Worker is served at. Meet's pages take their origin from the address they are opened at (they exist only on the space's host).
- **One push host, where Gmail is on** (`Install.gmailPushHost()`): the inbox's push door, the Worker's choice of what answers on it, and the mail server's push address read it; the constant is gone. The calendar's push URL stays a var, which the install kit writes (ADR 0007) from the declaration's push host and the calendar's path: there is no hand-kept copy.
- **Senders** (`senders: { forms, meet }`): the addresses Forms' and Meet's mail goes out from, each on the company's own domain (the Worker's send binding allows exactly them). Left out, `forms@<domain>` and `meet@<domain>`. Read with `Install.sender(app)`.
- **The signing service** (`formsService: { name, contact, runBy: { name, legalName, mailingLine, site } }`): what Forms is called ("Northfield Forms", which "via" follows in a signer's mail and a seal's subject begins with), the address a person writes to about the service and their information, and who runs it (its short name, its legal name, the mailing line of the email footer, its site). Left out, "<company> Forms", the first owner's address, and the company runs it. Forms' consent, terms, footer and privacy notice keep their sentences; only these names are the install's, so no legal wording is written (the one exception is `ownCompanyOnly`, below, which picks between two wordings Forms owns). Read with `Install.formsService()`. A company whose Forms another company runs declares who. `ownCompanyOnly: true` (left out, false, by every install that sends for its clients) says the install's Forms sends only for its own company, as a company's space does when it runs Forms for that company alone: there is then no second party, so a signer sees no "via <service>" and no "the service, not a party", and the landing page, the privacy notice, the consent, the email footer and the certificate read as the company using its own signing service, with Cloudflare still named as host. The company must be who runs the service, by its name and its legal name (a declaration naming another runner is refused), and Forms refuses to set signing up for any business but the company. Set when the install is created and never flipped on a live install: sent documents keep what their signers were shown, but a business sent as a client would be re-described as the company. Forms owns the sentences; this is the only switch for them.
- **Words.** Screens and mail say the company's name (`Install.company.name`) where they once said the operator's: meet's teammate tag, its list wording and privacy note, the phone's labels and mail, the chat's settings, the notification title and its permission text, the installed app's `name` ("<company> Space", written by the Worker over the one manifest file). They ask the owner's name and address from the owner (`Install.owner()`). The guests' pages, which no one is signed in to, ask the company and the owner's name of `GET /w/api/site`, and a signed-in guest is told the owner's address in the bootstrap.
- **Not changed.** Every deployed identity (meet's store object name, the seal's root and seal certificate names, every Worker, store, bucket, queue and token name) and an existing install's declaration values, which gain exactly the senders and the service it already had. The seal script names a new install's certificates "<service name> Root" and "<service name> Seal", so an install whose seal was made before the service name was declared has the same names.

## What stays constant, and why

The phone's caller name, greetings and screening (`LINE` in meet's `phone-line.ts`) and meet's refusal table for the phone are one install's content: the phone is out of scope for any other install for now, so they stay constants until a second install has a phone. The dev stand-ins and fixtures keep their invented people and hosts. The deploy's settings file, the assistant's personas and spam questions and the install kit are ADRs 0006 and 0007.
