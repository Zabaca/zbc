# The space checks the sign-in once, and the parts that run in its Worker are handed the person

Status: accepted. **Supersedes [0001](0001-the-apps-verify-the-assertion-the-space-forwards.md) for every part that runs in the space's Worker (forms, the knowledge app, meet and the inbox).** [0002](0002-one-service-token-comes-through-the-space-on-one-path-to-one-app.md) holds as written.

**Amended for the inbox by [the inbox's ADR 0004](../../../inbox/docs/adr/0004-every-mailbox-has-people-and-a-person-sees-only-theirs.md):** the rule the inbox kept after the check (that it admits the install's owner and nobody else) no longer holds as written. The inbox admits the owner always and any other person of the install who is on at least one mailbox, shows them only the mail of the mailboxes they are on, and keeps what changes the install's setup the owner's alone. The check being the space's, once per request, is unchanged.

## What changed, and why

0001 made every app behind the space verify the Access assertion the space forwards, because each app was a Worker of its own with a trust boundary of its own: the space was a front door that held nothing and could be wrong. The decision here is one app: the space's Worker runs the other parts' code, in-process, over the stores it owns or binds. There is no second Worker to be a boundary. **There is one trust boundary, the one Worker, as each app was for itself before the space existed.** The per-app audience variable (`SPACE_ACCESS_AUD`) goes with the apps that moved; the audience a request is checked against is the Worker's own (`CF_ACCESS_AUD`, and the Forms application's on `/forms`, see the space's ADR 0002).

## The rule

1. **One check per request.** The space's Worker verifies the Access assertion on every request, header only, never the cookie, against the audience of the application that answered the path (the layer's `identify`, the space package's `src/app.ts`, `whoIsAsking`). A request with no verified person never reaches a part. A service token is never a person, as before (Claude's one exception is 0002's, answered before this check, on its own path).
2. **The check's answer is the argument.** A part that runs in the Worker is handed `SignedIn` (`signedIn(person, exp)` in `asking.ts`), the person that check named and when the assertion stops being good, as the first argument of every method, where an app behind a binding was handed the `Assertion`. It cannot be called without one, and nothing but the check makes one: the compiler refuses a bare address, a `Person` read from the declaration or an `Asker` from `identifyForSpace`. Forms' operator API, which is a whole HTTP app, is handed the operator the check named through a `WeakMap` keyed by the very request it is handed, and answers 401 to a request the map does not hold; it verifies nothing and is not given an audience.
3. **Each part keeps its own rule about who may do what.** The knowledge app admits the install's owner; forms admits an operator (a person of this install). They answer `not-admitted` to a signed-in person who is not let in, as they did. This is a decision of the part, after the check, and stays the part's.
4. **No part is given the assertion, the audience or the key fetch.** The assertion is held for the length of one request by the Worker that received it. Fewer places hold it, and fewer places can fetch Cloudflare's keys: a key fetch in flight is shared by the checks that arrive together, and a Today load that made nine checks (its own and one in each of eight reads) when every app checked for itself makes one now that every part is in the Worker.
5. **Local development** (`vite dev`, localhost, the dev switch) hands the parts a person of no install; fixtures stand in for them, and a real part refuses.

## What this gives and costs

- **Gives:** one check where there were up to five; the sign-in refusals Today once showed from three apps at once (a race in the key fetch, which the layer's check now shares in flight) cannot be repeated by an app, because there is no app to repeat them; a part cannot be asked for a person the check did not name.
- **Costs:** the Worker holds what every part holds. A bug in a part's code runs with the Worker's bindings, which is the cost of one app that this decision accepts; the guard against it is the types (rule 2), the tests that drive the real parts through the real router (the space package's `test/in-process.test.ts`), and a part's own rule (rule 3).
- **The inbox and meet:** each was called over a service binding and verified the forwarded assertion against the space's audience, exactly as 0001 said. Their move replaced `Assertion` with `SignedIn` in their methods (`InboxForSpace`, `MeetForSpace`) and deleted their copies of the check, and the old `SpaceInbox`, `ClaudeInbox` and `SpaceMeet` entrypoints (which took the assertion, verified it and called the same methods) are gone. Claude's door (0002) is checked once in the space and its request handed to the inbox's router in-process with the token that check verified, and no assertion. Meet's part runs in the Worker (the meet package's `src/space-part.ts`). The workspace package's `test/space-contract.test.ts` pins which interface takes which.
- **Reading an older note:** a note that says an app "verifies the assertion itself against `SPACE_ACCESS_AUD`" is 0001's and is true of no app now. The knowledge app's and forms' entrypoints for the space (`SpaceKnowledge`, `SpaceForms`) were how the space reached them before they moved in and are deleted; where they were kept, and why, is the space's ADR 0015.

## What this does not decide

How the guest paths are checked (a call's name, a guest's session, a signer's token, each on its own path, with the Worker answering a guest path before the person check and a team path never without it) and how the live object checks a socket's person. The machine host for Gmail's push. Those are their own records' and extend this rule; none relaxes it.
