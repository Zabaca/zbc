# The apps verify the assertion the space forwards, and answer with a reply that tells a refusal from a failure

Status: accepted for the shapes it sets; the forwarded-assertion rule is superseded (below).

**Superseded by [0003](0003-the-space-checks-the-sign-in-once-and-the-parts-in-its-worker-are-handed-the-person.md) for the forwarded assertion.** Forms and the knowledge app, then meet and the inbox, now run in the space's Worker and are handed the person its one check named. The forwarded-assertion rule is therefore true of no app: every part is in the space's Worker, and the Workers that once received an assertion (`meet`, `forms`, `knowledge`, `inbox`) are deleted. The rest of what this record says (the shape of a reply, a refusal told from a failure, writes going out at most once, the compose key) is unchanged and applies to every part, in the Worker or behind a binding.

## Who is asking

The space draws Today, Conversations and People from the inbox, meet and forms, and each app has its own idea of who may see what (the inbox admits its owner only; meet admits the Teammate list; forms admits its operators). The space asks as the signed-in person. The question is how an app comes to believe it.

**The apps never trust a person the space names.** The space passes along the Access assertion it received, as a string, as the first argument of every entrypoint method (`Assertion`). Each app verifies it itself, with the company layer's header-only check (`identifyForSpace`, over `identifyAssertion`, the same code as `identify`), against the **space application's audience**, read from the var `SPACE_ACCESS_AUD` (`SPACE_AUD_VAR`) beside the app's own `CF_ACCESS_AUD`. The value is the `aud` output of the **`space-access`** zbc instance (the install's `space-access` instance file), the space's Access application in an instance of its own, so the one tag reaches the space (as `CF_ACCESS_AUD`) and the three apps (as `SPACE_ACCESS_AUD`, each `{ from: 'space-access', output: 'aud' }`, wired by their builders). It was an `access` block inside the space's instance on its first apply; `space-access` adopts that application by its domain and makes no second. Then the app applies its own visibility rules to the person it found: that is a second, separate step, and it stays the app's. The forwarded Forms pages take the same rule, so forms checks an assertion for the space's audience whether it arrives as a header on a forwarded request or as an argument on a method.

What this gives, and why this rather than "the space says who it is":

- **A compromised or buggy space cannot invent a person.** It can only hand on an assertion Cloudflare signed for someone who passed the space's own door. It cannot ask the inbox for the owner's mail as another address, because there is no field to put another address in.
- **Fails closed at every step, as the layer's check does.** No assertion, a forged, expired or other-team one, one for another application, a service token, one naming both a person and a token, a missing or malformed audience: all `not-signed-in`, with the same words. A verified person who is not of this install is `not-admitted`, even though Cloudflare signed them. The difference between the first group's causes goes to the Worker's tail and nowhere else.
- **No service token comes through the space, with one exception ([ADR 0002](0002-one-service-token-comes-through-the-space-on-one-path-to-one-app.md)):** Claude's one token, on one path (`/agent/inbox`), handed to the inbox, never treated as a person. Everywhere else the rule stands: no token is a person of the space, none reaches meet, forms or the knowledge app, and `identifyForSpace`, which every method of every entrypoint uses, does not allow tokens. Before that exception Claude's script reached the inbox on the inbox's own address with its own token (see the inbox package's `CLAUDE.md`).
- **The audience is checked, so an assertion is not a master key.** An assertion for the inbox's own application does not open `SpaceMeet`, and one for the space does not open the inbox's own address (that address checks its own audience).
- **The entrypoint is not reachable except by a binding.** A service-binding entrypoint has no route and no hostname, so only a Worker in the account with the binding can call it. The assertion check is not what keeps the internet out; it is what keeps the space honest about who it acts for.

Costs and limits, stated: the space holds an assertion for the length of the request that forwards it, and could in principle use it for anything the person could do in an app until it expires (the same reach it has when it forwards a Forms page, which this rule accepts). A person whose Access session is revoked stops working at the assertion's expiry, not at the next request, which is how Access itself behaves. An app that wants a shorter window checks `exp` (returned by `identifyForSpace`) against its own limit; meet already does this for its socket ticket, which ends with the assertion that made it.

If the stores make this unworkable, the thing to say is why, not to change it. They do not: the inbox already builds a `Who` from a verified identity (`roleFor`), meet builds `{ via: 'access', email }` from a verified address, and forms builds an `Operator` from a person. Each only needs its input to come from the string instead of from a request.

## What an answer looks like

Every method of every entrypoint answers a `SpaceReply<T>`: `{ ok: true, value }`, a `Refused` or a `Failed`, as data. An exception thrown across a service binding arrives as a message with its type and fields gone, and the space must tell a decision from a fault.

- A **refusal** is a decision: the app knew who was asking, or could tell the request was wrong. Reasons: `not-signed-in`, `not-admitted`, `not-found` (nonexistent and not-theirs are never told apart), `bad-request`, `conflict` (the draft or the thread moved since the caller saw it), `limit`. It carries a sentence the person may be shown. The space shows the other parts and says nothing about being unreachable: mail that does not admit a teammate is not mail that is down.
- A **failure** is a fault, and there are two types so that no app can claim what only the space can know. `Failed` has one reason, `broken`: the app was reached and something it depends on failed; the app catches its own faults and returns it, and on a write it means the app knows nothing went out. `Unanswered` is made by the space alone, from what it saw: `unreachable` (the binding threw), `timed-out` (a read not answered within `ANSWER_WITHIN_MS`, 4 seconds), `not-understood` (not the contract's shape, never half read) and `outcome-unknown` (a write it lost track of). The space shows that **part** as could not be reached while the others work.

The Conversations list (`ConversationList`) therefore carries a status per part, so a refusal and a failure look different on screen as they do in the data.

## Writes go out at most once

The 4 second wait is for **reads only**. A write (the inbox's `send`, `markRead`, `closeConversations` and `reopenConversations`, meet's `post` and `markRead`) is awaited up to `WRITE_WITHIN_MS` (30 seconds), past the inbox's own wait of 25 seconds for Gmail (`SEND_TIMEOUT_MS`), so the app's answer arrives before the space stops listening. If the space still loses track of a write (the binding throws, or it gives up), it shows **outcome unknown**, points at the app's own view of what went out, and **never offers a retry**.

**The exception is a write that is idempotent and re-guarded:** the inbox's `markRead`, `closeConversations` and `reopenConversations` and meet's `markRead`. Doing one twice is doing it once (a conversation already read or closed asks Gmail for nothing), and the position each names (`through`) is checked again on every call, so a second press can never act on mail the person has not seen. A lost answer to one of these is therefore not an unknown outcome: the space asks on the read path (`reach`, which waits `WRITE_WITHIN_MS`), reads the conversation again when no answer comes, shows how it stands, and the person may press it again. Nothing is sent and nothing is said to a person outside, which is what makes "never retry" the rule for the others.

That alone does not make a send safe, because the inbox's checks do not stop a repeat: they look only at inbound mail after `through` (the inbox package's `src/store/mail.ts`), so a second press with the same `through` makes a second draft and sends a second email. So every `send` variant and every `post` carries a **compose key** (`ComposeKey`), chosen once per message being written by the space. The app **holds** the key, for at least a day, only while its first use may have sent something: in progress, sent, or the inbox's own `sending` (Gmail never said). A second use of a held key answers a `Repeated` refusal: nothing new is drafted, sent or posted, and `earlier` carries the first outcome, or says it is still `in-progress` (a repeat that arrives while Gmail is being waited on). An in-progress record older than `WRITE_WITHIN_MS` ends: it answers as done with status `sending`, matching the inbox's stuck state, so "sending" is never shown for good.

A first use that was **refused releases the key**, because the app records nothing for a refusal; so does a `Failed`, since an app returns `broken` for a write only when it knows nothing went out. Without this rule the inbox's own `conflict` ("a new message arrived, read it and press Send again") would use up the key, and nothing could ever be sent from that compose; meet's `limit` hits the same wall. When the app cannot tell (Gmail timed out mid-send) it answers success with `sending` and holds the key. `markRead`, `closeConversations` and `reopenConversations` are idempotent and take no key. **The inbox's and meet's tests must each prove both cases: a refused first use followed by the same key sends, and a sent, in-progress or `sending` first use followed by the same key answers `Repeated` and sends nothing new.**

A draft is sent with the `through` of its thread's own `InboxThread`, not one the draft carries: the space opens the thread first (asking with `include` for a closed or Bulk one), so a send cannot name how far someone read a thread nobody opened and get past the inbox's rule that nothing is answered unseen.

## The live socket comes through the space

Meet's socket refuses a request whose Origin is not its own URL's origin. A page on the space that opened a websocket to meet's own host directly would be refused with a 403. A Worker that forwards the browser's upgrade request unchanged (`binding.fetch(request)`) reaches meet with the caller's hostname, so the same-origin check passes (shown live, into a plain Worker and on into a Durable Object with hibernation, over a 90 second hold). So the socket is always opened on the space's own origin: the ticket names the path (`socketPath`, meet's `/api/w/socket?ticket=`), and **`SpaceMeet` implements `fetch` for that one path and answers 404 to everything else**, so the space keeps one binding to meet and cannot reach meet's other routes through it. There is no meet address in the ticket.

## What this does not decide

How the space shows each state, and the wording of the notice. How an app maps its own errors onto the reasons (the inbox's store refusals 403, 404, 409, 422 and 503; meet's `RoomError` 400, 401, 403, 404, 409, 429 and 502): each app's builder does, and the reasons are the ones these map onto. The merge of the rows, which is the space's.
