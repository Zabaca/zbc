# calendar

The owner's Google calendars, read into the space and kept current: a library with no Worker of its own, run by the space's Worker (the Calendar tab with its agenda, week and month views, Today's events, Settings, Calendars). Claude reads the free time from it and makes, moves and cancels events through it, from the inbox's router and under the send rule (the space's `docs/adr/0023`); and its own alarm keeps a "Busy" block on each of the owner's other calendars for every meeting that makes them busy, when the owner has turned that on (the space's `docs/adr/0024`). The booking links (the space's ADR 0025: the owner's links, the free times, and the event a visitor's booking makes, moves and deletes) are the third thing that writes to Google, from the same object; the booking page itself is the space package's `booking/`. The store keeps three months back and half a year ahead (`CALENDAR_BACK_MS`, `CALENDAR_AHEAD_MS` in the workspace package's `src/calendar.ts`). What it is: `README.md`. Terms: `CONTEXT.md`. Why it is built this way, the token choice and what Google's documents say about push: the space's `docs/adr/0020`. The steps an apply cannot do: `docs/runbooks/calendar-google-setup.md`.

## Working here

- **It is a COPY of Google's calendars, so nothing in it is the only copy of anything.** `CalendarDO` (`src/durable.ts`, the one file that touches the Workers runtime) is a new class declared under `exports` in the Worker's settings (the infra package's `src/space-kit/worker-settings.ts`) (no `state`, nothing transferred, like `LiveDO`), found by `CALENDAR_NAME` in `src/store-access.ts`. A lost or replaced store costs a full read of each calendar and nothing else.
- **The layers are the knowledge app's and the inbox's:** `schema.ts` (migrations as code, appended and never edited, `test/schema.test.ts` pins the count), `store.ts` (every rule about what is kept and how it is read back, over a SQL port, no runtime), `google.ts` (the one place that speaks to Google: the sync's client, which lists, watches and stops and holds a `calendar.readonly` token, and the writer, which inserts, patches and deletes an event with a `calendar.events` token asked for each call, or for each account once in a run of the busy blocks), `writes.ts` (what Claude's invite, move and cancel do, and what they refuse, and the free time Claude reads: no runtime, and no rule about WHO may write, which is the inbox's), `inbox.ts` (the face the inbox is handed for Claude's script, `CalendarForInbox`), `sync.ts` (the order of operations around the awaits), `engine.ts` (what the object does: the methods the space calls and the alarm), `space.ts` (the owner-only face the space calls in-process: besides the tab's reads, `eventsByUid`, which finds the events an email's calendar card names by iCalendar id and occurrence, from the store alone, for the owner's "Open in Calendar" and the title and time a bare answer lacks), `blocks.ts` (the busy blocks: the plan worked out again from the store at every run, then as many writes as the pace allows; no runtime, and no rule about who may write, which is the owner's switch), `bookings.ts` (the booking links: what the owner sets and its checks, the free times offered, and a visitor's booking, move and cancellation, with the hold, the check with Google, the derived event id and the limits; no runtime, and no rule about who may book, which is the link's and the booking's secret), `booking-slots.ts` (the starting times of a link: a pure function of its rules, the clock and what is busy), `booking-store.ts` (the links and the record of what they did, over the same storage), `guests.ts` (the third face, `CalendarForGuests`, for the open path), `push-door.ts` (Google's notification, answered by the space's Worker on the push host), `event-parse.ts` (Google's event into what is kept, with every piece of untrusted text cut and stripped).
- **The contract is in the workspace package's `src/calendar.ts`, not here.** A method added to a face is added there first (`test/space-contract.test.ts`). There are THREE faces: the space's (`CalendarForSpace`, owner only: reads, and settings, none of which writes an event at Google: `setShown`, `setBlocking`, `setBlockingAccount` and the four booking-link settings store a choice and owe the alarm a run), the visitor's (`CalendarForGuests`, `src/guests.ts`: no person, authorised by a link's name or a booking's secret, handed to the booking door of the space's Worker and to nothing else) and the inbox's (`CalendarForInbox`: `events`, `invite`, `move`, `cancel`, taking no person). The inbox's face is handed to the inbox's part and to nothing else (the space package's `src/parts.ts`), and a method added to it is a method Claude can call: the rule in front of the three writes (the owner's switch and the owner's instruction, recorded) is the INBOX's, applied in its router before the calendar is asked, so a new write goes through that router and never has a path of its own.
- **Reads get a reading token; only a write Claude makes on the owner's instruction, the busy blocks the owner's own switch keeps and a visitor's booking through a link the owner made get the permission to change events.** Claude's: for that one call. The blocks': one token for each account in a run, asked by the run's writing client (`createRunWriter`, over the same `inbox.access(address, fresh, 'events')`, in-process, so the inbox is still not imported) and dropped by `end()` when the run ends. Nothing but `writes.ts` (Claude's three), `blocks.ts` (the alarm's) and `bookings.ts` (a visitor's booking, move and cancellation, each asking for its own token with the writer that drops it) may ask for one. Connect calendar asks Google for `calendar.readonly` and `calendar.events` so each account is connected once and without a second consent; "calendar connected" still means `calendar.readonly`, and `calendar.events` is recorded (`CalendarGrant.events`, `account.events`, migration 1), hinted in Settings and shown to Claude as `canEdit`. The token the sync is handed is `calendar.readonly` alone: `src/durable.ts` passes `[address, fresh]`, so the inbox's `calendarAccess` level defaults to `read`. Claude's events token (`level: 'events'`) is asked for by the writer (`createCalendarWriter`) for each call and dropped after it: nothing caches it, and the blocks' run writer keeps one only inside one run. A write changes the store only after Google has answered it (`putEvent`, `dropEvent`) and marks the calendar owed a read, so `events` shows it at once and the next incremental read confirms it.
- **No refresh token, ever.** The grant is the inbox's: the calendar asks the inbox's store, in-process, who is connected (`calendarGrants`) and for an access token limited to the calendar permission (`calendarAccess`). Nothing here may log, store or return a token; `test/read.test.ts` ("what never reaches a screen") and `test/schema.test.ts` hold that. This package must not import the inbox (its source needs the inbox's declaration alias to compile): the inbox's object name is written again in `store-access.ts` and the space package's `test/wrangler.test.ts` holds the two equal.
- **A write is one request and its outcome may be unknown.** A fault that is not a decision (Google did not answer) is thrown to the face, which answers it as `failed` with a sentence that says to read the calendar before trying again, because Google may have made the event. Nothing here retries a write, and an account is refused in words (the inbox's own sentences) before Google is asked when it is lapsed, disconnected or without the permission.
- **Event text is untrusted.** Anything new that carries text from Google goes through `cleanText` or `htmlToText` in `event-parse.ts`, and a link is kept only through `httpsLink` or `googleLink`.
- **Secrets and vars:** no secret. `CALENDAR_PUSH_URL` is a var of the space's Worker settings (the install kit's output); `INBOX_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` and its secret are the inbox's (the calendar has no client of its own).

## Checks

In a worktree, after `bun install` at the root, from the calendar package: `bun test`, `bun run typecheck`. From the workspace, inbox and space packages: `bun test` and `bun run typecheck` (the inbox also `bun run check`); from the space package `bun run test:e2e` (or `bun test e2e/calendar.test.ts e2e/calendar-lapsed.test.ts`, with `CALENDAR_SHOTS=<dir>` for the screens at 1440 and 390 pixels; and `bun test e2e/booking.test.ts`, with `BOOKING_SHOTS=<dir>`, for the booking page and Settings' card). A worktree has no Google and no Cloudflare: the sync is proven over a fake Google (`test-support/fake-google.ts`, a `fetch` that answers as Google's documents say), and the real one is the runbook's hand test.

## Where it breaks

- **A held time is let go by a read, not by the clock.** A booking waiting on Google keeps its time (`BookingStore.holds`) until the copy shows its event, or an hour has passed AND the calendar has been read successfully since (`CalendarBookings.run`); a move whose answer was lost is settled the same way (the event's times become the booking's and the move is counted). A booking whose event the copy does not hold yet stays `held`, never `booked`, so its time is not offered in between.
- **The per-address and per-network limits count canonical keys** (`canonicalAddress`: no `+tag`, no dots for Gmail's domains; `networkKeyOf`: an IPv6 address by its /64), and every refusal for who the visitor is or how much they used the link is one sentence and one reason (`NOT_NOW_WORDS`), given after the slot check. The visitor's note is never in the event's description (only in the booking's record, which the owner reads in Settings).
- **The time is held in the same turn as the check that it is free.** `CalendarBookings.book` does everything that needs an await (the hashes, the ids) BEFORE it reads the free times and writes the hold's row, and writes the row with no await between: that is what keeps two visitors from one time, in an object that takes one request at a time between awaits. Do not put an await between `slotsFor` and `addBooking`; `test/bookings.test.ts` ("two visitors who ask for one time at the same moment") holds it.
- **A booking's call is made once, after the hold and Google's check and before the event, and a failed booking leaves none open** (ADR 0026): the key is the booking's id; a refused event closes it, a held time let go closes it first (the hold stays while Meet does not answer), and a cancel or a move Meet did not hear is found by the alarm (`callsBehind`, the booking's `call_start`). The calendar imports neither the inbox nor Meet: `BookingCalls` is a port the space's `CalendarDO` subclass fills in.
- **A booking's event id is derived from the booking's id, and the secret is never stored.** An insert whose outcome is unknown keeps the hold and is found again by the id (never re-sent: the description with the secret cannot be made again). The secret is kept only as a hash (`secretHash`), the visitor's network address only as a hash keyed with a value made on first use, and `parseEvent` leaves the secret out of every stored description (`withoutManageSecret`). A new place that reads an event's description in must go through `parseEvent`, or the secret is stored.
- **A block is the calendar's own and never anything else's.** Every read of `event` for a screen or for Claude leaves out `block IS NOT NULL` (`agenda`, `event`, `eventsByUid`, `held`, `copyOn`); a new read of that table must too, or a block is drawn, counted busy or made into a block of its own. `test/blocks.test.ts` ("a block is the calendar's own") holds it.
- **The blocks' plan is a function of the store, never a list kept.** `blocks.ts` works out what should exist and what does exist each run, so a crash or a write whose outcome was lost leaves nothing to repair. Do not add state that a run must finish; the only memory is `more` (a pace), `stuck` (steps Google refused, tried last), the cached install key and the backoff in `blocking`. A block is removed for want of a meeting only when every taking-part account has been read once (`complete`), or a store made new would take down blocks that stand for meetings not yet read in.
- **A block's id is derived, and Google never forgets one.** Same install, meeting and account, same id, for ever: an insert of a removed block's id is a 409 for ever, which `blocks.ts` answers with the same body as a change that says confirmed. That, and Google giving back the private mark it was sent with, are the hand test's (the space's ADR 0024). A block is known by its mark, by the mark the store already held (`insertEvent` keeps it when a read brings none) or by its derived id AND its shape (`recognise`: titled "Busy", organised by its own account, no other guest, on the account the id was derived for, since the id is no secret and an invitation made with a guessed one must stay an ordinary event), so a Google that dropped the mark does not turn blocks into meetings; and a block of another install (the install key in the mark, hashed from the declared company id) is hidden like our own and never touched.
- **Its identity is (the SPACE Worker, class `CalendarDO`) and the object's name** (`CALENDAR_NAME`). Changing any is a new, empty calendar, which reads Google in full: nothing is lost.
- **Migrations are appended, never edited or reordered** (`src/schema.ts`). A store a newer build migrated refuses an older one, so undoing a deploy that migrated is a forward fix.
- **Keep `src/durable.ts` free of anything the space's environment would not have.** It reads `INBOX` (the inbox's store), `LIVE` (optional) and `CALENDAR_PUSH_URL` from the space's env; a moved object runs with the env of the Worker that owns the class.
- **Never keep a stub of another object.** A stub whose target was reset or aborted (the inbox's and knowledge's owner-route restarts use `ctx.abort`) is broken for good, and an object holding one fails every call until it restarts itself (this happened once in production). `inboxFor` and `engineFor` in `src/store-access.ts` get a fresh stub per call; `test/store-access.test.ts` holds it.
- **A push is a hint.** `receivePush` is one write and an alarm; never make it call Google. The channel's own secret and resource are its whole defence (Google does not sign a notification); never log a notification's headers.
- **A read that finds a calendar "owed" must always leave it either read, failed with a backoff, or not owed.** An alarm that finds something due and does nothing spins; `nextWake` and `calendarDueAt`/`listDueAt`/`channelDueAt` are the one definition of "owed", and `engine.work` never asks for an alarm sooner than a second on.
- **A sync token keeps the window of the read that made it** (an assumption from Google's pages, unverified until a real calendar has been read), so each calendar is read in full again every 7 days (`FULL_RESYNC_MS`) and a 410 reads in full at once.
- **A lapse is not retried.** The inbox marks the grant lapsed (`calendarAccess` does it when Google refuses); the calendar then asks nothing of Google for that account until the inbox says it is live again.
