# Channel admin in the space: the same rules, asked as the same teammate, and one email, once per key

Status: accepted. The first step of retiring `/start`; it follows the call work of [0006](0006-calls-in-the-space-a-call-is-a-room-with-a-record-of-what-it-is-for-and-every-link-is-meets-own.md) and is not part of it. Builds on [0003](0003-workspace-one-object-two-doors-live-sockets-emailed-codes.md) (the Workspace object, its two doors and the channel rules) and [0006](0006-calls-in-the-space-a-call-is-a-room-with-a-record-of-what-it-is-for-and-every-link-is-meets-own.md) (the keyed writes). The space's side is [packages/space ADR 0012](../../../space/docs/adr/0012-channel-admin-is-meets-answer-drawn-in-the-conversation-and-the-invitation-is-the-one-email-it-causes.md).

## What was decided

**`/start` did three things the space could not: make a channel, invite a guest, archive a channel** (and, beside them, the channel's people, a guest's access, Add, Rename, Leave, Browse and Join). They now work through `SpaceMeet`, with meet's rules unchanged and in meet's own words.

**The same rules because it is the same code, asked as the same person.** `SpaceMeet` calls the Workspace object's own channel methods (`createChannel`, `joinChannel`, `leaveChannel`, `renameChannel`, `archiveChannel`, `addMember`, `removeMember`, `invite`, `removePerson`, `deletePerson`) as `{ via: 'access', email }`, the actor the teammates' door builds, so who may archive (the maker, and the owner), who may remove a guest (the owner, or the teammate who invited them), that a teammate's address is refused as a guest, that a private channel does not exist for someone outside it, and the hour's limit on emails are not written a second time. The space gets what it needs to **draw** from one read, `channel`: the people in the channel, each with what the asker may do with them (`may`), the people who can be added, and what the asker may do with the channel. The space draws a button only where that says so; the refusal is still meet's if a button is pressed anyway.

**Scoping is tighter than a button needs, never looser.** `channel` answers only a channel the asker is in (an open one they have not joined is `browseChannels`: a name and a count). `archive` is asked in a channel the asker is in, and `remove-guest` and `delete-person` in a channel the asker is in with the person, as the members panel offered them at `/start`: a private channel the owner is not in cannot be archived, and a guest in none of the asker's channels is `not-found`, even to the owner, though the core would take the id. **`changeChannel` is a write for what a fault means:** it needs no key, but a fault after the object committed (a guest deleted, a channel archived) is thrown across the binding, never answered `broken`, so the space says "outcome unknown" and never reports a committed change as not done. Nothing about a private channel reaches someone outside it: not its people, its guests' addresses, nor whether it exists.

**Two writes carry a compose key, in the contract's rule** (`asking.ts`, "WHEN A KEY IS USED UP"): `createChannel` (a second press must not make a second channel) and `inviteGuest` (a second press must not send a second email). A key whose first use did something is held a day and answers `Repeated` with the first answer; a refusal records nothing and releases it. The key's record and the thing it made are one step in the object (`compose_keys`, prefixes `channel:` and `invite:`, so a key cannot collide with a post's or a call's). The other changes need no key: repeating one does the same again or is refused (a name taken, a person not found).

**The invitation is the one email, and it is sent here.** Until now meet sent nothing for the space ("meet sends no email for a call"). `inviteGuest` sends the guest's invitation, the email `/start` sent, from the install's meet address (`meet@<your-domain>`), linking to meet's own `/w` (`APP_ORIGIN`), when a teammate presses Invite, once per key. The core decides it is allowed and counts it (`mail_log`); `SpaceMeet` sends it through the same `Mailer`; a failure to send leaves the guest in the channel and says so (`mailed: false`), as `/start` did. A repeat of the key sends nothing and reports what was recorded of the first (`spaceInviteSent`, best effort), reading **unknown as not sent**, so a person inviting again is a resend and never a silent loss. The contract's test pins that this is the only method named for an email.

**A channel nobody has written in has a row.** `conversations` says when each channel was made (`createdAt`, optional in the contract so an older meet still answers), so a channel made in the space is in the list and opens at once. A direct message nobody has written in still has none: starting the first message to someone is not channel admin, and is listed as a gap in the space's README.

**The guest's door does not move.** A guest still signs in at `<meet-host>/w` with an emailed code; the invitation links there. A guest never reaches the space: their address is not on the Teammate list, so every `SpaceMeet` method answers `not-admitted` whatever Cloudflare signed.

## What was not decided here

Changing a channel between open and private after it is made (`/start` could not either); a direct message with someone you have never messaged, editing and deleting a message, and a person's settings (the name, the nudge email, the privacy note): none of them was channel admin, and each is in the space's list of what `/start` still has.
