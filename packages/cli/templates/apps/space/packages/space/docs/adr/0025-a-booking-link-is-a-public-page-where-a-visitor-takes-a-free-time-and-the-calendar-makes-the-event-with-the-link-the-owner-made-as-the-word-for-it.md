# A booking link is a public page where a visitor takes a free time and the calendar makes the event, with the link the owner made as the word for it

Status: accepted. **Amends [0018](0018-guests-and-signers-use-three-open-paths-of-the-spaces-host-with-no-sign-in-and-a-policy-that-keeps-their-pages-to-those-paths.md)** (a fourth open path, `/book/`, beside the calls', the channel guests' and the signers'), **[0020](0020-the-calendar-is-the-seventh-tab-over-a-store-of-its-own-synced-from-google-by-push-and-it-reads-through-the-inboxs-one-grant.md)** on one sentence (there is no booking page: there is one now, and it is a third writer to Google), **[0023](0023-claude-reads-the-free-time-and-makes-moves-and-cancels-events-through-the-inbox-under-the-send-rule.md)** on one sentence (a booking also asks for the write-level token, for each write, and the invitations Claude makes stay under the send rule) and **[0024](0024-the-calendar-keeps-a-busy-block-on-the-owners-other-calendars-for-every-meeting-that-makes-them-busy-under-a-switch-the-owner-turns-on.md)** on one sentence (scheduling links are no longer outside what the calendar does). The company layer's [ADR 0002](../../../workspace/docs/adr/0002-one-service-token-comes-through-the-space-on-one-path-to-one-app.md) is not amended: Claude still has one path, to the inbox.

## Why

To meet with the owner, a person wrote, and the owner (or Claude) read the calendars, offered times, waited and sent an invitation: a round of mail in each direction for every meeting, paid by every kind of call the company has. The space already holds every connected account's events and already knows which make the owner busy, and it already holds a grant that was asked, once, for the permission to change events. What was missing was a page where an outsider can pick a free time, and a way for that pick to become an event without a person in between.

## What is decided

### 1. A booking link is the owner's, in Settings, Calendars

A link has a name the visitor reads (also the title of the event, with the visitor's name), a short address (the last part of its URL), a length, the Google account whose own calendar a booking is made on, a few lines of description, and its rules: the hours of each weekday on the company's clock (up to four spans a day), the least notice, how far ahead, the gap kept around other meetings and an optional most-per-day. A new one starts as Monday to Friday 9 to 5, four hours' notice, thirty days ahead, a quarter hour around other meetings, no daily limit, thirty minutes long (`BOOKING_DEFAULTS`). The owner makes, changes, turns off and deletes links and copies a link's address in a card of Settings, Calendars; there can be twenty. The card lists each link's upcoming and recent bookings (who, when, when it was booked, its state), which is the record of what links did. A link is a setting: none of the owner's four methods writes an event, and `CalendarForSpace` still has no method that does.

### 2. The page is on a fourth open path, `/book/`

`/book/<name>` is a link's page, `/book/manage/<secret>` a booking's own, `/book/api/...` the calendar's public API and `/book/_/...` the page's own files. The prefix is the fourth entry of the one list of open paths (`GUEST_PREFIXES`), so the edge, the Worker's router and the build all read it from there. It has the page policy of 0018 without change: every source a path of the host, no inline or evaluated script, no frames, forms, base or plugins; and, since the page opens no worker, plays no media and takes no WebAssembly, `worker-src` and `media-src` are `'none'` and the script source has no `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`. It connects to its own folder and `/book/api/` and nothing else of the host; an end-to-end test shows the page's own `fetch` of the team's API blocked. The Worker answers `/book/` only where the install has the `gmail` switch (the calendar comes with Google): without it every path under it is a 404. Reserved names (`api`, `_`, `manage`, `m`) cannot be a link's address, and the router refuses them as pages.

The edge: an install with Gmail has one more Access application, a bypass for everyone on `<host>/book/*` (`space-guest-booking`, named by the install file's `access.guests.booking`), made by the kit as the other three are. An install file with Gmail on and no name for it is refused at the build and the apply, as the others are.

### 3. What is free

A starting time is offered when it is inside the hours of its weekday (the whole meeting, to its end), no earlier than the least notice and no later than the window ahead, clear of every busy interval by the gap on both sides, and on a day the most-per-day has not filled. Times are every half hour (a quarter for a meeting shorter than half an hour). A busy interval is an event of any connected account's shown calendars that is not free time and not declined (the same busy rule as the free time Claude reads), from the calendar's own copy and merged as the agenda merges it, so the same meeting on two accounts is one and a "Busy" block (which is never read as an event) is never counted twice; an all-day event that makes the owner busy takes its day. The times the other bookings hold count too. Everything is worked out on the server from the link's rules on the company's clock: a time on a day the clocks change is a time the clock really shows (a time the clocks skip is not offered; one they repeat is offered once). The page draws the moments it is given on the visitor's own clock, in a zone the visitor can change, and never shows anything of an event.

A link whose account cannot be written to (its sign-in ended, no permission to change events, its main calendar hidden or not read yet, no writing client wired) is not open: the page says "cannot be booked right now" in words that tell a visitor nothing of why, and Settings says why, with the button that cures it.

### 4. Booking: the time is held, Google is asked, the event is made

The visitor gives a name, an email and an optional note. Before anything is asked of Google the calendar writes a row that holds the time, in the same turn of its object as the check that the time was free (no await between them), so two visitors cannot take one time. Then, for every live account, it asks Google's `freeBusy` (the reading token is enough) for the account's shown calendars for the time and the gap; a time Google says is taken lets the hold go and is refused. An account that cannot be asked (its sign-in ended, Google did not answer) is checked against the calendar's own copy, as old as its last read, and the booking says so (`checked: copy`, shown in Settings); the booking is still made. Then it makes the event on the link's account's main calendar with the visitor as the one guest, a Google Meet link, the booking's own address in the description (and NOT the visitor's note: the invitation goes in the owner's name to an address the visitor typed, so a note could carry a link or a claim into it; the owner reads the note in the booking's record in Settings), and `sendUpdates=all`, so Google sends the invitation. The write token is asked for each call and dropped, as the writer's rule is.

The event's id is **derived from the booking's own id** (as the blocks' are), so an insert whose outcome nobody knows is found again and never made twice: such a booking keeps its hold, says "pending" to the visitor (whose page asks again for a few minutes and changes when it is booked) and to the owner, and the read the booking owed finds the event by its id and books it. A booking is booked only once the calendar's copy holds its event, so a 409, or an answer that is not an event, leaves it held too. A hold is let go by a read, not by the clock: after an hour AND a successful read of the calendar since the insert that still shows no event; while reads fail the time stays held. A move whose answer was lost is settled the same way: once the copy shows the event at the time asked for, that becomes the booking's time and the move is counted; one it never shows is let go after the hour and a read. A 409 for the id is read as "this very booking, made before the answer was lost". A decision from Google about the insert (a 400, a missing permission) lets the time go and tells the visitor only that the link cannot be booked right now.

The event is written into the calendar's copy at once, so it shows in the Calendar tab and Today as the Google event it is and takes the time for every later visitor; and with the busy blocks on it gets its blocks on the other accounts like any meeting (they are made by the alarm that the booking owes, after the read).

### 5. Moving and cancelling: the secret is the credential

The booking's address carries a secret of 192 random bits, shown to the visitor once (on the page that says it is booked and in the invitation's description) and kept by the calendar **only as a hash**. It is the only thing that can move or cancel a booking from outside. The calendar's copy of the event's description leaves the secret out (`[hidden]`), so neither the store nor the owner's card holds it, wherever the description is read in. The page behind the address shows the booking and lets its visitor pick another free time (the booking's own time counts as free to itself and the gap is not kept against it; the event is patched with `sendUpdates=all`, and the new time is held first, as a booking's is) or cancel (the event is deleted, Google tells the guest); at most three moves. A booking that is still being confirmed cannot be moved or cancelled; a meeting that has been cannot. After a link is turned off a booking cannot be moved but can be cancelled; after it is deleted the same. The owner cancels or moves a booking as any event of theirs: an event the owner removed at Google is read as "removed" on the visitor's page and the owner's card, and one the owner moved is the time the booking has.

### 6. Limits keep a link from sending invitations in bulk

A booking sends an invitation, in the owner's name, to an address the visitor typed, which may be nobody's who asked for it. So: one address is invited at most three times in a day and holds at most five meetings that have not ended; one network address makes at most ten bookings in a day; one link takes at most thirty in a day (`BOOKING_LIMITS`; a cancelled booking still counts, since its invitation went out). An address is counted in its canonical form (lower case, no `+tag`, no dots for Gmail's own domains), the invitation still going to the address as typed, and a network address by its /64 when it is IPv6 (the shared `PUBLIC_LIMIT` binding is forms' as well and is not changed; the door keys it by the same network key). The network address is kept only as a hash keyed with a random value the store makes on first use. Every refusal for who the visitor is or how much they used the link (the owner's own address, an address, a network or a link over its day) is one sentence and one reason, given after the check that the time is one the link offers, so a stranger learns nothing of which it was nor of who has booked. The visitor's name and note are cut to 80 and 1000 characters, stripped of control characters, an em dash made a hyphen (the text goes out in the owner's name), and a name that is an address is refused; they are drawn as text everywhere. In front of the calendar the door refuses a write from another site (the check forms' public API makes), a flood (the per-caller limit forms' public API uses, `PUBLIC_LIMIT`, keyed by network address) and a booking with the hidden field filled in, the one a person never sees.

### 7. The owner is told

A booking is told to the owner's devices, and to no one else's, by the inbox's notifications ("Booked: Ada Quill, Fri, Nov 6, 10:00 AM PST" over the link's name; a tap opens the Calendar tab), asked in-process by the calendar's object by the method's name, as it asks who is connected: the calendar still does not import the inbox. A booking is made whether or not anyone could be told.

## The rule

This is the third write the space makes to Google that the owner did not ask for event by event, after the blocks' (0024) and, under the send rule, Claude's (0023): **a booking link the owner made and left on is the owner's word for exactly this**, one event of the link's length and name, on the link's account, with one guest who asked for it, inside the link's rules, and an invitation from Google in the owner's name, and for nothing else. It opens nothing wider: the face the visitor is handed (`CalendarForGuests`: `link`, `slots`, `book`, `booking`, `bookingSlots`, `move`, `cancel`) takes no person, has no method of the owner's and is handed to the booking door and to nothing else; the space's face is still settings only; Claude's invites, moves and cancellations stay under the send rule through the inbox's router; and `test/space-contract.test.ts` holds the three lists. A link turned off takes no new booking at once; bookings already made stay as ordinary events.

## Consequences

- **Anyone with a link sees when the owner is free in the next weeks.** Not what they are doing: the page is given moments and nothing else. That is what a booking link is.
- **Free time is read from the calendar's copy, which can be seconds to hours behind Google.** The check with Google at the moment of booking is what prevents a double booking, and where it cannot be made the copy stands in and the owner is told.
- **The page is on the team's origin** and kept to its own paths by the policy of 0018, which the tests hold for the new bundle too: nothing a visitor typed becomes markup (a test over the bundle's sources refuses the usual ways), and no cookie is sent with a call.
- **A restart of the Worker and one more open path at the sign-in wall** when an install takes the update. An install with Gmail needs `access.guests.booking` in its install file and an instance file for `space-guest-booking` before it applies.

## What is not here

Meetings with several of the owner's teammates (round robin, collective), paid bookings, questions beyond a name, an email and a note, reminders of the space's own, booking into the space's own Meet call (the event has a Google Meet link), non-Google calendars, a visitor's own calendar link or file, and recurring bookings.

## What only the hand test settles

That Google's `freeBusy` answers with the reading token for every shown calendar of an account and what it counts as busy (a declined event, an all-day event, the owner's own "Busy" blocks); that an insert carrying an id the calendar chose, a Meet request and `sendUpdates=all` makes the event with a Meet link and mails the guest, including one outside the owner's organisation; that the description's address reaches the guest as a link that works; that a patch of `start` and `end` alone sends the guest the new time, and a delete the cancellation; that Access serves the bypass on `/book/*` and not on any path of the team's; that the shared `PUBLIC_LIMIT` binding limits as expected; and that the owner's notification reaches a real device and its tap opens the Calendar tab. The fake Google answers as the documents say, written by the same person who wrote the code.
