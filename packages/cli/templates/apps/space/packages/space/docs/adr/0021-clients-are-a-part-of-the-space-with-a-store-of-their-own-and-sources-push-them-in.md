# Clients are a part of the space with a store of their own, and a source pushes its Clients in

Status: accepted. **Supersedes the inbox's ADR 0001 decision 3 ("Clients are read through the site, never copied") and the parts of its decision 14 that ask the site before a link**, once the inbox is switched to the part (section 5 below; the inbox's ADR carries the note). The code is `packages/clients`; its glossary is `packages/clients/CONTEXT.md`.

## What is decided

### 1. The space owns its Clients, in a part of its own

The space should own its data model. The list of Clients lives in a package beside the knowledge base and the calendar (`packages/clients`), a library with no Worker of its own, run by the space's Worker over a Durable Object class (`ClientsDO`, bound `CLIENTS_STORE`) declared as a NEW class under `exports` with nothing transferred, like `LiveDO` and `CalendarDO`. **It is not a copy of anything,** unlike the calendar's: a Client made in the space is only in this store, so the class, the Worker and the object's name never change, and it keeps Cloudflare's 30 days of history with the operator's bookmark, restart and counts (the owner's, by POST, as the inbox's and meet's are). The contract is `packages/workspace/src/clients.ts`; the faces are the space's (Settings, the owner only), the inbox's (read the list and one Client) and the source's (below). It tells the live object, under a new live part `clients`, after every stored change and no other time.

A Client is a number (a whole number from 1, the row's own id, never reused), a name, a legal name (none, or one; the limits are Forms' own, held once in `packages/workspace/src/clients.ts`), who made and changed it, when it was archived, and a revision that an edit names. **Nothing deletes a Client.** Archiving takes it out of the lists and pickers and leaves its people linked.

### 2. Why not the inbox's store, and why not Forms'

The same reasons the knowledge base is not a table in the inbox's store: restoring the owner's mail must never take the Clients back, and a mistake in the Clients' store must never touch mail or signatures. Forms' database stays Forms': its client rows keep their ids (sealed into every signing record), and each is a Client's signing settings (section 6).

### 3. Sources push; the space asks no one

A first design had the space pull a website's directory every five minutes over a `CLIENTS` service binding, with a Check now. **That was changed: the space has no dependency on a client source. No pull, no use of a `CLIENTS` binding by the Clients part, and no adapter for any site in the space. A site integrates into the space by pushing.** The reason: a space is installed by companies that have none of the services of whoever built it.

One paragraph on how that is kept honest. **A source is a sender behind one generic door, not an adapter in the space.** The part knows only `ClientsForSource` (`upsert(id, fields)`, `gone(id)`, `sync(clients)`, `list()`), reached through the Clients door (below) by whoever holds the door's token. What a source says of a Client is generic: its name for itself, its id for the Client, an address of the Client in its admin, the name and legal name it last said, a `preferredNumber` it asks the Client to keep, and an `extra` bag of text fields it names itself (for a company's website, say a subscription status and a sign-in address; kept, shown with the labels the declaration gives them, and never read by the part). The core Client has no Stripe, website-specific or sign-in field, nothing in `packages/clients/src` names a source's fields or reaches for its binding, and a test greps for the words. An install with no source declares no `clientsDoor` and has no pull, no binding and no errors: its Clients are made in the space. The only install-specific pieces are the declaration's `clientsDoor` entry, the Access application for its token (`space-clients-access.ts`) and the sender on the source's side, which is a separate change in the source's repo; all of them belong to the install. The `clientDirectory` switch (any install with a source of its own may turn it on) turns the door on: the declaration requires `clientsDoor` exactly where it is on. (Amended by workspace ADR 0004: the switch was once limited to one install.)

The rules the part applies to what a source sends:

| Field | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Name, legal name | The source fills them when it first brings a Client in, and keeps them current until the owner edits them in the space (a name follows the source's only while it still equals what the source last said). Once edited, the space's stand; the source's is kept and shown beside it. |
| Address in the source's admin, extra fields | The source, always. Replaced whole on every push, never edited in the space. |
| Archived | The space alone. |
| Who is linked | The space alone (the inbox's links). |

A Client the source says is **gone** is marked with the time and kept, never archived or deleted by the source; sending it again clears the mark. A Client **keeps the number its source asks for** when it is free, so every contact already linked to a Client of the source stays linked to it; a Client made in the space is numbered from `CLIENT_LOCAL_NUMBER_FROM` (same file) where the install has a source (so the source's numbers stay free), and a Client whose asked-for number is held takes the lowest free local number and keeps the source's own id beside it. A full send is all or nothing, and the same push twice changes nothing and writes nothing.

### 4. The Clients door

A third machine path on the space, shaped exactly like Claude's and the Telegram bridge's (the company layer's ADR 0002, amended): `clientsDoor` in the declaration (`path`, `serviceToken`, `source`), `Install.clientsDoor()` and `clientsRoute()`, an Access application of its own (`space-clients-access.ts` in the install's infra environment) admitting one service token, named for the source (for instance "northfield-site clients"), and no person, and the space answering the path before its person check (`packages/space/src/clients-door.ts`). Its own audience (`CF_ACCESS_AUD_CLIENTS`) means an assertion minted for either other door or the space is not one for this path, and the Worker also checks the assertion's token against the id it was deployed with (`CLIENTS_ACCESS_CLIENT_ID`). Unlike the other two it answers the request itself, over the Clients part, instead of forwarding it to the inbox. Its calls: `GET /api/clients`, `PUT /api/clients/{id}`, `POST /api/clients/{id}/gone`, `PUT /api/clients` (the full set). It admits the declared source's name and nothing else.

What it costs, stated: the space now lets in three machines. A compromised Clients token can write the Clients of the one declared source (names it has not been edited over, its own fields, gone marks, and new Clients with numbers it asks for); it cannot read mail, act as Claude, archive a Client, delete anything, change a name the owner edited, or reach any other part. When the space is down the sender's pushes wait, and its full send is the recovery.

### 5. The switch and the screens

**The inbox asks the part, and the space has no dependency on any site.** The inbox is handed the part's inbox face in-process (`packages/space/src/inbox-clients.ts`, over `storeFor`, a fresh stub per call), in place of the `CLIENTS` service binding, which is deleted from the space's wrangler file with the inbox's `readDirectory`, its parser of a site's answer and its dev stand-in. The space declares no service binding at all, and a test greps both `src/` trees for the old words (`packages/space/test/wrangler.test.ts`). A source's own directory entrypoint is left alone (it is in the source's repo). What the inbox reads of a Client is the part's generic fields: the admin address is the source's `url`, and ONE key of `extra`, the one the declaration names as `clientsDoor.suggestByAddressKey` (for a website, its sign-in address), is the address a contact is suggested the Client by (so a Client made in the space, or an install that declares no key, is suggested by domain only); no site's admin address is hard-coded. **Neutrality:** the space and the inbox know no other key of a source's `extra`. Settings draws the whole bag as label and value pairs, the label being the declaration's for the key (`clientsDoor.fields`: a label, a kind of `text` or `time`, and whether the list shows it too) or the key made readable, so a source's labels (say "Client Portal sign-in" and "Subscription") are in the declaration and nowhere in the code. The subscription was display only: nothing in mail (Bulk, placement, notifications) ever read it. A test greps the three `src` trees for the old names (`packages/space/test/wrangler.test.ts`). A link is checked against the part (it must answer, hold the Client, and not have it archived: 503 or 422 with a sentence) and never waits on a source. The inbox's store is not migrated: `contact.client_id` keeps its numbers, and since the part keeps the source's numbers each is the same Client.

**Settings › Clients is made over the part and the inbox together, in the Worker.** `packages/space/src/clients-settings.ts` answers one read for the list and one for a Client's page, each the part's Client (through its space face, the owner only) joined with the inbox's people under it (counts, the people linked, the people it only suggests), and four writes (New, Edit, Archive, Put back), each one call to the part, never retried. An edit names the revision the page was shown, and over a screen that is out of date it is refused with the Client as it is now and what was typed kept in the boxes. Whether a name still follows its source is the store's rule, computed in the Worker; the page says "the names follow the source" or, for a name the owner edited, what the source calls it. A Client the source no longer lists carries the date; archived Clients sit in a fold and are left out of the Inbox menu, the pickers (People's) and Suggestions, while their people stay linked. People's picker gains New Client (two writes, the Client then the link: if the second fails the Client exists and the sentence says the person was not linked), and "Linked to ..." and the person's Client pill open the Client's page. The screens read again on the live part `clients` and on mail.

The Client's page also has a Signing part (section 6).

### 6. Forms joins the one Clients

**A Forms client row is a Client's signing settings, and keeps its text id for good.** 14 of Forms' tables point at it, every signing event's hash includes it and every signing email loads the logo by it, so nothing re-keys. Forms' migration 0005 adds one nullable column, `client_number`, and a partial unique index on it (at most one row per Client): additive, no row rewritten, the older code ignores it. A row made before it has no number and is "not linked to a Client": it is listed apart in Forms' Clients section with **Link** (to a Client picked from those without signing settings; the row then takes the Client's names) and **Make a Client** (makes the Client from the row's names in Settings, then links), and until then it sends and signs exactly as it did. Nothing is matched by name.

**One name, one writer.** Forms keeps a COPY of the display name and legal name on the row, because signing emails, signers' pages and sealed certificates read them there (44 lines in 17 files). The Clients part is the only writer of that copy, through one seam (`FormsClientNames`, `packages/workspace/src/forms-for-space.ts`, implemented by `packages/forms/src/client-names.ts`, called in-process): an edit in Settings writes it in the same request as the edit (`src/clients-settings.ts`), and the five-minute tick of the Worker's scheduled handler puts right any copy that differs (`src/clients-forms.ts`), which is what catches a rename a source pushed in through the Clients door (that changes the Client alone). Forms' own routes take no name: create needs the Client's number and no name (the names come from the Client, read through the Clients part's inbox face, handed to Forms as `Env.CLIENTS_READ`), and an edit that names one is refused with the sentence that says where names are changed. Linking a row takes the Client's names in the same write, from the same read. Every write of the copy is audited in Forms' own record with the actor (`client-names`, or `link`).

**What Forms refuses that the Clients part does not.** A name a signer sees as the sender of mail may not hold control characters or `< > " \ , ; : @ ( )` (a rule older than the Clients part, so a name cannot pass as another sender). The Clients part only limits lengths (Forms' own, held once in `packages/workspace/src/clients.ts`), so a Client can be named "Smith, Jones & Co". For a Client Forms signs for, an edit to such a name is refused BEFORE the part is asked (Forms is asked `check`), with a sentence and nothing changed; Set up signing and Link refuse it the same way. A Client Forms does not sign for is edited freely. Setting up signing for a Client with no legal name asks for one and saves it on the Client first.

**The screens.** Forms keeps its Clients section, for what a signer sees: logo, colour, where replies go, copies, time zone, years kept; its client page shows the names read only with the way to change them in Settings. The Client's page in Settings gains its **Signing** part: whether Forms sends for it, with a link to its signing settings, or **Set up signing**. Forms' Clients section lists every active Client (the one list), with signing set up or Set up signing; **Add a client** goes to Settings' New Client and comes back to Set up signing for the Client it made, so Forms makes no second Client. **Send** offers the Clients with signing set up (and, until linked, the clients made before) and not an archived Client; an archived Client's documents already out go on (reminders, signing, the sweep's purge by its years kept).

**What an envelope prints.** The copy of the names changes when a Client is renamed, but a document already sent must not. Forms' migration 0006 adds two nullable columns to `envelopes`, the names the envelope was SENT under, written by send in the same batch and by nothing else, and in no event, so no hash moves. The consent a signer is shown, its terms PDF and the certificate of completion (on the web and in the sealed PDF) print those. An envelope sent before 0006 has none, prints the row as it is now, and its certificate's consent note says the business was renamed since a signer agreed (none is live).

**What the page says when the copy is not the Client's.** A source can push a name Forms refuses (control characters, `< > " \ , ; : @ ( )`) or drop a legal name, and the tick can put neither right. The Client's Signing section says what Forms signs under, why it differs and what to do (`CopyDifference`, `src/clients-forms.ts`). Forms now measures a name's length as the Clients part and the column do (characters, not UTF-16 units). Clearing the legal name of a Client Forms signs for is refused in Settings with a sentence, because a signed document names it and Forms would go on signing under the old one.

**Who reads the Clients list.** The owner only, as Settings does. The Forms routes that read it (Clients without signing settings, Set up signing, Link) refuse any other operator with a 403, and `client_archived` on a signing-settings row is null for one. Send offers only the rows it knows are not archived, so an operator who is not the owner cannot send for a linked Client. The install has one operator, the owner, so nothing is lost today; a second operator who sends needs Forms to keep its own archived mark first.

**Deploy and undo.** `bunx @zabaca/zbc apply <env> space` applies Forms' migrations 0005 and 0006 (this instance migrates Forms' database, before the deploy; both are additive) and deploys the Worker; nothing else is applied. A bookmark of Forms' database (`bunx wrangler d1 time-travel info <forms database>`) is noted before. Undoing it is redeploying the build before: the columns stay and the older code ignores them; a row linked since keeps its number, and its copy of the names stays as it was last written. Nothing is lost.

## What it does not change

The inbox's store, Claude's script, the Clients door and the way a source pushes. A signed record, its chain and the logos in emails already sent are exactly as they were.

## Consequences

- **A deploy of the space that adds the class restarts the owner's mail,** as every deploy of the space does, so each waits for the owner's word. Undoing the store's first stages is redeploying the build before them; the store stays, unread and harmless.
- **The Access application mints a service token on its first apply.** That apply is done alone, first, with the secret captured into the sender's secrets store by the script that applies it (the Clients package README's "The first apply" has the steps); applying `space` first would mint it where nobody is watching for the secret.
- **A source's data reaches the space only if the source sends it.** Until its sender is built and run, the store is empty. The sender's job is one full send (which keeps the source's numbers) and then a push on every create or change.
