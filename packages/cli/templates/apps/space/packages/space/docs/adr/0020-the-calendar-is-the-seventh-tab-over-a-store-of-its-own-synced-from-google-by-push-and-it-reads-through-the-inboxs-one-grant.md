# The calendar is the seventh tab, over a store of its own synced from Google by push, and it reads through the inbox's one grant

Status: accepted. **Supersedes [0014](0014-conversations-is-split-back-into-inbox-and-meet-and-the-old-addresses-redirect.md) and [0008](0008-knowledge-is-the-fifth-tab-and-the-only-place-knowledge-changes.md) on the number of tabs**: the space has seven, and `TABS` in the company layer (`packages/workspace/src/install.ts`) is the one list. The calendar's code is `packages/calendar`; its glossary is `packages/calendar/CONTEXT.md`; the steps an apply cannot do are `packages/calendar/docs/runbooks/calendar-google-setup.md`.

## What is decided

### 1. Where it sits: a seventh, top-level tab, Calendar, after Meet

Calendar is a **top-level tab**, after Meet, at `/calendar`, in every install that has a Google account, as Knowledge was added. (Amended by workspace ADR 0004: the tabs are the install's, so Calendar is drawn only where the `gmail` switch is on; it was "in every install".) An earlier idea, "a sub-nav item inside Meet, beside Calls", is superseded.

- A tab is down the side on a computer and a strip across the top on a phone. Seven tabs and the gear no longer fit a 390 pixel strip at the type the six had: the gear (an icon alone) is 42 pixels instead of 52 (`settings.css`), the label type is 9.5 pixels and a little tighter under 440 pixels (`style.css`), and the longest label, Knowledge, is read whole in each tab's 49 pixels. `e2e/calendar.test.ts` measures the text against its tab so a label cut to an ellipsis fails the check.
- An event's card is an address (`/calendar/event/<key>`): the back button closes it, and on a phone the card takes the screen. Today's rows open it.
- Settings has a new section, **Calendars** (`/settings/calendars`), beside Mailboxes.

### 2. A part of the space's Worker over a store of its own, declared as a new class

The calendar is the knowledge app's shape: a package that is a library (no Worker of its own), a Durable Object class the space declares under `exports` as a NEW class (no `state`, nothing transferred, as `LiveDO` was in [0019](0019-one-live-object-per-company-holds-every-open-page-and-every-store-tells-it-after-a-change-is-stored.md)), a contract in the company layer (`packages/workspace/src/calendar.ts`, `CalendarForSpace`, five methods, each taking the person the one sign-in check named and admitting the owner alone), and a part in `src/parts.ts`. **Nothing in the store is the only copy of anything**: it is a copy of Google's calendars, so a lost or replaced store costs a full read of each calendar and nothing else, which is why it has no bookmark, restore or move steps as the stores of mail and chat do.

Claude has no door to it: the company layer's ADR 0002 gives Claude one path, to the inbox. Reading the calendar from a session is a later change.

### 3. The grant is the inbox's, and where the calendar gets its access tokens

The calendar re-uses the install's Google integration and adds to it rather than making a new one. There is no new Google project and no new OAuth client; the install's existing client gets the calendar permission. One grant per account now serves mail and calendar.

**The choice recorded here: the calendar gets its tokens from the inbox's part, not from the shared grant held in two places.** The refresh token lives in the inbox's store, in the `mailbox` row, as it always did, and nowhere else. The calendar asks the inbox's store, in-process (the object found by the name `inbox`, in the same Worker), two things and nothing else:

- `calendarGrants()`: who is connected, with the label mail calls the account, and four flags (`connected`, `lapsed`, `calendar`: the grant includes the reading permission, and `events`: it also includes the permission to create and change events, added with the permission, section 4). No token.
- `calendarAccess(address, fresh?)`: **an access token limited to the calendar permission**. The inbox uses the refresh token with Google's `scope` parameter set to `calendar.readonly` alone (the refresh grant takes a subset of what was granted), so the token the calendar holds, in memory until a minute before it ends, can read calendars and cannot read or change mail even though the grant behind it can. The answer is the token and when it ends, or why not (`disconnected`, `lapsed`, `no-calendar`, `no-events`, `failed`) in the inbox's own words. A third argument, `level` (`read`, the default and the only one the calendar's sync passes, or `events`), is the seam for booking: `events` asks Google for `calendar.events` alone and is refused (`no-events`) for a grant that lacks it. Nothing asks for `events` yet; anything but the word `events` is `read`. A refused grant is marked lapsed in the inbox's store at once, so mail and calendar say the same sentence about it.

Why this and not the alternatives:

- *Copy the refresh token into the calendar's store*: two stores holding the one secret that reaches mail, and a Disconnect in the inbox that the calendar would not hear of. Rejected: the secret has one home.
- *Hand the calendar the refresh token at call time*: the calendar's code would hold a token that can send mail. Rejected: the calendar needs less than the grant, so it is handed less.
- *Ask the inbox for a full-scope access token*: simpler, and the token could change mail. Rejected for the same reason; the narrowed token costs one parameter.

What changed in the inbox, stated plainly: **its store's schema did not change and there is no migration** (the `mailbox` row already kept the granted scopes, `scopes`, and the lapse stamp, `grant_lapsed_at`). Code changed: the consent request grew a second form for the calendar (`calendar.readonly` and `calendar.events`, `include_granted_scopes=true`) while the mailboxes' stays mail's three (section 4); `GoogleAuth` gained `accessToken(refreshToken, scope, fresh?)`; `connectMailbox` took an optional purpose; the Mailroom gained the two methods above (on `MAIL_METHODS`, and in `UNFOLLOWED_CALLS`, since no mail screen draws them). Their tests are `packages/inbox/test/calendar-grants.test.ts` and `gmail-client.test.ts`.

### 4. Mail is never held behind a calendar consent: two sign-ins, the mailboxes' for mail alone and Settings, Calendars' own for the calendar, incrementally

The first build put the calendar permission in the one consent request every Connect used. The review found the flaw: a Workspace admin who restricts the calendar scope would then block the **weekly** mail reconnect of every account outside the app's own organisation, which an app in testing mode forces. So there are two requests, and **no request that asks for mail ever names a calendar scope**:

- **The mailboxes' Connect (and Connect again)** asks for `gmail.modify`, `openid` and `email`, exactly as before the calendar existed (`OAUTH_SCOPES`). It sets `include_granted_scopes=true`, so a grant that already carries the calendar permission keeps it across a mail reconnect; the request itself never names it. **To verify in the hand test:** that a mail reconnect of an account with a calendar grant keeps the calendar permission, and that it still works when the organisation restricts the calendar scope. If it does not, the one line to drop is that parameter on the mail request (the calendar would then be added again after each weekly lapse).
- **Settings, Calendars' Connect calendar** (`/api/inbox/gmail/start?for=calendar`, `GMAIL_FOR_PARAM`) asks for `calendar.readonly` and `calendar.events` **and nothing else** (`CALENDAR_OAUTH_SCOPES`; not the full `calendar` scope), with `include_granted_scopes=true` and the account hinted. Google adds what the account already granted, so the grant that comes back carries mail's permissions too. A second cookie (`space_gmail_for`) tells the return it was a calendar sign-in, so it lands on Settings, Calendars. **Its value is the check of the sign-in it belongs to (`<check>.calendar`)**, so it can only ever speak for that one sign-in: a calendar sign-in left unfinished (Google's "access blocked" page, a closed tab) leaves a cookie that lives ten minutes, and a mailboxes' Connect started in that time has another check, which the stale cookie does not match, so it is handled as the mail sign-in it is (tested in `gmail-connect.test.ts`). The mailboxes' start also clears any such cookie, and a return spends it whether or not it was used; with no matching cookie a return is a mailboxes' one.
- **What the inbox does with a calendar sign-in's grant** (`Mailroom.connectMailbox(..., 'calendar')`): it must carry **both** permissions: mail's and the calendar's reading one. If it comes back without mail's, or without the reading one (unticked), **nothing is kept and nothing is revoked at Google**: revoking the new token ends the account's whole grant at Google, mail's included, and the grant held is still good. The page says what happened in the inbox's words and nothing changed. (The mailboxes' Connect is as it was: a grant without `gmail.modify` is refused and revoked.)
- **The permission to change events: asked with the reading one, once per account, so booking (a later change) needs no second consent.** Connect calendar asks for `calendar.events` beside `calendar.readonly`; the mailboxes' Connect still never names a calendar scope. **"Calendar connected" still means the reading permission**: a grant that has it and lacks `calendar.events` (the person unticked it, or the account was connected before this) is kept, not refused, and is live for reading. Whether `events` was granted is recorded: the inbox's grant list says it (`events`), and the calendar keeps it on the account (`account.events`, migration 1 of its store, which is the only schema change; the inbox's did not change) as the inbox last said, for booking to check. Settings, Calendars shows a small hint on such an account ("Connected for reading. Editing events needs Connect calendar again") with the same link as Connect calendar. **The token the sync is handed stays `calendar.readonly`**: reads never get write power, and the write-level token is the `level` seam above, used by nothing yet. A grant with `calendar.events` but not the reading permission is not a connected calendar.
- **A sign-in without the calendar permission is not refused**: it connects for mail, mail works as ever, and the calendar says "needs the calendar permission" for that account, with Connect calendar beside it. This is true of an account connected before the calendar was added and of one whose Google consent had the calendar box unticked.

The tests: `packages/inbox/test/calendar-grants.test.ts` ("the mailboxes' consent request never names a calendar scope", "the calendar's own Connect ... keeps mail's grant", the refusals that revoke nothing) and `gmail-client.test.ts`; the space's `test/gmail-connect.test.ts` (the second cookie and where each return lands) and `test/calendar-dom.test.tsx` (the buttons).

**Consequences:** each connected account gets the calendar by pressing **Connect calendar** in Settings, Calendars once (not by reconnecting in Mailboxes). While the Google project stays in testing, accounts outside the project's own organisation lapse every 7 days, for mail and calendar together, and a lapse stops the booking page when one exists. After a lapse, **Connect again** on the calendar's card is the mailboxes' own sign-in; if the calendar permission does not come back with it, Connect calendar adds it. There is **no Disconnect in Settings, Calendars**: one grant serves both, so removing an account is Mailboxes' Disconnect, which takes mail and calendar away together, and the page says so.

### 5. The sync: Google's data comes into the store and stays current by push; nothing polls and nothing reads through

The calendar should not poll all the time, as the inbox does not: it has its own persistence layer that Google's data is integrated into. For each shown calendar of each live account:

1. A first **full read** of a window (three months back, half a year ahead; section 8 widened the back from a month; Google expands repeating events, `singleEvents=true`, so the store does no repeat arithmetic), applied in one transaction with Google's `nextSyncToken` and the window.
2. **Incremental reads** from that token, which return what changed since, cancelled events included.
3. Woken by **Google's push notifications**: a watch channel per shown calendar's events and one per account's calendar list, to an https address on the space's Worker (section 6). A notification carries no body; it only says changes are owed, and the change is read with the token.
4. **Channels are replaced before they end**: Google does not renew a channel, so one with a day left is replaced (the new one made first, then the old one stopped).
5. A **slow safety resync**: every shown calendar is also read by its token every 6 hours, because Google's own words are that notifications are not completely reliable. A read that finds nothing is one cheap call.
6. A **410** (Google no longer honours the token) forgets the token and reads in full again. A full read also happens every 7 days, because the window rolls and a token keeps the window it was made with (an assumption, below).
7. A read that fails leaves the old picture and the old token (events and token change in one transaction), says what failed on the calendar, and is retried after a backoff (30 seconds, doubling to an hour). A grant Google ended is **not** retried: the account is marked lapsed with its one sentence, its last events stay drawn with when they were read, and nothing is asked of Google for it until the inbox says it is live again.

The tab, Today and Settings read the store and never Google. The store tells the space's live object after a change is stored (`LIVE_PARTS` gained `calendar`), so open pages read again; the ten-minute net of [0019](0019-one-live-object-per-company-holds-every-open-page-and-every-store-tells-it-after-a-change-is-stored.md) covers a lost message. The object's alarm does the work; it is asked for at the soonest thing owed (a notification, a retry, a safety resync, a channel to renew, the hourly look at who is connected) and while nothing is owed nothing is asked of Google (`test/sync.test.ts`, "no polling").

### 6. Google's push: what the documents say and where the notifications go

This was settled first. Sources are Google's own pages, as read when this was decided:

- *Push notifications*, https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/push: the receiving address "must use HTTPS" with a valid certificate (not self-signed, untrusted, revoked or for another host name); **no domain registration or verification is mentioned**. Notifications "do not include a message body"; the channel, its secret and the resource are in `X-Goog-Channel-ID`, `X-Goog-Channel-Token`, `X-Goog-Resource-ID` and `X-Goog-Resource-State` (`sync` when the channel is made, `exists` on a change). **There is no automatic renewal** ("you must replace it ... by calling the watch method"; use a new unique id, expect an overlap). A channel is stopped with `POST /calendar/v3/channels/stop` (id and resource id). The channel id is at most 64 characters and the secret at most 256. "Notifications are not 100% reliable"; "a small percentage of messages" are dropped.
- *Events: watch* and *CalendarList: watch*, https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events/watch and `.../calendarList/watch`: `params.ttl` is the channel's life in seconds and **the default is 604800 (7 days)**; the answer has `resourceId` and `expiration` (ms). `calendar.readonly` is among the scopes accepted by both. The maximum life is **not stated** on these pages; the calendar asks for 7 days and keeps the `expiration` Google answers with, so a shorter grant only makes it renew sooner.
- *Domain verification*, https://support.google.com/googleapi/answer/7072069: "Domain verification in the API Console is **no longer required** to make push notifications work with your domains." Older third-party notes and old forum threads (the error `push.webhookUrlUnauthorized`) still ask for it; the hand test is where a refusal of that kind would show.
- *Synchronize resources efficiently*, https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/sync, and *Events: list* and *CalendarList: list*: `nextSyncToken` only on the last page; "a 410 ... should trigger a full wipe of the client's store and a new full sync"; a sync token cannot be used with `timeMin`, `timeMax`, `orderBy`, `q`, `updatedMin` and some others, and `showDeleted` cannot be false with it; deleted entries always come in an incremental read.

**Where the notifications go: the push host's second path.** `https://<push host>/calendar/push`, a POST. The host is the inbox's machine address (answered by the space's Worker by hostname, no Access application covers it, so Google can reach it). It answered ONE path (Gmail's push) and now answers two; every other path and method is a 404 as before. The calendar's path is accepted only for a channel the store made, with the secret the store chose and the resource Google named, compared in constant time; anything else is a 404 with the same body as any other path, so nothing is learned about which channels exist. The channel's secret is a random 48 characters made for each channel and is the whole defence (Google does not sign a notification), which is enough because a notification only owes one read of Google with the store's own token. The address is a var of the space's wrangler file, `CALENDAR_PUSH_URL`; unset or not https, no channel is made and the safety resync alone keeps the calendar current. A new host was not made: Google needs only https with a valid certificate, and the existing host already has both and no Access in front of it, so a second host would add a DNS record and a route for nothing.

### 7. What the page shows

- **The agenda**: today first, then each day with something on it, every shown calendar of every account merged by time, each in its account's colour (six slots, kept for good, none the warning amber), the meetings booked in Meet among them (they are on no Google calendar), an unanswered invitation marked "Not answered", a declined one dimmed and struck, the same meeting in two of the owner's calendars once, naming both accounts. All times are on the company's clock; an all-day event has days, Google's, and never moves with the reader's zone.
- **The card**: the title, the time with the zone named, the place, the video call link, who is invited and how each answered, the organiser, which account it is on, the notes and a link to the event in Google Calendar.
- **Today**: Later today and Tomorrow list Google's events with Meet's meetings, in time order, all-day first, each opening its card; a declined invitation is not on the day; a calendar that did not answer is one line, not a missing section.
- **Settings, Calendars**: one card per account with its calendars, a switch for whether each is shown (owned and primary calendars are shown at first; holidays, birthdays and other people's are hidden), when it was read, and one amber sentence for an account that is lapsed or that needs the calendar permission, with Connect again (for a lapse: the mailboxes' own sign-in) or Connect calendar (for the missing permission: section 4), each hinting that account. The menu's foot gains a line only for an account that needs the calendar permission (a lapse is already said by its Gmail line: one grant, one sentence).
- **Event text is untrusted** (a title, a place, a guest's name and the notes are whatever the organiser typed, and an invitation can come from a stranger): it is cut to a length, stripped of control characters, and the notes' HTML is flattened to text on the way in; the page draws every word as text and no markup is ever made of it, and the only addresses made links are https ones the calendar vouched for (`packages/calendar/src/event-parse.ts`; `test/calendar-dom.test.tsx` renders markup as a title, a place, a guest's name and notes and checks nothing is made and nothing runs).
- **A refresh token is never logged, never in an error and never sent to the page.** The calendar never holds one; the access token it holds is in memory only and is in no answer, no log and no error (`packages/calendar/test/read.test.ts`, "what never reaches a screen").

### 8. The tab has three views, Agenda, Week and Month, and the address holds which one and which day

A day and a week grid were first asked for; the set chosen is Google's own, so the tab has **Agenda** (the list above, called Schedule in Google), **Week** and **Month**. A single-day grid is not built: on a phone the Week is three days, which is what a day view would have been there.

- **The address is the state**: `/calendar?view=week&date=YYYY-MM-DD`. No query is the agenda from today; no `date` is today, so "this week" is still this week tomorrow; a view or day that cannot be read is the default. An event's card is `/calendar/event/<key>` with the same query, so the card closes back to the same view and day, and the back button steps through what was done. Previous, Next, Today and the choice of view are buttons that change the address; the title names the range (`March 2030`, `Mar 3 to 9, 2030`).
- **Weeks begin on Sunday** (Google's default for a US company), on the company's clock throughout (the install's time zone, as the agenda and the card show it). A week that holds the clocks changing is read as the 7 days and an hour it is.
- **Week**: a column a day, an all-day row on top (it grows with the busiest day, nothing is hidden behind a count), an hour grid with overlapping events side by side (each shares the width of its group), a line for now that moves each minute, today lit. An evening that runs past midnight is drawn in both days, cut at the day's edge. A booked Meet meeting is drawn grey and 30 minutes long (the agenda answer carries a start and no end) and is not a link; its Join is in the agenda. On a phone: three days from the chosen one.
- **Month**: whole weeks (four to six rows). On a computer each day lists its events as chips coloured by account, **two and "+N more" past three**; a day's number, its "+N more" and the empty part of its cell open **that day's week** (a time grid is the closest thing to a day that exists). On a phone each day is its number and up to three dots (and "+N"), a tap **chooses** the day and its things are listed under the grid with the agenda's own rows; the chosen day is the address's `date`.
- **One read per view**: each view reads the agenda for exactly the days it draws (the week's 7, the month's 28 to 42, the phone's 3), and never more than the 62 days the agenda read already allowed. Moving to a span not yet read draws the grid empty and dimmed rather than the old span's events under the new title.
- **The window the store keeps is now three months back and half a year ahead** (`CALENDAR_BACK_MS` 92 days, was 30; `CALENDAR_AHEAD_MS` 180 as before), so paging a month back and forward has data: a month grid opened on last month and the one before is whole, and the one before that is partly there. The agenda API reads any span of up to 62 days and answers what the store holds for it (nothing outside the window, which the page says: "The calendar keeps events from about 3 months back to 6 months ahead, so part of this span is not here."). **A calendar already synced under the old window is read in full again at its next read** (`needsFull`: its window began more than the week a rolling window lags later than the new one reaches), so the longer past arrives within the safety resync's 6 hours and not within a week; a store that was made new reads three months from the start.
- Colour, dimming (declined struck, tentative italic, a lapsed account's last picture faded), the legend, the amber lines and the card are the agenda's, drawn from the same classes and the same colour slots; the switcher is the space's `chip` group and the arrows its `btn`.

## What is not here

No writing to Google (no event is made, moved or answered; the grant may carry the permission to change events, but nothing asks Google for a token that has it and no method of the contract could use one), no booking page, no moving or cancelling, no notifications or reminders, no door for Claude. (Section 8 added the week and month grids.) Those are later changes.

## What only the hand test settles

Each is an assumption the fake Google cannot prove (`packages/calendar/docs/runbooks/calendar-google-setup.md` lists the checks): that Google accepts a push to `<push host>/calendar/push` with no domain registered; the `expiration` Google actually grants a channel; that a refresh with `scope=calendar.readonly` returns a token whose scope is the calendar's alone (Google's reference says the scope must be a subset of the original grant but does not say the token is narrowed; the runbook reads the token's scope from Google); that a sync token made by a read with a window keeps the window (Google's pages say the restrictions apply to the first read and the token carries on; the weekly full read bounds the cost if not); that the admins of any other organisation whose accounts are connected allow the calendar permission on the app they already allowed for mail; that the project in testing lapses the calendar grant after 7 days exactly as mail's does; and that each account's next 7 days match the same account's count in Google Calendar.
