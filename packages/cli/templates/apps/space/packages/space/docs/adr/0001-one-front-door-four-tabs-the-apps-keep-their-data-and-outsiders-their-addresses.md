This record is the front door and the tabs. The merge rule below is recorded here because it is why the front door is a package of its own.

## The front door

A small Worker owns the space's host (`space.<your-domain>`) as one Custom Domain, never a route pattern. One Access application covers the whole host, with no bypass path, so nothing on it is public. (It is an instance of its own, `space-access`, that the Worker's instance imports, and not an `access` block inside the Worker's instance, because the inbox, meet and forms must read the same audience to check the assertion the space forwards: `packages/workspace/docs/adr/0001`.) The Worker checks the company layer's sign-in itself on every request, pages and files included, and reads the header only: a cookie-only request once got past the Access edge. It stores nothing. The inbox's mail, meet's direct messages, channels and calls, and forms' records and files stay in the stores they are in, and each keeps its own sending and its own sign-in rules. The front door draws Today, Conversations and People itself from what those apps answer inside the install's account; the Forms tab is forms' own screens, which the front door hands requests to with the sign-in passed along for forms to check again.

A sign-in lasts a month in the space (730h), and Forms asks again once a day: it has its own Access application on a path here ([ADR 0002](0002-forms-is-a-frame-on-its-own-admin-with-a-day-long-sign-in-of-its-own.md)). The alternative of one app underneath was not taken: the mail and the chat stay in two stores in either design, so the list is two reads either way, and a bad deploy of the inbox or meet takes down only its part here. The front door is a single point, though: if it fails, the whole space is down while the old addresses still reach each app.

## The four tabs

Today, Conversations, Forms and People, in that order, the same four in every install, down the side on a computer and as a strip across the top on a phone with the icon above the label. The space opens on Today. Each item has one home: an email, a direct message or a channel message and a huddle or call live in Conversations; a document in Forms; a person's profile in People. Today holds nothing to act on, only pointers. The tabs' names, order and paths are the company layer's (`TABS` in `packages/workspace`).

## The merge rule for Conversations

The Conversations list is the front door's merge of what the inbox and meet answer: one row per person (joined by lowercase email address across the inbox's contacts, the team and meet's people) and one per channel. Each row's time is its newest item from any source; unread is the sum of the inbox's and meet's unread for that person; how it came is the newest item's kind. Newsletters and other Bulk mail, and closed conversations, stay out of the main list and sit behind filters. If one app does not answer within a few seconds, the list shows the other's rows and says what could not be reached.

A person's conversation merges their emails (labelled by subject, and "with" the others on a shared thread) and their direct messages by time, with huddle, call and document cards in place. The reply goes by the inbox for an outside person (email, in the thread they choose) and by meet for a teammate or a channel guest (a direct message); a channel's conversation is meet's channel. Opening it marks read in both apps.

## The outsiders' addresses

Call guests (including those sent a call link by email) and signers land on the meet and forms hosts (`meet.<your-domain>`, `forms.<your-domain>`), and the space has no public door. Call links (`meet.<your-domain>/r/...`) and channel guests' sign-in (`meet.<your-domain>/w`) stay on meet; signing links (`forms.<your-domain>/s/...`) stay on forms. Machines keep theirs too: Claude's script and Gmail's push on the inbox's addresses.

The declaration keeps `apps` (each app's host, door and sign-in length) for these addresses and for the Access applications they still need. **Deferred:** the declaration could also name "the old addresses kept as aliases" that send people to the space. That is not built. The apps' team-facing addresses redirect to the space only once the space covers what each does, and the redirects arrive then: the forms admin first, then the inbox's address and meet's start page. Until then each keeps working as before, and the declaration says nothing more about them than it did.
