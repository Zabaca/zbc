# A shared business mailbox through Cloudflare Email: add it, apply it, check it

How an install adds a business mailbox that Cloudflare Email Routing feeds and Cloudflare's sending answers from, how to back up and restore the store those mailboxes live in, and what to check live. It is the procedure for one domain, `<your-domain>`, with an example address `support@<your-domain>`; the invented install's own domain is `northfield.example`.

The decisions behind it are the inbox's ADR 0001: decision 17 (the Cloudflare mailbox kind and its entrance), 18 (sending as the mailbox's address), 19 and 21 (the assistant and the draft chat), 20 (the restore), 22 (the spam check), 24 (a second mailbox and its own questions) and 25 (files and bounces, `inbox-attachments-and-bounces.md`). Who may see a mailbox is `inbox-mailbox-people.md`. This runbook is what to do and what to look at, not why.

## What it builds

- **A mailbox of its own kind in the inbox's store.** Mail to any address on the domain is accepted by the Worker's email entrance, kept first as the exact message in the raw-mail bucket, then filed under the mailbox, marked with the mailbox's short name, threaded and deduplicated. A redelivery of the same Message-ID stores nothing new.
- **Replies from the mailbox's address.** A reply goes out as `<Name> <support@<your-domain>>` through the Worker's `EMAIL` binding (`send_email`), plain text, with `In-Reply-To` and `References`, to whoever wrote. It is recorded as sent only once Cloudflare accepts it; a refusal puts the draft back exactly as it was; an outcome Cloudflare never reported leaves it `sending` for the owner to settle. The exact request is kept in the bucket under `out/<message id>`. Claude's draft and recorded-instruction send go through the same route and the same switch as for Gmail.
- **Optionally** an assistant persona (the draft chat) and spam questions (the spam check). A mailbox with no persona gets no assistant; one with no spam questions is never put to the spam model and its switch refuses to turn on.
- **It does not** move any other mail, change the site's published address, or touch Gmail accounts. A conversation's people and who may see the mailbox are the mailbox's people (`inbox-mailbox-people.md`).

## The pieces

1. **The declaration** (the install's `workspace.json`) lists the mailbox under `cloudflareMailboxes`: its address, its name, its short name, and whether the spam check begins on (`spamCheck`). The install kit writes the Worker's `INBOX_MAILBOXES` var and the `send_email` binding's `allowed_sender_addresses` from it, so the two cannot disagree. Every mailbox belongs to the business of its own name.
2. **The content file** beside the declaration (`workspace-content.json`) holds the mailbox's `personas` entry and its `spamQuestions` entry (the company layer's ADR 0006). The build checks it against the declaration and fails for a persona for an address no business has, questions for an address that is not a declared Cloudflare mailbox, a question kind the spam model's reason lines have no words for, or `spamCheck: true` with no questions.
3. **The raw-mail bucket** (the install file's bucket instance) and **the delivery-report queue** (`inbox-attachments-and-bounces.md`). The Worker's `RAW` binding names the bucket and the install file names it once.
4. **The zone's email instance**, an instance of the cloudflare email module in the install's repo (for example `support-email`), for the domain: `enableSending: true`, `enableRouting: true` and a catch-all whose target is the space's Worker (`catchAll: { action: 'worker', workerName: '<space worker name>' }`). It writes no literal address rules and no forward destinations, so no verification email is sent and nothing needs a click.

## Apply, in this order

1. **Apply the space** (`bunx @zabaca/zbc apply <env> space`, the named instance, never a no-argument apply). The Worker now declares the mailbox, so its entrance accepts mail for the domain and `EMAIL` lists the address as a sender. `wrangler deploy --dry-run` prints `env.EMAIL (unrestricted - senders: <the declared addresses>)`. No migration, no new binding, secret or var: the mailbox's row in the store is made by the first message that arrives for it.
2. **Apply the zone's email instance** (`bunx @zabaca/zbc apply <env> support-email`). It onboards sending for the domain, enables routing (which writes the MX records and the apex SPF and locks them) and points the catch-all at the Worker.

**Order matters.** Applying the zone first routes mail to a Worker that does not know the domain, and the entrance refuses it. If the domain has no MX yet, mail to it bounces regardless; the order only decides whether the first mail sent is the one that works.

**If another repo owns the zone's Worker or site records,** the email instance writes only email records: it never writes A, AAAA or CNAME records, so nothing there conflicts. If that repo's deploy applies every instance (`zbc apply <env>` with no name), merging it before the space is applied sends mail to a Worker that refuses it; merge and deploy it after.

**Expected records** after both applies: `MX` on the apex and on `cf-bounce` (route1, route2 and route3 `.mx.cloudflare.net`), the apex `TXT v=spf1 include:_spf.mx.cloudflare.net ~all`, the same SPF on `cf-bounce`, DKIM `TXT` at `cf2024-1._domainkey` and `cf-bounce._domainkey`, and `_dmarc`. The first apply's output is the real list.

## What goes wrong at the apply

- **The deploy token's scopes.** The apply needs Zone: Email Routing Rules Edit, Zone Settings Edit, DNS Edit and Account: Email Sending Edit, Email Routing Addresses Edit. The module's own error names the missing ones. A token limited to named zones needs the new zone added; that is a dashboard step on the account.
- **Two SPF records at the apex break SPF for the whole domain.** Sending and routing each want an SPF. The expected result is exactly one `v=spf1` at the apex (routing's) and one on `cf-bounce` (sending's). If the apex ends with two, merge them into one line before anything else.
- **A line `DNS record(s) not yet verified` is normal for a few minutes.** Apply the instance again (it is idempotent) until it is gone. A line about a forward destination means the instance was changed from the shape above.
- **Not every module capability exists.** The cloudflare module's `r2Bindings` rewrites `bucket_name` by deploying a generated copy of the Worker's config with `--config`, which skips the build tool's redirect to the built Worker; so the `RAW` binding is declared where the kit writes the Worker's settings and the bucket instance is only an import for ordering (the bucket exists before the Worker deploys). The r2 module cannot set a bucket lock or a lifecycle rule, so check by hand that the bucket has no lifecycle rule that deletes raw mail (nothing in the Worker deletes from it; it only calls `head`, `get` and `put`).

## Undoing it

- Redeploy the previous space (the mailbox row, if made, is inert). Setting the mailbox out of the declaration and applying again takes it out of the entrance and the sender list; its stored conversations stay in the store.
- **`zbc destroy <env> support-email` deletes the managed routing rules and disables routing, and, when `enableSending` is on, also deletes the sending subdomain.** If the domain sends other mail through Cloudflare (a site's own notifications), destroying the instance stops that too. Do not use it on such a domain.
- **Setting `enableRouting: false` and applying again does nothing.** The apply skips the whole routing block when the flag is false (rules, catch-all and the enable call alike), so routing stays on and the catch-all keeps pointing at the Worker. To stop receiving, make a one-off Cloudflare API call with a short-lived token: set the zone's catch-all (`PUT /zones/{zone}/email/routing/rules/catch_all`) to disabled or to drop, and only if the apex MX and SPF should go too, `POST /zones/{zone}/email/routing/disable`. Then set `enableRouting: false` in the instance file so the file matches. Sending is untouched by either call.

## Things to know about the mailbox

- **The catch-all means any address on the domain, mistyped ones included, files under this mailbox.** Mail for another domain is still refused (the entrance checks the envelope recipient against the declared mailboxes' domains).
- **A published address is not required.** If the site publishes a different contact address, the mailbox sees only the addresses people guess, delivery failures of the domain's own mail that come back as mail, and what the owner sends from it. Changing the published address is a change to the site, not to the inbox.
- **Cloudflare controls `Message-ID` and `Date`.** A structured send that names either is refused whole (`E_HEADER_NOT_ALLOWED`). The inbox files the sent message under the id Cloudflare reports. `In-Reply-To` and `References` are allowed. If the reported id equals the Message-ID header (with the angle brackets the inbox adds), a customer's reply threads by `In-Reply-To`; if it differs, threading falls back to the subject and the person, which the tests hold.
- **Sender allow-list.** `allowed_sender_addresses` is the declared mailboxes' addresses; a refusal with `E_SENDER_NOT_VERIFIED` or a sender error on the first reply means first that the sending domain's records are still pending, and if they are active, that the list does not contain the From address. The From is always the mailbox's address. `E_RECIPIENT_NOT_ALLOWED` would mean the binding is limited to known recipients and a customer cannot be answered: stop and say so. `E_DELIVERY_FAILED` is treated as "may have gone out", because its documentation does not say whether Cloudflare had handed the message on; if a live refusal comes back with that code and the message plainly did not arrive, add it to the refusals table (`REFUSALS` in `src/cloudflare/send.ts`).
- **A mailbox is the domain's, not one address's.** The same row serves the entrance, the reply sender and the script's `mailboxes`, which is `[]` only until the first message arrives, because the store records the row on first use.
- **Notifications.** A message Cloudflare delivered notifies the owner's devices once, when it is in the Inbox and not Bulk (a Client's people always; then a person's call; then no List-Unsubscribe header, no automated sender, and a spam score under the line or none), the conversation is open and unread, Cloudflare received it within the hour, and it is not from the mailbox's own address or domain. It is asked after the spam check has answered (or failed, or was not asked), so spam never rings. A message nothing judged (the switch off, the binding missing, the model down or slow) is not Bulk, so it notifies: an outage never hides a customer. The title carries the mailbox's name and the sender, the subject is the body, nothing of the text. A notification is not delivered inside the email handler: the Worker asks the Durable Object for it with `ctx.waitUntil` after the message is filed, because a push service can take up to its timeout per device and Cloudflare documents a time limit on the email handler. If `waitUntil` work is ever cut when the handler returns, the symptom is a message that is filed and never notifies; the next delivery run hands it on if it is still within the hour, and the fix is to deliver from the object's alarm.
- **Mail the mailbox's own address sends** (a site's confirmation mail with that reply-to, a tripwire that emails the address from itself) arrives and is filed, but the notification rule skips mail from the mailbox's own address and domain, so it is silent. That is intended.

## The spam check

- **What it does.** A message is kept in the bucket, filed, and then put to Cloudflare's spam-judging model on Workers AI (model id `@cf/cloudflare/clef`, a third party's name) with the mailbox's own spam questions, unless a rule already settled it (a List-Unsubscribe header or an automated sender: Bulk, no call), a Client's contact is on the conversation (never judged), a person has called the conversation, the message is the mailbox's own, or the switch is off. The score, the kind it chose and the model are stored on the message. **0.80 or more is spam: Bulk, marked Spam, kept, no notification.** Anything under 0.80, or not judged, is in the Inbox and notifies once. Nothing is drafted on arrival.
- **What the owner can do about it.** Mark as spam and Move to the Inbox on any conversation (and `spam <conversation-id> <on|off>` in Claude's script). A person's call decides over every rule but a Client's people, sticks when the same person writes again, and nothing is ever deleted. The switch is the owner's alone: the route refuses Claude's token, and a redeploy never turns back on what the owner turned off. Settings shows the switch, who last used it and how many messages the model has judged; a conversation lists its reasons with the score.
- **The questions are the install's content** and they lean to keeping mail: write them from who really writes to the mailbox (customers, however upset, long, badly written or in another language, are the first kind and the junk question says such a message is never kept out; the people who really write on the keep side are named; only sellers are on the spam side). The line is the shared 0.80 and is not raised for one mailbox: a message wrongly in Bulk is kept, shows its score and is one move back to the Inbox.
- **The binding.** The Worker needs the Workers AI binding `AI`. It authenticates itself: no secret, no var, nothing to create. Whether the deploy token may bind Workers AI is unverified until the first apply with the binding in the Worker's settings; if the apply fails on it, add the permission to the token and apply again. With no binding, every message is filed not judged, in the Inbox, and notifies. The draft chat's model key (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`) is used only by the draft chat: unset, a chat turn is refused with a 503 and nothing on arrival changes.
- **Names that did not change.** The mailbox's switch keeps its first name `assistant` (the mailbox column, the route `PUT /api/mailboxes/:id/assistant`, the script's `mailboxes` block). After a deploy check `bun scripts/inbox.ts mailboxes`: the mailbox's `assistant` block must say `enabled: true` where the declaration turns the check on, and `judged` rises after the first message; `enabled: false` means the model is not being asked, and the owner's switch in Settings turns it on.
- **Before pressing the switch on a new mailbox, measure the questions on the real model.** `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID=<the account> CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN=<a token of it with Workers AI> bun scripts/measure-spam-questions.ts` runs the sample mail through the model with the questions the Worker uses and prints each score, marking any message on the wrong side of 0.80. **Every message that should be kept and scores over the line is a reason not to press the switch**: change the questions, not the line. The deploy token has no Workers AI permission (Cloudflare answers 401), so this needs a token with it; the Worker's binding needs none. Then read the first week's scores: the Bulk view's rows and any message scored between 0.40 and 0.80 show their score in the reason line. A person in Bulk means the questions change, and that conversation is one move back. Switch off at any time: every message is then in the Inbox, and what the model said before stays.
- **A judgment that does not come is not retried**: the message is in the Inbox and notifies, the safe direction. The call is made inside the entrance's filing, after the raw copy and the row, with a five second limit, so a slow model delays that one message's notification by up to five seconds and nothing else.
- **Moving the whole Gmail view onto the same Bulk rule is a view.** It is computed when the queue is read, no Gmail label is read differently or changed, nothing is deleted, and nobody is notified. `bun scripts/inbox.ts bulk-report` says how many open Gmail conversations are Bulk only because the sender is automated, and names the senders, most conversations first, so the owner can move any wanted one back with one click.

## Backups of the store, and restoring it

For a Cloudflare mailbox there is no Gmail to refill from. The bucket holds the raw mail; only the store holds the sorts, drafts, chat, read and closed state and contacts. So the store needs a restore point.

**What is established.**

- The store is one SQLite-backed Durable Object, and one restore covers everything the inbox owns (ADR 0001, decision 2).
- Point-in-time recovery is a feature of SQLite-backed Durable Objects, on by default, covering the whole database for the last 30 days, with nothing to enable. A restore is not in the dashboard or in `wrangler`; the only interface is inside the object: `ctx.storage.getCurrentBookmark()`, `getBookmarkForTime(time)` (within 30 days; a bookmark is a string that sorts in time order) and `onNextSessionRestoreBookmark(bookmark)`, which arranges for the storage to match the bookmark at the object's next restart and returns a bookmark for the moment just before, so a restore can itself be undone. The page says to call `ctx.abort()` afterwards to restart the object.
- It is not available in local development, so no test can exercise it; the restore's tests run over a fake of the storage.
- **The 30 days are the whole window.** After that the store is not recoverable and the bucket holds only the mail. Nothing exports the store elsewhere; for an older copy the store needs an export to the bucket (not built).
- **A restore to before a message arrived loses that message from the store, not from the bucket.** Its raw copy is still there, and filing it again is what the `arrival_owed` path does.

**What exists.** An owner-only restore (`src/restore.ts`) over that recovery. Claude's script has `bun scripts/inbox.ts bookmark [time]`, a read (a time needs its zone, as in an ISO time ending `-07:00`: `bookmark <time>` prints the bookmark for then as `forTime`; `restores` lists the latest restores with their undo bookmarks). The owner restores from Settings, "Restore the store". Claude's token can read a bookmark and do nothing else: both restore routes refuse it, and so does the module under them.

**Before a risky deploy (Claude).** Run `bookmark` and write the `current` value into the deploy notes. It is the moment to go back to if the deploy goes wrong. If it answers 503, Cloudflare gave the real object no bookmark and nothing below can be undone: stop and say so. A migration that is one-way needs a bookmark taken before it, and an earlier bookmark does not cover a later migration.

**To restore (the owner).**

1. Settings, "Restore the store". Pick the time to go back to (the page shows the current bookmark and any earlier restores), or paste a bookmark Claude wrote down, and press "Show what would be restored". Nothing has changed yet.
2. Within five minutes, press "Yes, restore the store". The inbox answers the **undo bookmark**, restarts the store (a few seconds), and comes back as it was at the chosen moment. Reload the page.

**What a restore replaces.** Everything the store holds: conversations, read and closed state, drafts, contacts, the Gmail tokens and sync cursors, notification devices, the audit log, settings (Claude's sending switch and the spam check's included), and the assistant's sorts, drafts and usage count. Model calls made after the chosen moment were already paid for, and work on a message that is filed again after a restore is done and paid for again. Mail that reached the store after the chosen moment is gone from it and its raw copy is still in the bucket. **A message sent after the chosen moment went out all the same, and the store forgets it: it shows as an unsent draft again, so it could be sent a second time. Do not send it again; check the sender's Sent folder (Gmail's, or the mailbox's sent copies in the bucket) first, and Claude must not resend on an old instruction.** The Gmail connection goes back with the store: if an account was reconnected since the chosen moment, the old token may no longer work and it needs connecting again from Settings. A restore to before a migration is re-migrated by the object's constructor when it restarts. After any restore, run `mailbox` and `mailboxes` and compare with the numbers written down; what a store that is behind Gmail does on its first sync (the cursor is back, Gmail is ahead) is worth watching.

**To undo a restore.** A restore answers the bookmark of the moment just before it. Restore to that bookmark with the same screen: paste it, "Show what would be restored", "Yes". That restore answers an undo bookmark of its own, so any restore can be undone and the undo undone. A lost bookmark is not lost: `bookmark` lists the latest ten records, newest first, each with its `undoBookmark`.

**The confirmation.** The first step answers a token good for five minutes and for that one bookmark, and the second step must send the token and the bookmark back, so a restore cannot be made blind or from a stale screen. A newer preparation replaces the earlier one, and a token is spent when it is used. Only the owner's sign-in passes either step, in the route and again in the module (a default-deny on the actor).

**Where the record lives, and why it is not in the store.** Every preparation and every restore is written as a JSON object under `restores/` in the raw-mail bucket, named `restores/<time>-<prepared|restored>-<id>.json`, holding who, when, the target, the store's bookmark at the time and, for a restore, the undo bookmark. The store's own audit table is part of what a restore rolls back, so a row written there would be erased by the restore it records; the bucket is not touched by one. It is written **before** anything changes: if the bucket cannot take the record, a preparation is not made, and a restore that was armed is armed back to the moment it was made and the store restarts once, which applies that and nothing else (an arming left pending would apply at the next restart and erase every write in between). A Worker with no bucket bound restores nothing. The records have no more protection than the mail.

**While a restore is armed the object restarts instead of serving.** From the moment it is armed, the first thing every call into the object and its alarm does is restart it (`restartIfArmed`, the first line of `call()` and of `alarm()`), because a write between arming and the restart would land in a store about to be replaced and could not be undone. The route asks for the restart straight after confirming; if that call never comes, the next call of any kind does it. That call fails once, and the one after it is served.

**Check the restore on a scratch object, never on the real store.** Everything above rests on Cloudflare's documentation and on a fake of the storage. Before relying on it, make a throwaway Worker with its own SQLite-backed class (its own name, `workers_dev` on, no Access, deleted after; a debugging deploy, so raw `wrangler` is fine and the scratch has no state a file must describe) with six methods: `write(n)` (inserts a row and puts a key-value `k` = n), `read()` (the rows and `k`), `bookmark()` (`getCurrentBookmark`), `forTime(iso)` (`getBookmarkForTime`), `arm(bookmark)` (`onNextSessionRestoreBookmark`, answers what it returns) and `restart()` (`ctx.abort()`). Then, in order:

1. `write(1)`, `write(2)`, `write(3)`; `bookmark()` is B; wait ten seconds; `write(4)`, `write(5)`.
2. `forTime` for a moment between the writes, a few seconds after B was taken: expect a bookmark that sorts at or after B and before the later writes' (compare as strings). `forTime` for 31 days ago: expect an error, and note its words.
3. `arm(B)`: it answers U. Call `restart()` as a call of its own: note what the caller sees (an error is expected). `read()`: expect rows 1 to 3 and `k` = 3, both the SQL and the key-value part.
4. `arm(U)` then `restart()` then `read()`: expect rows 1 to 5 and `k` = 5. This is the undo.
5. Arm twice before restarting: `arm(B)`, then `arm(U2)` for the bookmark it was answered, then `restart()`. Expect the store as it was at `arm(U2)` (the second arming replaces the first). The "record could not be kept" path relies on this.
6. Time order: expect B < U < U2 as strings.
7. Delete the scratch Worker (`bunx wrangler delete`).

If step 3 or 4 does not restore, or step 5 restores to B, the restore here is wrong: do not deploy it, and say which. If the abort in step 3 does not restart the object, `restartForRestore` is wrong. A real restore of the real store is never part of a check; the first one is the owner's, for a reason.

Then, on the real install after its deploy, a read is safe: `bookmark` answers a `current` bookmark and `restores: []`.

## Live checks, in order

After both applies, with a second address of your own to write from. Each says what would be learned and what to do if it differs.

1. **Before the zone apply:** `bun scripts/inbox.ts mailbox` (the Gmail numbers: messages, conversations, open, unread), `mailboxes` (each other mailbox's numbers) and the audit's last entry, written down, to compare after.
2. **Apply the space, then the zone instance** and read the second's output: expect `Sending: <your-domain> onboarded` (or already onboarded), `Routing: enabled on zone` and `Routing: catch-all → worker`. A forward-destination line means the instance was changed.
3. **Look at the records:** the zone's DNS in the dashboard, or `dig +short MX <your-domain>`, `dig +short TXT <your-domain>`, `dig +short TXT _dmarc.<your-domain>`, `dig +short TXT cf-bounce.<your-domain>`. Expect the list above and **exactly one `v=spf1` at the apex**. Record what `_dmarc` says: its `rua` address, if any, receives reports and should be an address someone reads.
4. **Mail in.** Write to `support@<your-domain>`, and again to `anything@<your-domain>`. Expect both within seconds under the mailbox's name in the Rail, unread and open, and a notification titled with the mailbox's name and the sender. If the sender is told the address does not exist, or the mail never shows, read Email Routing's activity log for the zone and check that the space was applied first.
5. **Capture a real message.** Save the raw message from the bucket (the key is on the message as `rawKey` in the script's `conversation`) as a fixture for the tests: the tests' fixtures are written by hand to the documented shapes, because a worktree has no account to send through, and the tests assert the content so a captured one can replace them.
6. **Reply in the thread.** Reply from your own address to the first message: expect the same conversation (In-Reply-To), two messages, two raw copies. Then send the first message's text again from a different address with the same subject: expect a conversation of its own (no outside person in common).
7. **Redelivery.** Cloudflare's own retry cannot be forced; the nearest is forwarding the same message twice. Expect nothing new for a duplicate Message-ID.
8. **What Cloudflare does when the Worker's handler throws.** Cloudflare does not document it. Learn it on a test address while only that address routes to the Worker: apply a build whose `RAW` binding is missing (the Worker then refuses to keep a copy and throws), send one message, and read, from the sender's side, whether it bounces or its mail server queues and retries, and what Email Routing's activity log says. Then restore the binding. If the sender gets a permanent bounce, the hope that a failure is temporary and the mail is retried is wrong, and an outage of the bucket needs its own plan (the handler already never bounces for a failure after the copy is kept).
9. **What the handler is handed.** That `message.to` is the envelope recipient for a catch-all (the domain check depends on it), `message.from` the envelope sender, and `message.rawSize` the size. The first real message is the check. The entrance also heads the key and puts only when it is absent, so a retry never rewrites a copy: expect `customMetadata` (from, to, receivedAt) on the object.
10. **The first reply out.** Reply from the space. Expect it from `<Name> <support@<your-domain>>`, in the same thread as the original in your own mail, within a minute. In your mail's Show original, SPF, DKIM and DMARC should all say pass. Record the time and the three results. If the send is refused, read the code in "Things to know about the mailbox" above; a refusal is also the proof that a failed send never shows as sent: the draft is still there and nothing is on the conversation.
11. **What Message-ID and References Cloudflare really wrote.** In Show original copy `Message-ID`, `In-Reply-To` and `References`; `In-Reply-To` must be the id of your original. Read the kept copy (`bunx wrangler r2 object get <bucket>/out/<message id> --remote`, the id is `rawKey` on the sent message): `reportedMessageId` is what the binding returned. Equal to the Message-ID header means a customer's reply threads by In-Reply-To; different means it falls back to the subject and the person.
12. **A recipient that is not your own address.** Reply from a message sent from a second account, so the recipient is an address Cloudflare has never seen: expect it to arrive. This is the check that a customer, who is not a verified destination of anything, can be answered. If the first reply worked and this one is refused, the binding is limited to known recipients and a customer cannot be answered.
13. **`messageId` is always returned.** The code treats a resolved send with no id as an acceptance (the contract is that a refusal throws). If a real send shows no id (`reportedMessageId` null in the copy), threading is by subject only.
14. **The copy.** `out/<id>` exists in the bucket for each sent message, holds the request exactly as sent (`from`, `to`, `subject`, `text`, `headers`) and Cloudflare's id, and nothing deleted anything under `in/`.
15. **A new email from a contact's card** starts a draft from the mailbox the contact last wrote to (the card and the draft form say which): send it and expect a conversation of its own, closed (nobody has written in it yet); an answer joins it by subject.
16. **A Gmail reply still goes as the owner.** Reply to any ordinary Gmail conversation: it must still go through Gmail, from the owner's address (the two transports never cross; a test holds it, this is the real check).
17. **Claude's send** on a business-mailbox draft, with the switch on and a recorded instruction, goes from the mailbox's address and appears in `sent-by-claude` with the instruction.
18. **The assistant.** If the mailbox has a persona, open a conversation and ask "write a reply": expect a short reply signed as the persona says, that says what it could not confirm in the note when the knowledge base is empty, and that does not say what the law, a price or a date allows when the persona's rules say it must not. Ask for something it should decline and read what it does.
19. **Other mailboxes are as they were.** `mailboxes` again, compared with check 1.
20. **The switch.** Settings shows the mailbox's spam-check switch as the declaration set it; turning it on after the measuring step above is the owner's.

## What the tests prove, and what they cannot

- **Over a real SQLite, the real router and Mailroom, a bucket that can fail and a fake `send_email` binding** (`test-support/fake-email.ts`): the entrance, filing, threading, deduplication, the owed path, the stamps, the views, Bulk by the queue's one rule, a reply from the mailbox's address with the threading headers and no Message-ID or Date, filed only on acceptance with its copy kept, every refusal code putting the draft back byte for byte unchanged, every unknown outcome holding it as `sending` and refusing a second press, a double press sending once, the two transports never crossing, and Claude's send needing the switch and the instruction (`test/cloudflare-arrival.test.ts`, `cloudflare-parse.test.ts`, `cloudflare-thread.test.ts`, `cloudflare-send.test.ts`, `schema.test.ts`, and the per-mailbox suites).
- **The spam check over a fake Workers AI binding:** a person's message put to the model once (the model id, the message as `state`, the mailbox's questions) with score, kind and model stored; a List-Unsubscribe message and a no-reply sender Bulk with no call; a Client's contact always Inbox and never sent to the model; 0.80 spam and 0.79 not; the newest message deciding; a failed call, a call that never answers, a score outside 0 to 1, a kind outside the list, an answer of no shape and a missing binding each leaving the message filed, not judged, in the Inbox and notifying; the switch off asking nothing; a redelivery judged once; the owner's own address and domain neither judged nor notified; a person's call sticking and being audited (`test/spam-check.test.ts`).

Those are this section's live checks.

## Gaps in the infra modules

Recorded so each can become a module capability later; none is built.

- A declared mailbox is a var made by the kit, not a `workerVars` entry: `workerVars` passes values with `--var`, which has no safe spelling for a list of objects.
- The r2 module has no per-bucket protection (it can create and destroy a bucket; a destroy of a non-empty bucket is refused by Cloudflare, which is what keeps the raw copies safe, but a bucket lock or lifecycle rule is checked by hand).
- The cloudflare module has no `ai` handling and needs none (wrangler reads the binding from the Worker's settings); what it cannot do is say whether the deploy token may bind Workers AI, which turns a plan-time answer into a failed apply.
