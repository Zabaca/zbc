# Every mailbox has people, and a person sees only theirs

Status: accepted. A mailbox per person, and shared ones: the first of the team steps. People kept by the space itself and claiming a conversation in a shared mailbox are not built here; this is designed so that the first replaces the source of the lists and the second adds an assignee, with nothing here redone.

## What this supersedes, and what it leaves

- **Supersedes** the rule of ADR 0003 and the company layer's ADR 0003 that the inbox admits the install's owner and nobody else, and that every mailbox is the owner's. Any person of the install is admitted once they are on at least one mailbox, and sees only the mailboxes they are on.
- **Leaves** Claude's script and the rule that Claude sends only on the owner's word as they were (ADR 0001 decision 15): they stay the owner's, and Claude reads and sends with the owner's scope. **Leaves** everything that changes the install's setup the owner's alone: connecting or disconnecting Gmail, Claude's sending switch, the spam check's switch, restore, the operator's three, linking a Client and the contacts' writes (rename, fields, merge, split).

## Decisions

1. **A mailbox has people: one person, or a list.** One table, `mailbox_person` (migration 19, store version 20): the mailbox, the person by their work address (the declaration's `people` address, which is the actor every write stamps; the address they sign in with is only how Access names them), who put them there and when. A person may be on several mailboxes and a mailbox has several people; a Gmail account a person connected is a mailbox with that one person on it, a shared mailbox is the same shape with a list. No foreign key to `mailbox`, which has been made again three times.
2. **The rule is one pure function, and everything else is a translation of it.** `canSee(scope, mailboxId)` in `src/queue.ts`, the inbox's pure queue module. A scope is the ids of the mailboxes a person is on, plus whether what belongs to no mailbox is theirs (the install's owner's: a text or a note, a draft not made from any mailbox, a contact with no mail). `scopeWhere` (`src/store/scope.ts`) is the same rule as SQL for the reads that cut in the database, held equal to it by `test/mail-scope.test.ts` over a real SQLite. `ALL_MAIL` is for the store's own jobs and never for a read that answers a person.
3. **Every read is cut before it is counted or cut to a screen.** The queue's facts, the lists, search, the menu's counts, the Drafts list, the contacts and their counts and mailboxes, a Client's counts and page, Claude's reads and the audit, notifications and their week all take the scope: no screen is cut from mail that is not the person's, and no count gives away mail that is not theirs. The conversations and messages named by an id (a file, a mark, a close, a reopen, spam, the draft chat, a draft's edits, a send) are asked first (`seesConversation`, `seesMessage`, `src/space-gate.ts`), and an id the person may not see is a 404 exactly as one that does not exist.
4. **A contact is seen through the mail they are on.** A contact is shown to a person when they are on a conversation, or hold a draft, in a mailbox the person is on, with counts over that mail alone; one with no mail at all belongs to no mailbox and is the owner's. Read marks stay shared within a mailbox (a person's read marks it read for everyone on it, and in Gmail).
5. **A person sends only from the mailboxes they are on.** A send of a draft or a reply is refused (404 by the part, 403 by the store) for a mailbox that is not theirs; a new email goes from a mailbox chosen among theirs (the contact's last, else their default Gmail account, never the owner's), and a person on none a new email could go out from is refused before a draft is made. The part checks and the store checks again where every route to a send passes (`createDraft`, `planSend`), so a route that forgets cannot send. Claude's sends are checked with the owner's scope.
6. **Where the people come from is a port, and the declaration is the source until people are kept by the space.** A declared business mailbox (Cloudflare's or the mail server's) names its `people` in the company declaration (work addresses of people of the install, each once; left out, the owner alone). A Gmail account is its connector's, written when it is connected. The Telegram mailbox is the owner's. `settleMailboxPeople` makes the table agree with the source when the object starts, before it serves a request, takes off any address that is no person of the install (an actor that was never a person, or one who has left), and gives a mailbox nobody is on to the owner. Adding a person to a mailbox is therefore a deploy of the declaration; keeping people in the space changes the source and not one read.
7. **A device is its person's.** Whoever turned notifications on owns the device (one from before devices had people is the owner's); a person lists, tests and removes only their own, and a notification goes only to the devices of people who may see its mail: devices owed the same notifications are handed them together exactly as before, a different set is handed its own, and a notification no device may see is dropped.
8. **The Inbox admits the owner always and anyone else once they are on a mailbox.** The owner connects the first Gmail account before any mailbox exists, so they are admitted with none. A person on none is `not-admitted` on every method, as a stranger is.

## Deferred to a later team step (named so they are not read as decided here)

- **Each person connects their own Gmail.** The owner alone connects an account today (the Gmail connection stays owner-only); an account is its connector's, so the day connecting opens to a person it is theirs without a change to what is read.
- **A Gmail account shared with people.** The lists already allow it (`mailbox_person` holds any number of people on any kind of mailbox), but nothing yet sets the list of a Gmail account: a business mailbox's is the declaration's `people`, and a Gmail account's is its connector alone until people are kept by the space and the owner can put people on it.

## Consequences

- **For an install whose declaration names no `people`, the outcome is exactly what it was for the owner.** The migration fills each Gmail account with its connector (`connected_by`), and the first start fills every business mailbox, the mail server's and Telegram's with the owner alone: the declaration names no `people` for any of them, so the owner alone is on each. The owner's scope is every mailbox and what belongs to none, so nothing is cut for them. A suite over every kind of mailbox pins it by comparing what the owner is shown with what the store holds, and the existing suites pass unchanged.
- **Migration 19 is one-way** in the way every migration here is: a build from before it refuses a store at version 20, so the deploy takes a bookmark first, and an undo is a build of the old code that still carries it. It adds a table and an index and rewrites no row; `test/schema-people.test.ts` runs it over a store with the previous schema.
- **A mailbox nobody is on cannot be made.** A row with no people is the owner's from the next start, so a person taken off a list never leaves mail that nobody can see. A person put on a list who is not a person of the install is refused at deploy by the declaration's check.
- **A second install with a shared mailbox** listing two owners admits both. For example, a declared shared mailbox listing Tessa Marlow and Owen Hale admits both; Owen Hale, a second owner, is let into its Inbox and shown only that mailbox, and what is only the owner's stays Tessa Marlow's.
- **Not done here, and named so it is not mistaken for done:** the calendar reads every connected Google account for any owner of an install (a later step makes it per person); the Settings page draws the owner's controls for a person who is refused them (they answer refused); the Telegram bridge is the owner's, and its door is not cut to a scope (it pushes the owner's own Telegram in).
