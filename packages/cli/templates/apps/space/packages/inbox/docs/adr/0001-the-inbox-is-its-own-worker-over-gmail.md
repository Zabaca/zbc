# ADR 0001: The inbox is its own Worker with its own store

> Code comments in this package cite this record as "ADR-007" (its earlier number) and as "decision N"; both mean this record and its numbered decisions.

## Status
**Accepted.** Recorded with the first build of the inbox, which makes the app, store, sign-in, drafts, contacts, Claude's
script and the Outbox carry. Gmail, sending and the Client directory arrive in later decisions and
follow this record. This is the part of the original spec that outlives the build. Decision 16 records the
installable app and its notifications. Decision 17 records a
second kind of mailbox, fed by Cloudflare; decision 18 records
sending as that mailbox's address, decision 19 the assistant that runs on it, and decision 20 the owner-only restore of the store. Decision 26 lets Claude choose the mailbox a new email is drafted from. Decision 22 gives a Cloudflare mailbox's mail the same Inbox and Bulk rules as Gmail's with Cloudflare's Clef model as the one judgment, takes the sort and the drafting on arrival away from decision 19's assistant, and says which parts of decisions 17, 19 and 21 it supersedes. Decision 23 puts the draft chat on every email conversation, the owner's own Gmail included, which removes the "Cloudflare mailbox only" of decisions 19 and 21 and reverses, for drafting when the owner asks, the original "no assistant inside the inbox". Decision 24 adds a second Cloudflare mailbox and says the spam check's questions are written per mailbox, first one mailbox's alone and then the second's own.

## Context

The owner reads and answers their mail in Gmail. The first tool built for their messages held drafts they copied out and sent by hand. They want an inbox that receives the owner's address, sends as them, groups people under the Clients the company already
keeps, and grows into other channels. The site is where Clients live, so the first question was
whether the inbox is part of the site.

## Decision

**1. The inbox is its own Worker (`packages/inbox`, on a hostname of its own), not routes inside the site's.**
It will hold a key that can read, send and relabel the owner's mail and answer one public push
endpoint; the site deploys with no staging in front of it and holds signed Agreements. Receiving
also needs a Durable Object with alarms and a daily trigger the site does not have.

**2. Everything the inbox owns lives in one SQLite-backed Durable Object, not in the site's D1.** A
D1 binding cannot be read-only, so sharing the site's database would give a Worker with a
send-capable key write access to a legal record. One Durable Object also serializes writes, which
the double press of Send and the claim of a conversation both rely on. Its migrations are code,
append-only, applied when it is constructed (`packages/inbox/src/store/schema.ts`).

**3. Clients are read through the site, never copied.** The site stays the only owner and writer
of Clients and gains one read-only entrance for the inbox, a service binding that never touches the
internet or Access. The inbox keeps no list of businesses of its own, and a contact holds only a
Client id that the directory vouches for when it is assigned. Nothing assigns a Client by matching
an address, which is a standing rule.

*Superseded by the space's [ADR 0021](../../../space/docs/adr/0021-clients-are-a-part-of-the-space-with-a-store-of-their-own-and-sources-push-them-in.md): the Clients are the space's own, in a store of
their own (`packages/clients`), made and edited in Settings and, for a company's website, pushed in by the site. The inbox still keeps no list and copies nothing: it asks the Clients part in-process whenever a request needs
Clients. "Nothing assigns a Client by matching an address" stands.*

**4. Gmail is the mailbox of record and the inbox is a working copy.** If the store were lost, mail
and its read and closed state would come back by resync; contacts, Client assignments, send records
and drafts would not. A push from Google is a hint, not a delivery: the inbox keeps its own cursor,
asks Gmail what changed since it, and stores the same message any number of times as one.

**5. Read and closed follow Gmail's unread and Inbox labels, with Gmail changed first.** The inbox
changes only those two labels, on conversations it holds, and its Gmail module has no way to ask for
any other change. Claude's reads never mark anything read.

**6. The token that can send lives only in the store.** Not in a secrets file: the only place an
application key could live is the install's secrets file, which whoever can deploy code that reads the
store can already decrypt, so it would add nothing.

**7. Claude sends only a draft already stored in the inbox, on the owner's instruction, and the
instruction is recorded with the send.** There is no approval step and no mode: the exact text is on
record before it leaves, a send without an instruction or with the switch off is refused, and the
instruction (the owner's words as Claude received them, where the owner said them, when) is Claude's own account,
because the inbox cannot see a chat. One switch, which only the owner can change, stops Claude sending
and leaves its drafting alone.

**8. The Google side may share a Google project with other tooling.** The owner accepted that the inbox
and another credential on the same project (an advertising API credential, in the original install) then stand or fall together; the inbox keeps its own sign-in client to keep them
apart where that is cheap. A deploy service account of its own was also considered; the
organization blocks service account key creation and keeps that policy, so the Google module runs as
the operator through their own gcloud login instead (decision 10), and there is no such account or key.

**9. Access is verified in the Worker, with a copy of the site's verifier.** Access protects a
hostname, and the push hostname has no Access application, so the Worker never assumes one is in
front of it. Two copies of the verifier is the accepted cost until a third consumer makes a shared
package worth its weight.

**10. Gmail receiving: the watch, the operator's identity and resumable work.** The
watch is unfiltered, PROVISIONALLY: it publishes every change in the mailbox, because a label filter
could hide the change that matters most (a message leaving the Inbox) and a push that finds nothing
costs one history call. The choice is conditional on the post-deploy observations
(a reply sent from Gmail, a read and an archive on the phone each reaching the inbox within a
minute, listed in `docs/runbooks/inbox-google-setup.md`); until those are made this is a default, not
a finding, and the observations may change it. The Google module authenticates as the operator
(`gcloud auth print-access-token`, the operator's own address, an Owner of the project) and refuses any other
account, because applies run from the operator's own machine only and a key would have been a standing
credential for a project shared with other tooling. Gmail's work is resumable: the first fill and a resync are jobs kept in the mailbox
row and advanced a budget of fetches at a time, because a Worker invocation has a hard subrequest
limit. The push endpoint's URL is also the audience of Google's token, so the Worker takes its push
hostname, path and audience from one setting, which the Google module outputs. The message status
`sent` is also what a received message carries, with `direction` telling them apart, because SQLite
could not alter the column's CHECK for a new value.

**11. The organization policy is overridden for the project.**
An organization that enforces domain restricted sharing (`iam.allowedPolicyMemberDomains`)
refuses the publisher grant for Gmail's own account (`gmail-api-push@system.gserviceaccount.com`,
outside the organization). The owner chose a permanent exception for this project only: the constraint
is overridden to allow all on the project (set with `gcloud org-policies
set-policy`), and the rest of the organization keeps the rule. The risk is that anyone with Owner on
that project can now share it with accounts outside the organization, and the project may also hold
another credential; it is accepted on top of decision 8, and the override lives in Google, not in this
code. The same apply showed two smaller facts the runbook records: the IAM API must be on as
well as Gmail and Pub/Sub, and a push subscription created seconds after its service account can fail
with INVALID_ARGUMENT until the account propagates (a re-run succeeds).

**12. Read and closed are kept in step with Gmail.** For an email conversation the state is derived on every read from Gmail's labels on its
messages: unread while any carries UNREAD, open while any carries INBOX. Migration 3 adds read and
closed stamps (when and by whom: a person, `agent:`, or `gmail`) to `message`, and every write of a
message's labels goes through one function that keeps the stamps true to them, so a change made in
Gmail and one made here leave the same shape. For a manual conversation the stamps are the whole
state, and a message marked sent by hand is filed read and closed, as Gmail files a sent-only
conversation. The inbox changes Gmail first, on the whole thread, through exactly three fixed changes
in the Gmail module (take UNREAD off, take INBOX off, put INBOX on; nothing takes a label as an
argument), and applies the same change to the messages it holds only when Gmail accepts, serialized
with the sync; Gmail's history event for the change then finds it done and changes nothing, which is
also why the person's name stays on the stamp. Opening a conversation (the owner) marks it read;
Claude's script has no command for that and the endpoint refuses the agent, a firmer rule than "its
reads mark nothing" because a refusal cannot be broken by a later change to what a read does. Claude
may close and reopen. The queue's Inbox view excludes Bulk and Closed, Bulk is the open bulk, and Closed
is every closed conversation; the exemption for a Client's people arrives with the Client directory. Read and Close act on the
whole thread in Gmail, which would cover a message that arrived after the person's page was loaded, so
both carry how far the page reached (`through`) and the inbox refuses with a 409, changing nothing, when
a message the change would affect (unread, for a read; in the Inbox, for a close) is stored beyond it.
Before it changes Gmail it also reads the thread there and stores every message it does not hold, so a
reply still on its way through Pub/Sub is caught too; old messages from before the first fill are stored
the same way and refuse nothing unless the change would affect them. Claude's script is given
`through` by the caller (the number `conversation` printed) and does not look one up, so it cannot
archive a reply it has not read. What is left is the interval between the thread read and the change,
milliseconds, which Gmail's own app has as well. A thread deleted in Gmail is closed (or marked read)
here without a request, since there is nothing to be out of step with; this is the one case where the
inbox changes without Gmail having accepted. A message in the trash, in
spam or an unfinished draft takes no part in whether a conversation is read or open, and reopening
puts INBOX only on messages in play, one request each and never a batch; if Gmail refuses part way
the answer says the conversation is partly reopened, and the inbox shows it open once Gmail reports the
messages that did move. The queue's counts cover every conversation and its rows are the first screen
of the filter asked for, so they agree with the numbers Settings shows for the Gmail connection.

**13. Sending: one path, claimed once, and what a failure means.** One send
path, `Mailroom.sendDraft`, runs inside the same `exclusive` as the sync, and it plans, builds the
message, claims, calls Gmail once and files. The claim is one conditional write (draft to sending,
only while the row is still an outbound draft with no Gmail id at the revision the press named), so
a second press, a retried request and a double click find it taken. The press names a revision
(`rev`, a counter added to every draft edit) and a reply also names `through`, so a draft edited
after it was shown, or a reply over a message that arrived after the page was drawn, is refused
(409) with nothing sent; the reply check also reads the thread in Gmail first, as Close does. The
recipients are the draft's own address and Cc, written into the draft when it is made and shown on
it; there is no Bcc input anywhere, an address must be printable ASCII and bare, none may be ours,
and an em dash in the subject or body is refused at the send whoever wrote it (a draft is policed
only when an agent writes it, so a draft the owner typed, or one carried in, is caught here). A reply
goes to the newest inbound message's Reply-To, else its From (messages stored before this step did
not record Reply-To, so the first reply drafted on one reads it back from Gmail). What a failure
means is decided in the Gmail module, once: a refusal (4xx, a sign-in that no longer works, a token
that could not be had) means nothing went out and the draft is a draft again; anything that leaves
it unknown whether Gmail acted (a dropped connection after the request left, a 5xx, a 200 that
names no message) is `GmailSendUnknown`, which is not a `GmailError`, so no handler of "Gmail
refused" can catch it, and the draft stays `sending` and is never sent again by anything. Only the owner
can say, after looking in Gmail Sent, whether it went out (`resolveSend`). A refused
send returns to draft; what an unanswered one does is a separate question, and returning it to draft would
let the next press deliver a second copy. The agent's send was at first refused in the route, the Mailroom
and the store; Claude's own send arrived with decision 15 on this same path. Migration 4
adds the revision and the Reply-To.

Three choices made on purpose and recorded here so they are not found
by accident. (a) A refusal from Gmail that says the input was bad (HTTP 400) is answered 422, not
502: a refused send is otherwise answered 502, but a failed send should say whether
to fix what was typed or to try again later, and 400 is Gmail saying the former. Every other
refusal is a 502. (b) "Copy everyone" copies everyone on the message being answered (its Reply-To
or From, To and Cc, less us and the recipient, as Gmail's Reply all does), not everyone who has
ever been on the conversation: a person who dropped off the thread several messages ago is the
surprising address to copy (an address the author types is a separate list, decision 35). (c) Mark sent is for texts and DMs only: an email
is sent with Send, and an email sent by hand in Gmail arrives by sync as itself, so a record made by
hand as well would be a second copy of one email and a draft that could be unmarked and sent again.
The carry of an earlier outbox's drafts still files an email that outbox had marked sent.

Two details of the send that follow from the safety rule. Every removal of a carried draft writes the
same tombstone (`Inbox.tombstone`): a deleted draft, and a stuck send the owner says went out, because a
re-run of the carry would otherwise bring an email that went out back as a draft to be sent again.
And "it did not go out" is refused when Gmail's own copy of the message, with the Message-ID the
claim recorded, is already held, since that is Gmail saying it did; where Gmail rewrote the
Message-ID there is nothing to match and the call stays the owner's. Each of the two answers asks once
more in the page before it acts. The message carries a Date header made from the same clock as the
sent time it is filed under, and a subject that holds `=?` or is too long for one line is sent as
encoded words, so a stranger's subject cannot read as something it is not.

**14. Clients: a directory read through the site.** The directory is
`ClientDirectory`, a named `WorkerEntrypoint` exported from the site's `src/worker.ts` beside its
default export, with one method, `list`, that reads through `listClients` (the admin's own listing)
and reduces each Client to five fields: id, name, legal entity, sign-in address and the Mirror's
status with the time Stripe said it. It has no `fetch`, so HTTP cannot reach it, and no other file
in the site imports it (a test holds both). The inbox binds to it as `CLIENTS`, a service binding
in its `wrangler.jsonc`, and a named export surviving the adapter's build was checked rather than
assumed: the built `dist/server/entry.mjs` exports it, and it answered over a service binding
under workerd with the real shape. The inbox reads the directory per request and keeps none of it;
what comes back is checked against the exact shape and refused whole if it is not (a list with a
Client silently missing would read as a Client gone). `contact` gains `client_id` and who and when
(migration 5), with no foreign key because the Client is in another store. The Worker, not the store,
asks the directory before an assignment is written: an id it does not list is 422, an unreachable
directory is 503, and taking a contact out of a Client needs no answer. Mail and everything that is
not a Client's name carry on when the directory is down, and Clients show as "Client 7". The Bulk
exemption and the Client filters are decided from the ids the store holds and so do not move when
the directory does. Suggestions are a pure function over the directory and every contact's email
addresses; they are shown on the contact and through the script and written nowhere, and accepting
one is the same assignment as any other. Whether a contact is an automated sender is computed the
same way, when a contact is read, from its name, its addresses and whether every conversation it
is on is Bulk (a contact under a Client never is). A contact with a free-mail address (the domains the
suggestions already ignore, `FREE_MAIL_DOMAINS`: gmail.com and the like) is exempt from the all-Bulk reason only,
because a person's only conversation can land in Bulk and a newsletter does not send from gmail.com; its name and
its no-reply reasons still apply. Never stored. Merge and split are the
store's and the owner's or agent's:
a merge moves every address to the survivor, which keeps its own name and Client (the other's only if
it has none), and the audit keeps both contacts as they were; a split moves one address to a new
contact named by it, with no Client, and refuses a contact's only address.

*The first half of this decision is superseded by the space's ADR 0021: the directory is the Clients part, asked in-process (no `ClientDirectory`, no `CLIENTS` binding, no
parsing of a site's answer), and "an unreachable directory is 503" now means the part itself not answering, never a source being down. An id the part does not hold is still 422, and so is an archived
Client's, with a sentence. Everything from "Mail and everything that is not a Client's name carry on" onward stands.*

Four readings chosen on purpose. (a) The Client filter is a Client's OPEN conversations,
and a Client has one when something of theirs is open; their closed conversations are on the Client's
page, which lists every one, so the filter is the work to do and not the history. (b) The Bulk
exemption looks at everyone on the conversation ("one of the participants"), not
only the sender, so a Client's staff copied on a vendor's receipt keeps it out of Bulk. (c) The install's own
domain is never evidence for a domain suggestion, because our address is on every
conversation and one mistaken assignment of our own contact would otherwise suggest that Client for
everybody at that domain; the free-mail list is otherwise fixed in code. (d) Taking a
contact out of a Client is allowed, because an assignment that cannot be undone is a trap, and it is
audited as its own action.

**Deploying it was two applies, the site first:** the site's directory entrance had to exist before the inbox bound to it. (This goes with the first half of the decision, superseded above: there is no binding now.)

**15. Claude sends on the owner's instruction.** **Superseded in part by decision 25: "the inbox serves no other file" (the picture route below) holds for the owner's Gmail only; the inbox now also gives back the files of mail that came through a Cloudflare mailbox, to its owner, as downloads.** There is no second send path. Claude's send is `POST /api/drafts/:id/send`, the route
the owner's press uses, through the same `Mailroom.sendDraft`: the same plan, the same one conditional
claim, the same single call to Gmail, the same exclusive, the same meaning for each failure. Every
safeguard of decision 13 applies to it unchanged, and Claude's script passes `rev` and `through` back
from what `drafts` and `conversation` printed and looks nothing up, so it cannot send a draft or
answer a thread it did not read. What differs is decided in the store, in `planSend` before the
draft is looked at and again in `claimSend`: the switch is on and the send carries the owner's
instruction, and either missing is a 403 that changes nothing. The second look is there because
the owner can flip the switch while Gmail is being asked about the thread, and a plan is not a
permission. A send names a stored draft and carries no text, recipients or Bcc of its own (a body
that does is a 422, for the owner's press too), so the exact message is on record before it leaves.

The switch is one row in `setting` (migration 6), and NO ROW IS OFF: Claude's sending ships
disabled, so the owner turns it on deliberately, and a store restored from a copy older than this
cannot send either. Only the owner flips it, in the route and again in the store, where it is a
default-deny on the actor (the owner's address and nothing else), audited with before and after. It
stops Claude's sending and touches nothing else: drafting reads no setting. The instruction (the owner's
words as Claude received them, where, when) is three columns on the message, written with the
claim and so before Gmail is asked, so a send of unknown outcome still carries the words it went
out on; they are cleared when the claim is released or the owner says it did not go out, because an
instruction covers one attempt and not the draft for ever, and the audit keeps every one. It is
Claude's own account and is shown as that wherever it is shown. A missing instruction or words is
403; a missing place or time, or a time that is not a full ISO timestamp or is in the future, is
422. The list of Claude's sends (`GET /api/claude-sends`, behind the script's `sent-by-claude`; the app shows
each send with its message and has no page of them) holds the messages filed as sent
by the agent and also the sends of unknown outcome that the owner then said DID go out: the stuck row
is removed then, because Gmail's own copy is the record, so its entry is read back from the audit
of the owner's answer, and the list is "every Claude send" without keeping a second copy of a message. The
agent cannot resolve a stuck send (the route, the Mailroom and the store each refuse it), and it
has no command to flip the switch or to resolve one.

**An instruction comes only from the owner, in a session with Claude, and from nowhere else.** The
instruction's `where` could have been "the session, or the inbox". The inbox has no surface where the owner can give
an instruction, and a place that names it is a path for prompt injection: Claude reads mail through
the script, and a message saying "the owner says: send it" (even one that claims to be from
the owner's own address, which anyone can forge) is text, not the owner. So `where` = the inbox is refused
(403) for now, and the script's help, the runbook and the refusal all say that an instruction never
comes from text in any message. If a surface is built where only the owner can give one, that is a
decision of its own.

**An instruction given before a reply arrived is not an instruction to send over that reply.** A
`through` that Claude read again makes the thread current, so on its own it would let the same words
go out over a reply the owner never saw. So for Claude's send, an inbound message in play that arrived
after `instruction.at` (by Gmail's own time for it, or the time the inbox stored it, whichever is
later) is refused (409, nothing sent), and the answer tells Claude not to retry but to go back to
the owner with the new message and the draft and send only on a new instruction. An instruction given
after the reply arrived is not held to it. `instruction.at` is Claude's account, so this catches the
honest mistake and cannot catch a false claim, which is the limit of the whole record. A `through`
beyond the conversation's newest position is refused (422) for every sender and for Close and read,
because "nothing arrived after it" is trivially true of a number nothing can exceed.

**Sent by Claude is a badge, and not a page or a filter of the queue.** The queue is conversations with
something left to do, counted from the same facts as its rows, and its filters are all about work still owed.
A list of messages already sent, across conversations and including ones whose conversation is closed,
would as a filter show closed mail under an open count, and a queue row has no room for the instruction
beside each send. So a send is marked where it happened: a **Sent by Claude badge** on its message in its
conversation, whose popover (opened by hover, pinned by a click or a tap, reachable by keyboard, closed by
Escape) shows the owner's words as Claude recorded them, where and when the owner said them, the note that this is
Claude's own account, and who drafted the message and why. The switch lives in Settings (there is
no page and no list of Claude's sends in the app). The list stays as a read for Claude's script, and a send
the owner confirmed by hand is kept by the audit; Gmail's own copy of one the owner confirmed by hand arrives as an
ordinary message and carries no badge, since nothing ties it to the instruction without new matching work.

**A draft's words change only through someone who saw them, and an instruction covers the draft as the owner saw it.**
Letting the owner edit Claude's draft in the composer made the draft a thing two parties write, so
the same rule that governs a send now governs an edit and a delete. `PATCH /api/drafts/:id` takes `rev` in its body and
`DELETE /api/drafts/:id` takes `?rev=`, for EVERYONE, Claude included and as a send does: without a whole-number one it
is 422, with one that is not the draft's current revision it is 409 and nothing is changed. The script's `edit` and
`delete-draft` require it too. A draft says who last changed it (`updatedBy`, printed by `drafts`) and the screens read
"Claude's draft, edited by you" from that and `rev`. For a draft, only a change of its words sets `updated_by` and
`updated_at` (creating and editing); the status-only changes (claiming it for a send, putting it back after a refused one,
resolving a stuck one as not sent, marking a text sent, un-marking it) and a send that goes out leave them alone, so an attempt
to send never reads as an edit. Claude's send is refused (409, like a reply that arrived after the instruction) when anyone
other than Claude created or edited the draft after `instruction.at`, found in the audit (every creation and edit is there, so
a later edit of Claude's cannot hide the owner's): the owner agreed to words the owner saw, and the refusal sends Claude back to the owner. It is refused the same way (with words of its own) when ANYONE, Claude included, did anything to the draft after `instruction.at` that the audit records, EXCEPT Claude's own edit of it that leaves who it goes to (its To and Cc) as it was, and the send path's own record of an attempt (`message.sending`, `message.send-refused`; a refused send goes back to a draft and may be tried again on the same word): so a draft created after it, imported from the Outbox after it, marked sent and put back after it, resolved or talked over with the assistant after it, or given other recipients after it, is not covered (decision 35). The rule is an allow-list on purpose: a new way of putting a sendable draft in place is covered without anyone listing it. Claude's own edit of the words is still not held against it. The
instruction's time is a full ISO timestamp with seconds so that an edit and a "send it" in the same minute order correctly. The conversation
answer also says what a reply would be before it is a draft (`reply`: who it goes to, the subject it takes, everyone "copy
everyone" works from; the same reply plan a draft uses), so the composer can name the recipient before anything is saved. A subject is at
most 300 characters. None of this changes what may be sent or to whom; it stops a save or a delete from landing over a
change its author never saw.

**`/admin/send` is not for correspondence, and only the runbook stops an agent using it.** The
site's endpoint admits the agent's token, stores no draft first and records no instruction, so
nothing in code refuses an agent that sends a client a message through it. The rule is a written
one, kept with the site, and the inbox is the path that records an instruction. Closing the gap in code would be a
change to the site's admin, which is outside this record.

Three readings chosen on purpose. (a) The instruction's time is compared with the draft's changes by anyone but
Claude (see "A draft's words change only through someone who saw them"), and not with Claude's own: "draft it and send
it" is one sentence from the owner, so Claude drafting after the instruction is no reason to refuse, and the inbox cannot check
a claim about a chat in either direction. The revision Claude read is what ties a send to the text it saw. (b) the owner's own send ignores an
instruction handed to it and never records one, because a record of an instruction the owner did not give
Claude would be a false one. (c) Claude may send a draft the owner wrote, since the owner's "send it" can be
about the owner's own draft; the record shows who wrote it and who sent it.

**A picture attached to a message is read from Gmail when someone opens it, and is never kept.** The conversation
shows a thumbnail for a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP attachment (Gmail shows them and the chip did not).
`GET /api/messages/<id>/attachments/<n>` asks the Mailroom, which reads the message from Gmail again, checks the part at
place n against the name the store kept, fetches its bytes with `messages.attachments.get` (a read, covered by the
`gmail.modify` the inbox already holds) and hands them back to be served. Nothing is written: no attachment byte is in the
Durable Object, a sync never fetches one, and Gmail stays the only copy, which is decision 4 applied to files. What makes it
safe to serve a stranger's file from the inbox's own origin: only four kinds are served and the file's first bytes decide
which (never the type or name the sender gave, never SVG or any other); the response carries that exact type,
`Content-Disposition: inline`, `nosniff`, a policy that allows nothing and sandboxes it, `no-store` (a picture someone
sent is mail, and no answer of the Worker's own is kept on the device, as decision 16 says,
so opening it again reads Gmail again), and a size
limit of 5 MB checked before and after the fetch; and the route is a GET behind the same Access check as every read
(the owner and Claude's token), which the public push hostname never reaches. A GET calls nothing that changes a
conversation, so looking at a picture marks nothing read (decision 5). Other attachments stay chips that link to the
message in Gmail; the inbox serves no other file. The earlier rule that "no attachment bytes are served" is
superseded for these four kinds only.

**16. An installable app, and a notification when a person writes.**

*The installable app keeps only itself on the device, and every navigation reaches Access.* The app has a
manifest (fetched with credentials, because Chrome fetches one without the Access cookie otherwise) and a service worker
at `/sw.js`, all served through the Worker's gate like every other file; nothing became public. The service worker keeps
the app's own built files (its content-addressed scripts and styles, the manifest, the icons) in one cache named for the
build, and nothing else: never anything under `/api`, never an answer that carries mail, never a notification's contents
(`src/client/sw-rules.ts`). Every API answer, and every answer of the Worker's own, also says `Cache-Control: no-store`,
so the browser's disk cache keeps no mail either. A navigation (launching the app, a reload, a tap on a notification)
always goes to the network first, so a device whose sign-in has lapsed meets Access's PIN page; a cached page served in
its place would loop between "sign in again" and the cache. Only when the network fails is the kept page shown, and the
app says it is offline. An address that must survive the PIN names the conversation in the query (`/?c=<id>`), because
the part after `#` never reaches Access. **A sign-in lasts a month** (730 hours), declared
in the inbox's Access instance: someone holding the owner's unlocked phone or Mac can use the inbox for up to a month without a
code, as Gmail on the same devices already allows. The Worker still verifies every assertion, its expiry included.

*What leaves the inbox for Google's push service, and what does not.* A notification carries the sender's
name (else address) and the subject, and the ids a tap needs, and nothing else: never the text, the snippet, the other
addresses, a Client or anything Claude wrote. It is encrypted to each device (RFC 8291, aes128gcm, a fresh key pair and
salt per message) and signed with the inbox's VAPID key (RFC 8292, ES256, audience the push service's origin, expiring
within 12 hours, subject an `https:` address of the install's), all written here over WebCrypto and checked against the RFC's
worked example, with no web-push library on the path that holds the key. Google's push service learns that a
notification went to a device, when, and its size; the request has a time to live of an hour, urgency high, and no
Topic (a Topic is readable by the push service). Only Chrome's push service (`fcm.googleapis.com`) is accepted as a
device's address, so the Worker never calls an address a request chose. Web Push is outbound only: no hostname, route,
DNS record or Access application was added. The Worker's log names message and device ids, never a sender or subject.

*The key is a Worker secret, and without it notifications fail closed.* `INBOX_VAPID_KEY` is one P-256 JSON Web Key
holding both halves, pushed by zbc from the install's secrets file beside the Google client secret; the public half the
browser needs is derived from it, so the two cannot disagree. The inbox package's `scripts/vapid-key.ts <env>` makes it on
the operator's machine and writes it through `sops set --value-stdin`, printing only the public half. With no key, or one that is not
a whole P-256 key, every notification route answers 503 and nothing is ever sent; what the sync marks owed meanwhile is
dropped, so a key added later replays nothing. Replacing the key turns notifications off on every device until each is
turned on again, and Settings says so on a device turned on with the old key. Dev uses its own key from the gitignored
`.dev.vars`, and a dev push service that calls nothing.

*Which mail notifies is the inbox's existing rules, and only mail the sync stores is asked.* The rule
(`src/notify/rule.ts`) reuses the queue's Bulk rule and the contacts' automated-sender rule rather than restating them,
so a change to either changes notifications with it, and a Client's people pass both, which is the rule "Clients' mail
always counts". Every check must pass: it came in and is not from the mailbox's own address; it carries Inbox and Unread
as stored; Gmail received it within the hour; its conversation is not Bulk; its sender is not automated. They are asked in
that order, the ones that read only the message first, so the two that read the whole store (Bulk and the sender's
judgment) run only for mail that could notify. The store asks the rule for a message first stored by the history sync (a
push's, or the daily pass's), inside the transaction that stores the page and after that page's label changes, and for
one stored by the thread read before Close or Send when CLAUDE is acting, since nobody is looking then and the sync that
follows would find it already held. Never for the 30-day fill, a resync, the thread read before the owner's own Read, Close
or Send (they are looking at that conversation), a send the inbox filed, a draft or the dev seed (`applyHistoryPage` is told
whether to ask). A message is stored once by its Gmail id, so one email is one decision. The marks live on the message
(migration 7: `notify` owed, sent or dropped, and when), not in a table of their own. Devices are a `device` table; no
route returns a device's keys, Settings is shown each device's push address (so the page can tell which one is the
browser it runs in), and the audit records neither.

*Delivery is one attempt and never holds up mail.* After each alarm run's pages are stored (and at once after Claude's
thread read stores one), the owed notifications still within the hour go to every device, one request each, and are
marked sent; one past the hour is dropped. **More than three at once are one**: when a run has more
than three to hand on, every device gets ONE notification ("5 new messages", "From Ines, Owen and 3 others") whose tap
opens the Inbox, and all of them are marked sent, so none is sent on its own later; up to three arrive one by one. That
also bounds a run to three requests per device, so no cap on requests is needed. A 404 or 410 deletes the device
(audited as gone, by the push service); a 400, 401 or 403 marks it failing, and only then does Settings tell the owner to
turn notifications on again on it, since that is what a key the device no longer matches looks like; a 413, a 429, a 5xx
or no answer marks it failing; none is retried. A request the push service has not answered in 10 seconds is abandoned
as no answer, because delivery runs inside the Mailroom's one-at-a-time chain and must not hold up the next sync.
Delivery never moves the cursor or fails the sync; an error is logged and the run goes on. A run that dies between
sending and marking sends again, and the device shows it once, because the notification's tag is the message's id (a
summary's, its first message's).

*Settings is the owner's only, and Claude has no part in it.* The four routes (the settings, add, remove, test) refuse the
agent, in the route and in the notifications module and store, and Claude's script has no command for any of it, so what
reaches the owner's lock screen is only what the owner's rule decides. The service worker stays quiet only on the device where that very
conversation is open in a visible window (it asks each window, because whether Chrome reports a window's hash route is
not known); the other device, which cannot see that screen, still notifies. Opening a conversation clears its
notifications on that device.

**17. A second kind of mailbox, fed by Cloudflare.** This changes decision 4 for one kind of mailbox: for a
Cloudflare mailbox there is no Gmail, the inbox is where the mail lives, and R2 and the store are the record. Everything else in this
ADR stands for the owner's Gmail mailbox, which behaves exactly as before.

*A mailbox has a kind, and "the mailbox" means the Gmail one.* (**Superseded in part by [ADR 0003](0003-the-inbox-takes-several-gmail-accounts.md): there can be several Gmail rows, and no code means "the first Gmail row" any more; a caller names the account.**) The `mailbox` table accepts `gmail` and `cloudflare`. SQLite cannot alter
the CHECK that said `gmail`, so migration 8 makes the table again. The textbook rebuild (a new table, copy, drop, rename) fails inside a
Durable Object with "FOREIGN KEY constraint failed", found by running it with the checks enforced as a Durable Object runs them: `PRAGMA
foreign_keys = OFF` does nothing inside the transaction every `exec` is, and `PRAGMA defer_foreign_keys = ON` postpones the checks but the
drop still counts a violation for every row that pointed at the old table, which only a row inserted into a table named `mailbox` settles. So
the migration copies the rows aside, drops the table, creates the new one under its own name and puts the rows back, and nothing is left at the
commit. It runs once, in the object's constructor, on the owner's live Gmail row (token, cursor and job included, copied column for column). It was
run against a real workerd Durable Object SQLite over a store holding a mailbox, a conversation and a message (the checks enforced, the
foreign-key check clean afterwards); the deploy itself is one way, since a build that predates it refuses a store at a version it does not know.
Every place that read "the mailbox" as the first row now asks for the Gmail one, and every place that read "has a mailbox" as "is Gmail" asks the
mailbox's kind (`mailboxKindOf`): a Cloudflare conversation takes its read and closed state from the inbox's own stamps, as a manual
conversation does, and never from labels.

*Mail arrives through a handler that keeps the raw copy first and never fails after it.* Cloudflare Email Routing calls the Worker's email handler
(src/cloudflare/entrance.ts). It refuses a message for a domain no mailbox is declared on (the declaration is a var in wrangler.jsonc, one mailbox per
domain, so adding one is a reviewed change deployed with the routing rule), writes the raw message to the `inbox-raw` bucket under the SHA-256 of its
bytes, awaited and not behind `waitUntil`, and only then asks the store to file it, from that copy. Filing reads the copy back and parses it
(src/cloudflare/arrivals.ts), so the first filing and a retry are one path. If filing fails, the copy is recorded as owed (`arrival_owed`, with the
envelope and the time the Worker saw) and the object's alarm files it again, 30 seconds doubling to an hour, up to 12 attempts (about six hours): a sender is never
asked to send twice for the inbox's trouble. A message that still cannot be filed then is given up on, which is not forgotten: it stays counted as unfiled on the
mailbox (amber, with the raw copy kept), nothing retries it by itself, and the same bytes delivered again are filed if they can be. A mailbox's last filing
error is cleared when nothing is owed any more. The handler throws only when the copy cannot be written or the store is unreachable, so not even the debt
could be; what Cloudflare then does with the sender is not documented, and is checked on the real account (`docs/runbooks/inbox-cloudflare-mailbox.md`). It
never calls `setReject` for a failure of its own: a bounce is permanent, and a customer's mail must not bounce because the inbox hiccuped.

*A message is stored once, threaded by three ordered rules, and never moved.* Unique per mailbox by its Message-ID, or by the hash of its bytes when it has
none, so a redelivery stores nothing. Rule 1: In-Reply-To or References naming a message the mailbox holds (the one answered first, then
References newest to oldest, at most 25), else the same subject (Re: and Fwd: stripped, case ignored) with an outside participant in common, where outside
means not the mailbox's own address and not any address on its domain, else a conversation of its own. A reply to something from before the mailbox's first
message starts a conversation (the owner's fresh start), unless an earlier message since then matches by subject and person. No window is applied to rule 2, as
the standalone app applies none; the participant test bounds it.

*The queue reads a Cloudflare conversation by its mailbox and its sort.* (Superseded by decision 22: Bulk for a Cloudflare conversation is the queue's one rule, shared with Gmail's, and the sort is gone.) Bulk is the assistant's sort (spam, promotional, newsletter) and not an envelope, a
conversation nobody has sorted is never Bulk, and a Client's people exempt it as everywhere. Each Cloudflare mailbox has a view of its own (`mailbox:<id>`);
Inbox, Unread, Drafts, Bulk, Closed and the counts cover every mailbox from one list. A store with no Cloudflare mailbox has none of this in any answer.

*A draft on a Cloudflare conversation could not be sent at first.* The send was refused before anything else (409), so a business-mailbox reply could never go out
through Gmail as the owner. Decision 18 replaces that refusal with sending through Cloudflare.

*Consequences.* For the business mailbox the store holds drafts, sorts, chat and the knowledge base that nothing else holds, and R2 holds only the mail: backups of
the store are a precondition, not an open question (`docs/runbooks/inbox-cloudflare-mailbox.md` says what is established and what is not). The assistant, on a key
of its own and only on a mailbox whose row says it runs, is a later decision (19).

**18. Sending as the mailbox's address.** There is still one send path (`Mailroom.sendDraft`), and everything decision 13 and 15 say about the claim, the revision, `through`, the em dash, the
instruction and the switch holds for both mailboxes unchanged. What the mailbox decides is the transport and the sender, and they never cross.

*The mailbox a draft is from is decided when it is made.* A reply is from the mailbox its conversation is in; a new email is from the one the drafter chose (the owner at the space's compose box, Claude through `from` since decision 26) and, when none was chosen, from the mailbox the contact
last wrote to at that moment (a customer hears from the business mailbox, a client from the owner; Gmail when they never wrote to a mailbox), which the contact's card shows as
`sendsFrom`. A business-mailbox draft records its mailbox on the draft itself (`mailbox_id`), the draft card and Claude's `drafts` print it as `sentFrom`, and Send
uses the recorded one (`sendTransportOf`), so a contact who writes to the other mailbox afterwards cannot change whom a draft waiting in the queue goes out as. A
draft that records none is the owner's own, through Gmail. A plan for the other transport is refused (409) in the Mailroom as a belt, and a test holds both directions (Gmail is not
asked about a business-mailbox draft, the binding is not asked about the owner's). The sender of a business-mailbox message is the mailbox row, never anything typed: a draft names
recipients, subject and body and nothing else, so the binding, which Cloudflare lets claim any address on an onboarded domain, can only ever be told the
conversation's mailbox, and the binding is limited to the declared mailboxes' addresses as senders in wrangler.jsonc (`allowed_sender_addresses`, which wrangler's
config schema has; a test pins it to `INBOX_MAILBOXES`; whether Cloudflare applies it to this form of send is a live check). The recipients rule has its own idea of "ours" for a Cloudflare mailbox: its own address and every address on its domain, and not the owner's
Gmail address, which is a person who writes to the business mailbox and is answered as the mailbox's own address (the first version's `replyPlan` and the recipient check counted it as ours, so a reply to
the owner's own test mail was refused; both are now per mailbox).

*Cloudflare composes the message, so the inbox cannot choose its Message-ID.* The first plan was to build the message itself with a Message-ID and Date of its own
and hand it to the binding. The binding's structured form is the one Cloudflare recommends and the one the install's other senders use, and its headers
reference lists `Message-ID` and `Date` as platform-controlled: a request naming either is refused whole (`E_HEADER_NOT_ALLOWED`). The raw form (a pre-built
message) would allow it, is the legacy path, and nothing read shows it accepts any recipient. So the inbox sends the structured form with `In-Reply-To` and
`References` (the only threading headers the allowlist has), and files the sent message under the id the binding reports. A customer's reply that names a
different Message-ID still joins the conversation by threading rule 2 (the subject and the customer as the outside person). Whether the reported id is the header
Cloudflare really wrote is a live check; if it is not, rule 2 does the threading and nothing breaks.

*The order around the call is claim, send, keep the copy, file, and only an acceptance is a send.* The claim records the mailbox on the draft (so a send of unknown
outcome is still known to be a Cloudflare one) and writes no Message-ID (it has none yet). The binding is called once. It THROWS for a refusal: the codes in
Cloudflare's documentation that mean nothing was sent (a payload, a recipient, a sender, a limit) put the draft back byte for byte (the store is identical to before,
a test compares them) and answer 422 for what was typed or 502 otherwise. Every other failure (`E_DELIVERY_FAILED`, `E_INTERNAL_SERVER_ERROR`, a code nobody
documented, no code, a thing that is not an error) is an outcome that may have gone out: the draft stays `sending`, is never sent again by anything, and shows as stuck
with the mailbox it was sent from. A resolved call is an acceptance, with or without an id. Only then is the copy kept and the message filed. The standalone app filed
a reply as sent before calling Cloudflare; this does the reverse, and the order is what makes "a failed send never shows as sent" true.

*The copy is the request, kept under `out/<message id>` in the raw-mail bucket.* With the structured form Cloudflare composes the MIME and the inbox never sees it, so
the copy that can be kept is exactly what was handed over and what Cloudflare answered. It is written after Cloudflare accepts and before the message is filed, and a
bucket that fails then does not undo the send: the message is filed without the copy's key and the audit says `message.copy-failed`.

*The owner's answer about an unknown outcome keeps the message, filed with what it was sent with.* The claim's audit entry records the In-Reply-To and References
the message went out under, and the answer files the message with those (not with whatever the conversation says by then), with the id Cloudflare reported and
the copy's key when `out/<id>` exists (a send Cloudflare accepted and the inbox failed to file), and with neither when no copy was kept. For Gmail "it went out" removes the stuck draft because Gmail's own copy arrives by sync and is the
record. Nothing arrives for a Cloudflare send, so removing it would lose the only record: "it went out" files it as sent, on the owner's word, by whoever claimed it and
with the instruction it went out on (a Claude send then still appears among Claude's sends, once); "it did not" makes it a draft again, with no claim left. Only the owner
can say, and Claude cannot, as before.

*What a business-mailbox message does to the conversation.* The sent message is filed read and closed by its sender (a message with neither stamp would reopen the
conversation it answers), so replying does not close it and Close does, as for Gmail. `through` and "a message arrived after the instruction" read every message
that came in on a Cloudflare conversation, where Gmail's read labels.

*A business-mailbox message notifies the owner's devices.* Filing a message that Cloudflare delivered marks a notification owed unless it is from the mailbox's
own address or domain or arrived more than an hour ago, and hands it on, with the mailbox's name before the sender's, the subject, and nothing of the text. The
assistant's sort (a later decision) is what will keep spam from notifying; until it exists every message notifies, and decision 19 REPLACES this interim rule with the sorted one
(a customer, or the assistant gave up: once, after the sort; Bulk never). Delivery runs one at a time, because the Gmail sync and a business-mailbox arrival can now both
reach it while the Durable Object waits on a push service, and it is asked for AFTER Cloudflare's email handler has answered (`ctx.waitUntil` in the Worker), so a slow
push service never spends the handler's time limit; a notification marked owed and never handed on goes with the next delivery run if it is still within the hour.

**19. The assistant on a business mailbox, without screens.** This reverses the earlier rule of "no assistant inside the inbox", scoped to the business mailbox: for the owner's own mail nothing in the inbox calls a model, and Claude drafts from sessions as before. **Superseded in part by decision 23: the scope is no longer the first business mailbox's alone. The assistant writes a reply when the owner asks, on the owner's own Gmail too; it still does nothing on arrival for either, and Claude still drafts from sessions.** **Decision 22 supersedes the sort, the drafting on arrival, the owed work and its retries, the "could not draft" mark and the notification rule below; what stands is the key, the persona, the em dash rule, what the model is shown, that it never sends, and the usage record of its calls.**

*It runs on a mailbox only when the row says so.* `mailbox.assistant` is 1 for a mailbox the assistant runs on. It starts as the deploy config declares (`assistant: true` on the declared mailbox) and follows the declaration until the owner first uses the switch; from then on the owner's switch decides and a redeploy never turns back on what the owner turned off (each change audited, with its actor). Every path that writes a sort or a draft checks that the conversation's mailbox is a Cloudflare one that says it runs, so a Gmail conversation reaches none of it (a test puts owed work on a Gmail message by hand and finds it refused and dropped). A mailbox with no persona written for it (`src/assistant/prompt.ts`) gets no assistant either: the system prompt names the business, and only the business mailbox's is written, in the standalone app's words.

*The work is owed, not fired and forgotten.* Filing a message in such a mailbox writes `assist_owed` in the filing's own transaction (migration 9), the entrance returns without waiting on a model, and the object's alarm does the pass. A failed call (down, refused, unreadable) counts an attempt and is tried again 30 seconds later, doubling to five minutes, until 50 minutes after Cloudflare's handler received the message (not after it was filed, so a message filed late is not given a fresh window); then the assistant gives up: the conversation stays in the Inbox with no sort (an unsorted conversation is never Bulk, and one an earlier message sorted as junk is unsorted again the moment a new message is filed), is marked as one the assistant could not draft (with the reason), and the owner is notified anyway. The original rule was "up to an hour", with mail notified "within the hour"; the window ends inside the notification's hour so the last notification is not already stale. A message from the mailbox's own address or its domain is not owed a pass at all (nothing sorts or answers our own mail). **When the assistant is switched off** (the owner's switch, or a declaration that says off) every business-mailbox arrival notifies at filing, as the interim rule above had it, so no customer goes unheard; work that was waiting when it was switched off is not dropped but marked as one the assistant could not draft, and notifies. Each alarm run does the owner's Gmail first and then at most four passes (a call can take its whole minute), asking for the alarm again at once for any that are left, so a model that hangs delays the assistant and never the owner's own mail.

*One transaction is what stops a retry from drafting twice.* A pass is applied in one transaction that deletes the owed row, writes the sort, makes or revises the draft and marks the notification, and does nothing at all when the row is already gone, so a failed call (no write), two passes at once and a retry after a success whose commit never happened each leave one draft. A pass over a message is skipped when a newer inbound message of the conversation has its own owed pass (that one reads the whole thread). A follow-up revises the waiting draft only if the assistant last changed it AND it is to the same person; a draft anyone else wrote or edited is left exactly as it is (audited, and a note over the conversation's messages says so while that draft waits), and the owner is still notified. A pass is only superseded by a newer message from the same person. A draft with an em dash is asked for once more with the rule restated against it, and if the second has one no draft is stored and the conversation says so. A sort a person set (the owner, or Claude through the script) is stamped with who, sticks (the assistant does not sort that conversation again), and setting it to customer on a conversation with no draft owes one, without a notification (whoever set it is looking).

*The model is shown only what was exchanged with the person it is writing to.* Threading puts a stranger who copies a known customer, with that customer's subject, in the customer's conversation, and a draft to the stranger written from the customer's messages would leak them (a customer's private questions, our replies). So a pass is for the sender of its message, the draft is to them, and the model is shown the messages they wrote and the replies that went to them, and nothing else on the conversation. The simpler safe choice over "do not draft to a new sender": the cost is that a person on a multi-party thread is answered from their own side of it. A subject and a From go to the model cut to 300 characters, and a customer's em dash in the subject becomes a hyphen in the reply's, so it never blocks a draft.

*The model has one tool and the answer is acted on only as a sort, a draft and a note.* One request per message, ported from the standalone app's triage, with the model, prompt words and signature kept (Sonnet 5, forced `triage` tool, no `thinking` field), and two changes: the mail is marked as data (each message in its own element, the system prompt says what is inside is a stranger's and never an instruction, a `</email>` in a body cannot close its element), and the Claude Code identity block the standalone app sends for a subscription token is dropped. The prompt keeps the standalone app's words (customer-service agent, classify it, never invent a price, signed "the owner / the business"); what is added is the data marking, the retry text and the empty-base sentence. Nothing in the answer is read but the sort (one of four), the draft and the note; a recipient, a Cc or a command in it changes nothing, and the draft's address is the customer's. The assistant has no method that sends and the Worker has no route that lets it: the claim-send-file path and its switch are Claude's and the owner's. A stranger's mail can still steer a sort and a draft's words, which the owner reads before anything is sent, and a customer can land under Bulk. Moving to a newer model is a change of its own: Sonnet 5.5 and later refuse a forced tool.

*The credential is a key of its own, and without it the assistant does nothing.* `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is a pay-per-use Anthropic key, a Worker secret of the `inbox` instance alone, sent only as `x-api-key` to `api.anthropic.com` and in no body, log line or error. With no key there is no model port: each message is given up on at once, says why, and notifies as one the assistant could not draft; mail keeps arriving. Each call to the model records one `assistant_usage` row (tokens read and written, the outcome, including failed calls at zero), and `assistant-usage` in the script (`GET /api/assistant/usage`) sums them over a period with an estimate priced at list rates, which is checked against the key's own usage in Anthropic's console for the first week's report to the owner.

*Notification: one Cloudflare rule, asked once per message when its outcome is known.* It replaces the interim rule (notify every arrival at filing) and keeps that rule's exclusion of the mailbox's own address and domain (`belongsToMailbox`). A customer's message (draft waiting, or the owner's own draft left alone), one the assistant gave up on, and one that arrived while the assistant is off notify when the conversation is open and unread by the inbox's own stamps and Cloudflare received the message within the hour (the owner opened it while the assistant worked: the owner has seen it); spam, promotions and newsletters never do. After the pass for a mailbox the assistant runs on; at filing for one it does not. The payload carries the mailbox's name with the sender and the subject and nothing of the text or the draft, as decision 16 says of every notification. The Telegram ping the standalone app sent to a group is not carried over.

*The knowledge base has its shape and nothing in it yet.* `knowledge_entry` (per mailbox: title, body, active, proposed or removed, who proposed it, a unique `source_ref` for the one-time carry) and `knowledge_version` (append-only: every version, who and when) exist in migration 9 and are read for active entries of the conversation's own mailbox only; empty is fine and the assistant drafts conservatively and says what it could not confirm. The page, the edits, Claude's proposals and the carry are a later decision.

**20. The owner-only restore of the store.** A business mailbox's mail has no Gmail to refill from,
so the store must be restorable before it holds any. The inbox uses the point-in-time recovery of the SQLite-backed Durable Object (the only interface is inside the
object: `getCurrentBookmark`, `getBookmarkForTime`, `onNextSessionRestoreBookmark`, then `ctx.abort()`), in `src/restore.ts`, and adds no migration and no secret.

*A restore is two calls, and only the owner's sign-in passes either.* `POST /api/restore/prepare` names a bookmark or a time (with its zone) and answers a confirmation token
good for five minutes and for that bookmark; nothing changes. `POST /api/restore/confirm` must send the token AND the bookmark back, and arms the restore, keeps the
record and answers the **undo bookmark** (what Cloudflare returns for the moment just before), so a restore can be undone by a restore. The agent's token is refused by
both routes and again by the module (a default-deny on the actor, as decision 15's switch is); it may read `GET /api/restore` (the current bookmark, the bookmark for a
`?time=`, the latest records), which is the script's `bookmark`, so Claude can capture one before a risky deploy. **The report is not owner-only, though the two calls that restore are:** a bookmark restores nothing (it is a name for a moment, and only the two owner-only calls use one),
and without it Claude could not capture a restore point before a deploy. The undo bookmarks in the records are readable by the same token for the same reason. The pending token
lives in the object's key-value storage, which a restore also wipes.

*The record is in R2, not the store.* The audit table is part of what a restore rolls back, so a restore recorded there would erase its own record. Each preparation and
restore is a JSON object under `restores/` in the raw-mail bucket (who, when, target, undo bookmark), written before anything changes: no record, no restore, and a restore
already armed when the write fails is armed back to the moment it was made and the store is restarted at once, because an arming left pending would apply at the next
restart and erase every write between. The bucket was chosen because it is already bound, already outside the store, and written
only by `put` from this Worker.

*The abort is a call of its own.* `ctx.abort()` throws in the call that makes it, so the confirming call answers first and the route then asks for the restart, expecting that
call to fail. Between arming and the restart a write would land in a store about to be replaced and could not be undone, so once a restore is armed the first line of every call
into the object and of its alarm restarts it (`restartIfArmed`), rather than refusing: if the route's restart never comes, the next call of any kind does it, and an armed object is
never left refusing everything while the page's polling keeps it alive.
Whether any of this behaves as Cloudflare's page says is a live check on a scratch object; the tests run over a fake of the storage.

The kinds are a visitor's question, feedback on a page, an organization or professional (advocacy groups, legal aid, government, libraries, journalists, researchers, and an attorney introducing themselves), a vendor pitch, a scam or phishing, and an internal test; a visitor's question is the first kind and the instructions say it holds however the message is written. Its `true` side is only what mass senders send: SEO, link building, guest and sponsored posts, web design and marketing offers, lead generation and lists, partnership mass mail, crypto and investment offers, phishing and fake invoices and alerts. A professional introducing themselves is kept; a marketer selling the site a listing is a pitch. **The threshold is the shared 0.80** (`CLEF_JUNK_THRESHOLD`), not a higher one for this mailbox: nothing here has run Clef on the second business mailbox's mail, a second number with no measurement behind it would be a guess, and a wrongly judged message is kept in Bulk, shows its score and is one move back. **Unmeasured:** the questions are tested at the inbox's seam with a fake model (a worker's message is put to it whole and, judged a person, is in the Inbox and rings; a judged pitch is Bulk and rings nobody), which says nothing about what the real model scores. `scripts/measure-spam-questions.ts` runs the same sample mail through the real Clef; run it and read the first real week's scores before trusting the switch. The second mailbox is still declared without `assistant`: the deploy leaves the check off until the owner presses the switch (the owner's alone, and a redeploy never undoes it), so its mail is in the Inbox and notifies until then, except what the Bulk rules settle without Clef (a List-Unsubscribe header, an automated sender), which goes to Bulk.

*Two Cloudflare mailboxes and the knowledge base.* The knowledge routes without a mailbox named mean "the only Cloudflare mailbox" and refuse (422) once there are two. The page, the Rail and the script all name the mailbox, so nothing of the owner's use changes; the one-time carry of the standalone app's knowledge base is the first business mailbox's alone: it resolves its target by the business mailbox's address, leaving the mailbox out means the business mailbox's, naming any other is refused (422) and writes nothing, and its once-only guard counts only the business mailbox, so a refused or misaimed carry never uses it up. The script's `knowledge` and `propose-knowledge` commands need the mailbox id once there are two.

*Where the zone lives.* A mailbox's mail routing is declared in the repo that owns that zone: in the install's own repo, beside the Worker it routes to and applied after it (the Worker has to know the domain before mail is routed at it, or the entrance refuses the mail); where another repo owns the site on the zone, that repo declares the routing and names the Worker by name. `docs/runbooks/inbox-cloudflare-mailbox.md` has the apply order and the checks.

**25. Files and bounces for the Cloudflare mailboxes.** *Files come back from the kept copy, to the owner only.* A file on mail that came through a Cloudflare mailbox is read out of the message's raw copy (`in/<hash>`), and one the inbox sent out of `out/<id>/<n>`, when it is asked for, by the message and the file's place in its list. Only the owner is given one (on the inbox's own `/api/messages/<id>/attachments/<n>`, with `?download=1` to save, and through the space's `SpaceInbox.attachment`); Claude's token is refused (403); a picture is shown only when its first bytes say PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP, as the type they name, and every other file is a download served as `application/octet-stream` with `nosniff` and a policy that allows nothing. Decision 15's rule that the inbox serves no other file stands for the owner's Gmail, whose other files open in Gmail. *Opening one parses the whole message, so reads take turns and the size is checked first.* The Durable Object is the whole inbox in a 128 MB heap and a parse costs several times the message, so kept-file reads run on a chain of their own (not `exclusive`, so they do not queue behind a sync), and a message whose raw copy is over 10 MB (`MAX_OPEN_MESSAGE_BYTES`) is refused in words before a byte of it is read; its files are listed, named only. *A reply from a Cloudflare mailbox carries up to ten files and 15 MB,* through the binding's `attachments`, each kept as its own object beside the sent copy; from the owner's Gmail it is refused in words and nothing is sent. *Cloudflare's delivery reports are kept one row each (migration 13, `delivery_event`) and shown on the reply they belong to.* Email Sending publishes a report per recipient to a queue the account subscribes to (the `queue` module and the `inbox-delivery` instance; the Worker's `queue()` consumes it); a report is believed only if its sender is a declared mailbox's address, is kept once however often it is delivered, and is matched to its reply at read time by the id the binding returned (a report can arrive before the reply is filed). The one assumption nothing could prove is that that id is the id the reports name; a live check reads it. The migration is one-way.

**26. Claude may choose which of the owner's mailboxes a new email is drafted from.** Until now only the owner could (the space's compose box, `DraftInput.sendFrom`, decision 18 and ADR 0003 decision 7); Claude's drafting route took no choice, so a new email went from the mailbox the contact last wrote to, which was wrong when the owner wanted to write to a family member from a personal account after that person had last written to the work address. Now the draft route takes an optional `from` on a NEW email: a mailbox's address or its name as `mailbox` and `mailboxes` list them (a Gmail account, or a business mailbox as the compose box can name one), or its id. The store reads it as text (`DraftInput.chooseFrom`) and turns it into the same `InboxFrom` the compose box makes (`MailStore.resolveChosenFrom`), so one check and one recording serve both; `sendFrom` itself is still never read from a request body. Refused, with nothing drafted and no contact made (422): a name that is none of ours or is two, a Gmail account that is disconnected or whose grant lapsed (a draft that could not be sent is not made), and any `from` on a reply (a reply always goes from its conversation's mailbox). Absent `from` is the rule of decision 18, unchanged. The chosen account shows on `drafts` as `sendsFrom` (a business mailbox as `sentFrom`, as before).

*What did not change.* It is a draft. Claude's send is decision 15's, untouched: the switch, the owner's recorded instruction, the revision and `through`, and the send uses the mailbox recorded on the draft. A From the owner did not expect is therefore seen on the draft before anything leaves. Claude cannot connect or disconnect an account, and cannot send from a mailbox that cannot send now.

**27. A third kind of mailbox, `telegram`, fed by a bridge through a door of its own.** The inbox gains one mailbox for the owner's one Telegram account, and a conversation per one-to-one chat in it, written only by the bridge (a small program the project keeps outside this folder). Everything else in the inbox is as it was; this records what is new and what is chosen.

*The bridge is a second machine on the space, with a token and a path of its own* (`/agent/telegram`, the Access application `space-telegram-access`, the token "telegram bridge"; the company layer's ADR 0002, amended). The space checks the assertion once and hands the token it verified to the inbox's bridge router in-process (`InboxPart.telegram`, `src/telegram/app.ts`). The router admits the bridge's token by its id (`TELEGRAM_ACCESS_CLIENT_ID`, a var of the space) and nobody else (Claude's token is refused there, and the bridge's on Claude's door), and serves four calls by POST and nothing else: `push`, `events`, `work`, `status` (`src/telegram/wire.ts` is the contract and the parser; it bounds every size and checks every shape, because every string in it is something a stranger wrote). The fifth call, the outcome of a send or a read mark the bridge was owed, arrives with decisions 30 and 31, which are the ones that owe something; `work` returns `owed: []` until then.

*One mailbox row, one account.* Migration 16 (store version 17, **one-way**: bookmark before the deploy; an undo build keeps migrations 12 to 16) makes `mailbox` again for the third kind (the technique and the reasons are migration 8's) and adds, apart from the three bridge columns on the mailbox (`checked_in_at`, `bridge_state`, `bridge_state_at`): `conversation.telegram_chat_id` (unique per mailbox, as a Gmail thread is), `message.telegram_msg_id`, `edited_at` and `deleted_at`, and a small table `telegram_person` (`identity_id`, `bot`, `username`) so the shared `identity` table keeps its shape. The row's `address` is the Telegram user id, its `history_id` holds Telegram's position for the account (pts, qts, date, seq as JSON), written **in the same transaction as the batch it follows** and never moved back by a late repeat; the bridge signed in as another account is refused (409) and stores nothing. The mailbox is made by whichever the bridge says first (a push, a work request, a status).

*A message is stored once.* Its arrival key is `<chat id>:<Telegram's message id>`, on the arrival key that is already unique per mailbox, so a push repeated after a timeout stores one message and says so. Its time is Telegram's; its stamps are the inbox's own, as a Cloudflare mailbox's are: a message from the person arrives unread and open (so a new one **reopens a closed conversation**), and the owner's own message, sent from the owner's phone, is filed outbound, read and closed. In a **mirror** (the first fill, or a read-through after a gap too long to ask for) a new message is stamped read when Telegram has it read (`readInboxMaxId`), and a conversation the push makes comes in **closed when the chat is in Telegram's archive folder** ("open or closed as Telegram has it" has no other meaning in Telegram than the archive; an existing conversation is never closed again by a read-through). Telegram's own folders and read marks are never written to. (Decision 28 changed the last sentence of this paragraph.)

*Edits and deletions.* An edit replaces the text and stamps when; a deletion for everyone removes the text and the snippet and leaves the row (`deleted_at`). A deletion in a chat with a person names ids and no chat, so it is found by Telegram's own id; a deleted message nobody had read is stamped read, so it never holds a conversation unread.

*People.* A Telegram person is an identity of kind `telegram` whose address is the Telegram user id (stable for life; a username is only a name). It is put on the contact whose text-message number matches the phone number Telegram gave, **only when exactly one contact has that number**, audited (`identity.join`, the bridge as actor), and Split undoes it; never by name; never for a bot. Otherwise it is a contact of its own named as Telegram names it (the name the owner saved the contact under first, which is what the server gives), and a later change of Telegram's name does not rename a contact the owner may have renamed. A bot (Telegram says which a user is) is an automated sender by a new rule in `src/automated.ts` (`bot`), which is the existing Bulk rule's own input: its chat is filed in Bulk, out of the Inbox, Unread and Today's Needs reply, and is never a person in the contacts' judgment.

*Telegram's service chat (777000, where login codes arrive) and Saved Messages are never stored,* here as well as at the bridge's edge: a push that holds one is stored without it and counts it as skipped. A chat that is not one-to-one (a group, a channel) was refused by the parser until decision 29.

*What Claude sees.* Telegram's conversations are in `queue`, `conversation` and `search` like mail (a conversation's `kind` is `telegram`, its mailbox is named Telegram and carries `mailboxKind: 'telegram'`), and the script has one new read, `telegram` (`GET /api/telegram`): whether the bridge is talking (live, catching up, signed out, not checked in after five minutes), when it last pushed, and the counts. Reading marks nothing read. A draft on a Telegram conversation is a hand draft (the identity's kind is not an email), which the inbox cannot send; sending through the bridge is decision 31.

*Consequences.* One more thing in the store of live mail that only a bookmark undoes. The inbox now trusts a second machine to say what the owner's Telegram holds: a compromised bridge token could push false messages into the Inbox (it cannot read anything out, send, or reach Claude's routes; **decision 34 changed the first: it can read the picture of a send while that send is offered, and nothing else**), and a compromised login on the bridge's side is the weightier risk.

**Routes inside the site's Worker.** One deploy, direct access to the Clients table. Rejected for the
key and the public entrance sharing a deploy with the live site, and for the Durable Object and
trigger the site does not have.

**Extending the earlier outbox tool.** Rejected: the Clients it needs are in the site, and an app that receives mail is a different size of thing from a page of drafts.

**Buying Missive or Front.** Neither groups by the company's Clients, and Claude's drafting and sending
go through our own API. Their prices were not checked.

The inbox deploys on its own from the operator's machine through zbc, and its first apply creates

no staging in front of it, when the Client directory arrives. Two copies of the Access verifier and
two copies of the Gmail parsing and MIME code (with the earlier outbox scripts) exist until something makes

## Addendum: the inbox has no address for people

Decisions 1 to 6 above describe an inbox that had a page at a hostname of its own behind its own Access application. It no longer does. The inbox is still its own Worker with its own store, and nothing about the store, the Durable Object, the bucket or Gmail's grant changed; what went is the address and the page. Its doors are now three, and none is an address for people: the **space's service binding** (the `SpaceInbox` entrypoint, which the owner's work goes through), **Claude's binding** (the `ClaudeInbox` entrypoint, reached through the Access application on the space's agent path), and **Gmail's push** at a push hostname of its own, the Worker's one custom domain, with no Access application by design. Cloudflare's mail arrives through Email Routing as before. The Access application of the old hostname was deleted and the hostname removed in one sitting; that deletion is one-way. Where this ADR says "the app", "the page" or "the inbox's address", read the space.

**28. Telegram's one-to-one chats on screen, and notifications by the mail rule. No migration.** A message stored by a push the bridge marks `live` (its live drain: not a first fill, a read-through or a catch-up) is put once to a Telegram branch of the notification rule (`telegramNotifyDecision`, `src/notify/rule.ts`), after the push is filed and its conversation touched, and marked `notify = 'owed'` when it passes. The branch is the Cloudflare branch's shape (a person's message, the conversation open and unread as the inbox's own stamps have it, within the hour, never Bulk, never the owner's own) with three checks first: the push is live, the message is not the owner's own, the chat is not muted. **A fill, a read-through and the start-up catch-up never ring, whatever the age of what they bring** (a gap of under an hour would otherwise ring a burst); the catch-up the live drain starts mid-session (a reconnect, a pts jump) is marked live, since what it brings happened seconds ago, and the hour limit and the collapse of more than three into one bound a burst. The seam is the bridge's `live` mark and not the hour alone. **`muted` is stored nowhere**: the push carries Telegram's answer with each message and the rule reads it once at arrival, so no column was needed (the bridge reads the chat list again when a live message arrives and its copy is a minute old, so a mute is at most a minute stale). The Durable Object hands the owed marks on right after a `pushTelegram` call (`InboxDO.call`, in `ctx.waitUntil` and not awaited, so a slow push service cannot hold the bridge's reply); a failure to deliver is logged and never refuses the push (`test/telegram-do.test.ts` drives the real class). The notification names the sender and says "New Telegram message" (a Telegram message has no subject, and its words are never in a notification), and its `person` is the key the space opens the contact by: the contact's first email address, else `mail:<contact id>`. A group's rule (decision 29, rule l) is one more check in the same function.

*A Telegram conversation is not answerable from the space until decision 31.* The space's `canReply` is false for one (`SpaceStore.shape`), so no reply box is offered and a draft on it is a hand draft (Copy words, Mark sent), as a text's is; Claude's drafts and `send` through its script behave as decision 27 made them. A Telegram message is drawn whole (the mail quote-folding is not applied: `read.quoted` is empty), an edited one says so (`edited`) and a deleted one is a line (`deleted`). The menu gains a Telegram entry in the Mailboxes tree (a filter like a business mailbox's, from `navigation.telegram`) and a foot line, and Settings a card (`mailboxes.telegram`); both say the bridge's state (`live`, `catching-up`, `signed-out`, `not-checked-in` after five quiet minutes) in the same sentence, worded by the page (`packages/space/src/client/telegram.ts`) so the time is in the person's zone. **A chat the owner archived in Telegram comes in Closed**.

**29. Telegram groups and channels. Migration 17 (store version 18), one-way.** This records two rules: every member who writes in a group is a person and the group stays one row under its own name (k), and a group rings, needs a reply and reopens only for a message that mentions the owner or replies to the owner (l). The door's four calls are unchanged; the wire widened (`src/telegram/wire.ts`).

*A chat's id is one spelling for three sequences.* Telegram keeps users, basic groups and channels (supergroups included) as separate id sequences, so a number can be in more than one. The wire spells a person's chat by their user id, a basic group's by `-` and its id, and a supergroup's or channel's by `-100` and its id (the Bot API's way), which is also the conversation's `telegram_chat_id` and the arrival key's chat part (`-1001234567:42`). The parser refuses a person's chat with a `-` id, a group with a person's id, and a `sender` anywhere but in a group.

*One-way migration 17* adds four nullable columns and nothing else (no table made again, no row rewritten; a store at version 17 reads exactly as before): `conversation.telegram_kind` (`user`, `bot`, `group`, `channel`; NULL for the one-to-one conversations of decision 27), `conversation.telegram_pts` (a supergroup's or channel's own position), `telegram_person.chat` (`group` or `channel` on the identity that stands for one) and `message.telegram_to_me`. A build before it refuses a store at version 18: **bookmark before the deploy, and an undo build keeps migrations 12 to 17** (`scripts/inbox.ts bookmark`). It is the one second migration this needed, and nothing more is needed.

*The group is one contact that stands for it.* A group or channel is a contact named by its title with one identity of kind `telegram` whose address is the chat's marked id and whose `telegram_person.chat` says which it is; it is the conversation's ONLY participant, so the inbox's rule that a thread is one row under whoever last wrote (placement) leaves the group's row under the group whoever speaks. **There is no identity kind of its own for a group and for a channel:** the kind stays `telegram` and the `chat` column says which, because a second and third kind would have widened `MailKind` through the company layer's contract and every screen that tests for Telegram (the hand-draft rule, the heading, the quote folding, the pill), for no behaviour the column does not already give. A title Telegram changes later does not rename a contact the owner may have renamed (a person's name is held the same way). A sender the bridge knows only by id (`bot` absent on the wire) is made a person when new and otherwise changes nothing about the person held, so an undescribed message can never turn a bot into a person, is headed by the name the inbox holds of them (never the number), and a contact still named only by the id takes the name when a later push describes them (its earlier messages' headers with it; a name the owner set is never replaced); a basic group read back 30 days is marked by a `setting` row (`telegram-back-read:<chat>`, written with the last page, answered by `work` with `newest`), which needs no migration; and the one spelling of a chat id (`-100` for a supergroup or channel) is `src/telegram/wire.ts`'s, read by the bridge, the parser and the store's SQL.

*Every member who writes is a person.* A group message is filed under its SENDER's identity (`message.identity_id`), the Telegram user id, so a member who also writes to the owner one-to-one is the contact they already are, the phone-number join applies as for any person, and a bot that speaks is a bot. The sender is not a participant. **A member's own history is what passed between the two of them:** it is read through the conversations they are a participant of, so a group's messages are never in it (`test/telegram-groups.test.ts`, "a person's own history holds only what passed between the two of them"). The reads that find a message by its identity rather than by the conversation's people were listed and checked: the notification read (`NOTIFY_SELECT`, which names the group's contact for the tap and the title "Ana in Founders"), the placement times (keyed by contact, only the participant's are used), the spam gate and `mailboxContactLastWroteTo` (Cloudflare and Gmail only), the draft count (drafts only), the manual-history split (conversations with no mailbox), `removeUnusedContact` (a member with group messages is never removed as unused), and the fold's sender judgment. **The groups a person writes in** are one new read, `groupsWritten` (`src/store/space.ts`), exposed as `groups` on the contact the space reads (name, the group's contact and when they last wrote there). People leaves the group's and channel's own contacts out (`spaceContacts`), and does not count them among the automated senders.

*Needs reply, ringing and reopening (rule l).* At arrival, for a group message from somebody else, `telegram_to_me` is 1 when the bridge says Telegram marks it as mentioning the owner (`mentionsMe`) or it names a message (`replyTo`) that is one of the owner's own stored messages in that chat (`<chat>:<id>` with direction `out`), and 0 otherwise; a reply to a message older than the first fill is therefore known only by Telegram's own flag. **Ringing:** `telegramNotifyDecision` takes `group` and `toMe`, and a group message that is not addressed to the owner never rings (after the mute, before Bulk); the live, own-message, mute, hour and collapse rules are unchanged. **Reopening:** a message addressed to the owner is filed as the other kinds are (unread, not closed), so it reopens a closed group; a message not addressed to the owner in a group that is CLOSED is filed closed (`closed_at` set at arrival), so it does not; in an open group it is an ordinary unread message, so a group "sits in the Inbox as an open row with its unread count". **Needs reply** is a new fact on the space's line, `needsReply`, true for a group when an addressed message (one its sender has not deleted) came in after the owner's last message there (`SpaceStore.shape`); the space's Today uses it in place of the direction rule for a group and leaves a group that does not need a reply out of Today altogether (its unread chatter is not Today's business). A group's row says who wrote the newest message before its words ("Tomas: ..."), made by the store (`groupSnippet`) so the list, the search and the person's page read it alike.

*Channels are Bulk by the one Bulk rule.* **Not as a new clause in `bulkReason` but through the rule that already feeds it:** the channel's contact is judged an automated sender by a new rule in `src/automated.ts` (`channel`, beside `bot`), and a channel's posts are filed under that contact, so the newest inbound message's sender is automated and `bulkReason` answers `automated`, which is why the Inbox, Unread and Today's Needs reply never hold one and why the conversation says "Automated sender: A Telegram channel: broadcasts, filed in Bulk." A Telegram GROUP is never judged by who last spoke in it (`stateAndEnvelope` and `queueFacts` pass no judgments for one), so a bot that talks in a group does not move the group to Bulk. A channel has no reply box (`canReply` is false for all Telegram until decision 31, and the space says "A Telegram channel is one you follow: there is nothing to reply to."); a channel the owner runs is read-only here too.

*A position of its own for each supergroup and channel.* The account's position (pts, qts, date, seq) covers a person's chats and basic groups; a supergroup and a channel have a pts of their own. A push or an events call carries `chatPositions` (chat id to pts), stored in the same transaction as the batch it follows on the chat's conversation, never moved back, and a position for a chat with no conversation yet is not kept (the bridge then reads that chat again: "no position is stored, the bridge reads instead"); `work` answers all of them (`chatPositions`), which is what each one's catch-up starts from. A deletion with no chat names the account's own id sequence, so it is found only among a person's chats and basic groups (`telegram_chat_id NOT LIKE '-100%'`): a supergroup's or channel's ids are their own and a deletion there names the chat.

*What did not change.* The service chat and Saved Messages are refused in the parser's callee as before; the bridge's door, its token and its four calls (the fifth arrives with decision 30); Close and Reopen; read marks (decision 30); sending (decision 31; a group's reply box waits for it). *Consequences.* One more thing in the store of live mail that only a bookmark undoes. People grows by the people who write in the owner's groups.

**30. Read marks both ways, "like on your phone". No migration.** Reading a chat on the owner's phone marks it read in the space; opening a conversation in the space marks it read in Telegram, so the other person sees it read where Telegram shows that. The door gains its fifth call, `outcomes`, which decision 27 held back ("the outcome of a send or a read mark the bridge was owed"); `work`'s `owed` stops being empty and `events` carries read marks.

*From Telegram.* `events` takes `reads` (chat id, the newest message the owner read, when): every message of the other side in that chat at or below the mark is stamped read, `read_by` `telegram`, and the conversation is touched for the live pages. It only ever reads (a mark below what is read changes nothing, nothing goes unread), a chat the space does not hold is counted as `unknown`, and **nothing is owed back to Telegram for it** (it would be an echo). The bridge hears them as `UpdateReadHistoryInbox` (a person's or a basic group's chat) and `UpdateReadChannelInbox` (a supergroup's or channel's), live and inside a difference's `otherUpdates`; the outbox forms (the other side reading the owner's messages) stay position-only. A read update keeps its place in the position line exactly as the position-only update it replaces did; one the bridge cannot read is a hole (a catch-up), never a position, and a mark the bridge itself makes puts the position Telegram answers it with into that line, so it does not look like a jump.

*To Telegram.* The one thing that owes Telegram a mark is **The owner's read** through `applyConversationChange` (the space's mark-read, the call the owner's opening of a conversation makes, which already refuses a read of a conversation that has grown past what the page showed, `planStampChange`). Before the stamps are written, the store takes the newest Telegram message id among the messages that read is about to stamp (the unread ones, inbound, with a Telegram id), and owes the chat a mark up to exactly that: **never the newest message the chat has, only what the owner was shown**, because an unread message stored beyond what the page showed refuses the read first. A conversation with nothing unread owes nothing. The debt is a `setting` row, `telegram-read-owed:<chat id>`, whose value is that message id (a later read of the same chat only raises it; no table, no column, so no migration). Claude never owes one: its script has no read (decision 5, unchanged), and the store owes a mark only for the owner's actor, so a read reaching the store by any other actor owes nothing. The bridge's own news of a read, a first fill and a read-through never owe either.

*What the bridge is offered and what it reports.* `work` answers up to 50 owed marks (`{ kind: 'read', chatId, upTo }`, oldest first) with every request, until an outcome settles it. `outcomes` (`{ kind: 'read', chatId, upTo, ok: true }` or `ok: false` with a short Telegram code) deletes the row if it still holds that `upTo` (a mark raised meanwhile stays for the newer one) and counts an outcome that names nothing; a mark Telegram refused for good is audited (`telegram.read-mark-failed`, actor `telegram`, the reason) and not offered again, and the conversation stays read in the space. The bridge makes the mark by the call the chat takes, from the dialog list's own description of it: `messages.ReadHistory` for a person or a basic group, `channels.ReadHistory` for a supergroup or a channel, with `maxId` exactly the owed number. It never marks Telegram's service chat or Saved Messages; it reports an answer Telegram will not change (a closed or unknown chat) as failed, tries a passing trouble (a flood wait, a dropped connection) on its next requests and gives up after five, and a space that does not take the outcome only means the mark is offered, and made, again (a read mark twice is a read mark once). These two calls are the only writes the long-running bridge makes to Telegram, and the boundary test pins them.

*What this cannot promise.* Telegram's read mark is a high-water mark: every incoming message in the chat at or below `maxId` becomes read, including one the space does not hold (older than the first fill, or one the bridge is still catching up on and has not stored). The space can vouch only for what it holds, so a message below the newest one the owner saw that the space has not stored yet is marked read on the phone with the rest, as reading past it on the phone would. A group's or channel's unread counts that the phone keeps by mention are Telegram's own.

*What did not change.* Close and Reopen are the space's own; Telegram's folders and archive are still never written; a draft on a Telegram conversation is still a hand draft (sending is decision 31).

**31. Sending on Telegram, "like on your phone". No migration.** The owner pressing Send in the space sends on Telegram as the owner, in a one-to-one chat or a group, through the bridge. A channel has no reply box. Claude's send is the existing instructed send, on the same path, and nothing else can make the bridge write: the bridge's door still has five calls, and none of them is a way for anyone to send.

*It is the one send path.* Telegram is a third transport beside Gmail's and Cloudflare's: `Mailroom.sendDraft` asks the store which way a draft goes (`sendTransportOf`, now `gmail`, `cloudflare` or `telegram`, decided by the conversation's mailbox), and a Telegram draft runs the same steps up to the call: `planSend` (the draft is an outbound draft at the revision named, a body and no em dash, a reply is refused when something arrived after what the page showed (`through`), and for Claude the switch is on, the owner's instruction is recorded, nothing arrived after it and nobody but Claude changed the draft after it), then `claimSend` (one conditional write, draft to `sending`, the instruction written with it, so a send whose outcome is never known still carries the words it went out on). What differs is that there is no call to make: the claim also **owes the bridge the message**, and `sendDraft` comes back with the draft as claimed, `sending`. The space answers `InboxSent.status: 'sending'`, which for a Telegram chat is the ordinary answer (the page says "Sending on Telegram. It shows in the conversation when Telegram has it."), and the conversation changes by itself when the outcome arrives. The compose key is the space's own and unchanged: a repeat of the press answers "already on its way".

*Telegram's repeat key.* The claim makes a random 62-bit number once and stores it with the send; every offer of that send to the bridge carries the same one, and the bridge sends it as `random_id`. Telegram stores a message sent twice under one random id once (a probe of the real service: the same id twice gave one message both times), which is the only reason the bridge may be offered the same send again. The owed send is a `setting` row, `telegram-send:<message id>` (JSON: the random id, the chat, who pressed Send, when it was claimed, when it was first offered), the way decision 30's read marks are, so no table and no column: **no migration**. A refusal's words live in `telegram-send-failed:<message id>`.

*Outcome unknown, and what is never retried blindly.* The first offer of a send stamps `offeredAt`. The send is offered again by every work request, with the same random id, for **five minutes** from that first offer (`SEND_RETRY_FOR_MS`, `src/telegram/wire.ts`) and then never: how long Telegram remembers a key is not known (a probe proved only an immediate repeat), so past five minutes a repeat could double. **A send can still land after its last offer**, and the inbox does not tell the owner it is done with until it cannot: the last offer may start a request that Telegram does not answer, GramJS holds such a request pending and sends it again after a reconnect, and a "did not go out" followed by a new send of the same words would then be two messages. So (a) **a send with no answer ends the bridge process** (`SendUnanswered`: no answer in `SEND_TIMEOUT_MS` (30 s), a dropped connection, or an answer the bridge cannot read; what it had done before it in the same pass is still reported), which drops GramJS's queue, and the supervisor restarts it; (b) the inbox treats a send as **in flight for `SEND_IN_FLIGHT_MS`** from its first offer (the five minutes, plus the send timeout, plus the bridge's silence restart (`BRIDGE_SILENCE_DEFAULT_MS`, five minutes), plus a minute's margin: 11.5 minutes in all), and neither answer is the owner's to give before then (409, "the bridge may still be sending this"); (c) an outcome carries the `randomId` it was offered under, and **`outcomes` settles only the claim that holds that very key**, so a late answer for a send the owner took back and sent again (a new claim, a new random id) settles nothing (`unknown`) and cannot undo the new claim.

**A send the bridge was never offered within five minutes of its claim is not left to go out whenever the bridge comes back, possibly an hour later:** it is returned to a draft with the plain note "Not sent: the Telegram bridge was not running. Press Send again." (`expireUnofferedSends`, run before every read of drafts and every work request, audited `the bridge was not running`), its owed row goes, and nothing is asked of the owner (only the bridge sends, and it was never offered this, so nothing can have gone out). It shows as sending for those five minutes. A send offered and not reported is **outcome unknown** once it is past `SEND_IN_FLIGHT_MS`: it stays `sending`, is counted in the Telegram status (`unknownSends`), is never offered again and only the owner settles it (`resolveSend`: "it went out" removes the stuck draft; "it did not" makes it a draft again). The person is shown **"Sending on Telegram" (`InboxDraft.sending`, `stuck` false, no buttons) while the send may still land, and the "check Telegram" card (`stuck`) only after**; a send never offered shows as sending for five minutes from its claim and then as the card, which lets the owner take it back. **The chat settles most of them without the owner:** the owner's own message with exactly these words, in the same chat, stamped no more than two minutes (`SEND_CLOCK_SKEW_MS`) before the claim, arriving from the bridge's stream IS the send, but only for **a send the bridge was actually offered and only within `SEND_IN_FLIGHT_MS`** (`adoptStreamCopy`): it is settled as sent when it is filed, whether the bridge's outcome comes next or never. A send never offered is not settled by a phone message (nothing has tried to send it), and neither is anything past the bound: the copy is then for the owner to see, and the owner settles only when there is no copy and the bound is past. ("It did not go out" is still refused when such a copy is held, with the same skew.) A late outcome for a send still waiting (the bridge did send it after all) files it, because it names the claim that is waiting.

*What the bridge reports.* `outcomes` takes `{ kind: 'send', messageId, randomId, ok: true, telegramId, at }` or `{ kind: 'send', messageId, randomId, ok: false, reason }`. Taken, the draft becomes the sent message: arrival key `<chat>:<Telegram's id>`, direction out, filed as the owner's (or Claude's, with the instruction), read and closed as every message the owner sends from anywhere, in the conversation it answered. **The bridge hears its own message as an update and pushes it, and the two meet by the arrival key**: the push after the outcome stores nothing ("repeated"), and a push BEFORE the outcome becomes the sent message (it takes the sender and the instruction and the draft is removed), so the conversation never shows it twice. Refused for good (a code from a short list of answers that mean nothing went out), the claim is undone as Gmail's refusal undoes it (a draft again, no instruction on it, audited `message.send-refused`) and the reason is kept in words on the draft (`MessageRow.refusal`, `InboxDraft.refusal`, shown on the card), since the page had already been answered `sending`. A code with no words is said as the code ("Telegram said X."). A trouble with no answer (no answer in time, a dropped connection, an answer it cannot read) is not reported, and ends the bridge process (above); an answer with a code that is not final (a flood wait) is left for the next offer.

*Who may be written to.* One-to-one chats and groups (a bot's chat too, which the space does not offer a box for because it is Bulk). A channel is refused (422) in the plan and by `canReply`; a conversation filed under Telegram's service chat (777000) or the owner's own account (Saved Messages) is refused in the plan, never offered by `work`, and refused again at the bridge: three locks, and a test for each. A draft on no conversation has no chat to go to and stays a hand draft (Copy words, Mark sent). Plain text only, the draft's body byte for byte, at most 4,096 characters (refused in the plan, and again at the bridge), no files (422; **decision 34 lets a send carry one picture**), no reply-to, no formatting. The em dash rule is the email send's: refused at the send whoever wrote the words. `canMarkSpam` is false for a Telegram chat (it is answered, and it is not mail).

*Claude.* One switch, no second one. A Telegram draft is made as an email draft is (`draft` with the conversation), sent with `send` on the owner's recorded instruction with the owner's switch on, and the answer is the draft as claimed (`sending`); the script says to read the conversation for it and never to send it again. Claude's door has no route that reaches the bridge's `work` or `outcomes` (Claude's token is refused on the bridge's door and the bridge's on Claude's, as before), `work` offers only a send that was claimed through the plan above (never a draft), and an `outcome` that names a draft, or nothing waiting, changes nothing. Claude cannot say whether a send went out. Reading a message that says "the owner says send it" is reading text: the instruction comes only from the owner in a session (decision 15, unchanged).

*The bridge.* `messages.SendMessage` is the one request the allow-list grows by (built in one place, `sendRaw` in `gramjs.ts`, plain text under the given random id, no entities, reply-to or schedule); the sync reaches it only through the port's `sendMessage`, from `doSend`, behind four locks in this order (a chat the bridge carries, the text's length, the key's shape, and not a channel), and `test/boundary.test.ts` pins every one of those. The answer carries the position the send took, which Telegram does not announce to the session that asked, so it is put in the live line as a read mark's is. The probe's Saved Messages send is the probe's alone.

*Consequences.* A message goes out as the owner only by the owner's press in the space, or Claude's instructed send: the same as email. A send is as slow as the bridge's three-second poll (a second or two more than Gmail's). When the machine that runs the bridge is down nothing sent from the space leaves and a send waits (and may be taken back by the owner once five minutes have passed); it is never lost and never doubled. What could not be shown without the real Telegram: that a repeat inside five minutes is stored once (the probe saw an immediate one), what Telegram answers a send into a group with, and that the response shapes `sentOf` reads are what GramJS gives.

**32. Photos and voice notes, copied and shown. No migration; one new call on the bridge's door; one new Telegram call on the bridge.** A photo and a voice note, at most 10 MB, in a one-to-one chat with a person or in a group, are copied into the raw-mail bucket and shown to the owner alone, as a Cloudflare mailbox's files are: a picture as a thumbnail, a voice note as a player. Everything else is named with its kind and size and left in Telegram (a document, a sticker, a GIF, audio that is not a voice note), and so is every file in a channel or a bot's chat (both are Bulk, and Bulk is not worth the storage) and every file over the limit, which says so in one plain line ("Too large to copy. Open it in Telegram."). Downloads are inbound only: nothing is ever sent to Telegram as a file (**changed by decision 34: one picture is, for the picture send alone**). **Changed, see the follow-up at the end of this decision: a picture sent as a file and a video are copied too (a video as a player), and the first version's mapping bug that copied nothing is mended.**

*The message first, the file after.* A push stores the message with its words and its file named (`message.attachments`, one entry, a `tg` object on it: kind, length, and whether the bytes are `kept`, `owed` or `left` and why) in the same transaction, and for a photo or voice note within the limit writes one `setting` row `telegram-media-owed:<message id>`. The work request offers the bridge up to ten of those, newest first (`{ kind: 'media', chatId, id, mediaKind }`); the bridge downloads each (a read: the message is read again, the file fetched only if it is a photo or a voice note, never past 10 MB) and uploads the bytes on the door's sixth call, `POST /api/media?account&chat&id` (a raw body, read with a ceiling, 413 past the limit). A file the bridge cannot get is reported as a `media` outcome (`too-large`, `gone`, `failed`) so the space stops asking and says why under the message; a trouble that may pass is retried by the next offer and given up after five tries. Nothing a failed download does touches the message, and a file still owed after a week is given up on (`failed`), so "Copying from Telegram." never stays for ever. The store decides what is copied (`planTelegramFile`); the bridge only does what it is owed, so the rule has one place. No new table or column: the file's facts are in the `attachments` JSON the message already had, and its bytes are named by `raw_key` (`tg/<message id>`, a key of its own, so nothing that reads `in/` or `out/` ever meets one).

*The bytes are a stranger's.* The upload is accepted only for a message the space is waiting on (a bridge token cannot fill the bucket, and cannot overwrite a kept file), is written to the bucket first and the store told after (an object the store no longer wants, a message deleted meanwhile, is deleted again), and is served only to the owner (Claude's token is refused: the agent is never handed a file, and its script lists none), one at a time on the chain the Cloudflare files use, after its first bytes say what it is: a picture of the four kinds (shown if within 5 MB, else saved) or, for a voice note, an Ogg file (`OggS`), as the type THEY name; anything else is a download, `application/octet-stream`. The space serves a sound inline (`audio/ogg`, the one sound it plays) with the inert headers of a picture, and answers a `Range` request for it with 206, which Safari needs to play audio at all. A message deleted for everyone (see "Edits and deletions", decision 27) takes its file with it: the entry and `raw_key` are cleared in the same transaction, `telegram-media-drop:<key>` is written, and the Durable Object deletes the object after the call (`TelegramMediaKeeper.sweepTelegramMedia`); a delete that fails leaves the row for the next sweep. `RawBucket` gains `delete`, used only for these.

*The door.* It now has six calls. The sixth is the only one whose body is not JSON, the only one the bridge's allow-list on the space's side had to learn, and the only one that writes to the bucket; the bridge's token is still the only one admitted. The space's Access path, Worker and wrangler files are unchanged (the door forwards any `/api/` path), so there is **no new binding, no new secret and no migration**. **Deploy order: apply `space` first, then re-apply `bridge-app`**; either order loses no message. The work request carries a flag, `media: true`, that only a bridge that takes files sends, and `media` items are offered only when it is there: a bridge from before this decision treats every item in `owed` that is not a send as a read mark, so it must never be offered one. That also makes a downgrade of the bridge safe while files are owed (the rows wait for a bridge that asks). An old bridge on the new door never sends a `media` description, so nothing is copied until the new bridge arrives (and the photos pushed in that window are named by their words only). A new bridge on an old door sends a `media` description the old parser drops (it keeps only the fields it knows), so a photo pushed in that window is stored as its bracketed words with no file and is never copied: that is why the space goes first.

*The bridge.* One client method joins the allow-list: `downloadMedia`, called in one place (`fetchMedia` in `gramjs.ts`), after the kind check (photo or voice only) and the size check, and `test/boundary.test.ts` pins that, that no request of the `upload` namespace or anything that sends media is built, and that only the sync reaches it, behind the carried-chat lock (never Telegram's service chat or Saved Messages) and the channel lock. It runs apart from the heartbeat (`mediaLoop`, one file at a time, two minutes each), so a slow download never makes the bridge look silent; a login that ended while fetching ends the bridge as everywhere. Nothing is written to the guest's disk: the file is held in memory and handed on.

*Cost.* Nothing new beyond R2: a photo is a few hundred kilobytes and a voice note under a megabyte, so a month of the owner's chats is tens of megabytes (the 30-day fill of 289 messages held perhaps a dozen files), which is a fraction of a cent a month at R2's storage price, one Class A write per file (the free tier's million a month is not approached) and no egress to the space's Worker. The work request every three seconds is unchanged, and a file is one more request. The Durable Object holds a file in memory only while it is being kept or read (at most 10 MB, one read at a time).

*Consequences.* A voice note plays in the browsers that decode Opus in Ogg (Chrome does, and the e2e proves it); whether Safari on the owner's iPhone does is a hand test, and the page says where to save it when a browser cannot (a Save link beside the player, and a line when playing fails). If it does not, the bridge's guest would convert voice notes (ffmpeg) to a format Safari plays, which is a later change. A photo from Telegram is a JPEG of at most about 1280 pixels, so the 5 MB preview limit is never the reason it is not shown. Self-destructing media is not copied (it is only its bracketed words). Only files that arrive after the deploy are copied: a photo already stored before it has no description of its file (the first fill stored its words only), and the store already holds a position, so nothing reads those chats again. A one-time backfill of the last 30 days was built later (below).

*Follow-up: the bug that copied nothing, pictures sent as files, and videos. No migration; no new Telegram call; no new call on the door.*

- **The bug.** The first version copied no file at all. The bridge read `media.ttlSeconds !== undefined` to leave out self-destructing media, and GramJS gives `null`, never `undefined`, for an optional field a message does not carry, so every file was read as self-destructing and every message went out as its bracketed words alone. The tests used hand-written objects with the field missing, a shape the library never makes; `test/gramjs-shape.test.ts` now builds each message with the library, writes it to the bytes Telegram sends and reads it back (the round trip fails on the old line). The same unset-is-null mistake was in four `accessHash` checks and is mended with them (`isPresent`, `src/mapping.ts`).
- **What is copied now.** A photo, a voice note, an **image** (a picture sent as a file, a document of type png, jpeg, webp or heic; a gif sent as a file is a file) and a **video**, each at most 10 MB (a video 50 MB since the second follow-up below), in a one-to-one chat or a group. An image is a thumbnail (a heic one is saved, not drawn: no browser but Safari draws it); a picture of up to the 10 MB copy limit is shown, because it is one object read alone from the bucket and not mail parsed out of Gmail's text (mail's previews stay at 5 MB). A video is an inline player (`how: 'view'`, served as `video/mp4`, `video/quicktime` or `video/webm` by its first bytes, in ranges so Safari plays it, with the inert headers of a picture); it loads nothing until played. A video over its limit keeps the plain line ("Too large to copy. Open it in Telegram."). Telegram's own thumbnail of a large video is NOT copied: it would be a second object per message (`raw_key` names one), a second upload and a second thing to delete, for a picture of a file that is not here. The contract gains `TelegramMediaKind` `image` (and the owed `mediaKind` is any of the four copied kinds), `InboxAttachment.video` (additive), and the names `Image.png` and `Video.mp4` take their extension from a table of ours keyed by the declared type.
- **Repairing what was stored without a file.** A message already held that is pushed again WITH a file, and has none recorded, is given the file's entry and owed the bytes (`adoptTelegramFile`; a message deleted for everyone or already naming a file is left alone). The space asks the bridge to push them: `work` answers `mediaRead`, up to five chats a time that have a conversation and no marker `telegram-media-read:<chat id>` (a `setting` row, no migration), only to a bridge that says `media: true`; the bridge reads the last 30 days of each person's chat or group, pushes the messages that carry a file as a mirror (which changes nothing of a held message but its file's entry) with `mediaRead` naming the chat, and the marker is stored with them, so a chat is read once. A bot's chat, a channel and a chat Telegram no longer lists are only marked. A chat that cannot be read is tried five times and then marked done by the bridge (a push of no messages), so it gives its slot to the next one and its earlier files are not read again. The reads run apart from the heartbeat, with the downloads. This is the backfill decision 32 owed, and it also brings the photos and files of the last 30 days that were stored before copying existed.
- **Deploy order is strict: apply `space` FIRST, then re-apply `bridge-app` and `bridge-service`.** The parser of a space from before this change refuses a push carrying the kind `image` whole (a refusal ends the bridge, which the supervisor restarts into the same refusal). The space first is harmless to the bridge that is running (it sends no file, so nothing is owed, and it ignores `mediaRead`).
- **A bracketed word is never broken across lines in the page** ("[Video]" had come out as "[Vide" and "o]" in the bubble of a message of the owner's own with a short time over it): the bubble holds the words of any message with no text unbroken (`.placeholder`).

*Second follow-up: videos up to 50 MB, played inline.* A 19.6 MB screen recording was "Too large to copy", so the video limit went from 10 MB to 50 MB (50 x 1024 x 1024 bytes). No migration, no new Telegram call, no new call on the door.

- **Two limits, not one.** A video is 50 MB (`TELEGRAM_VIDEO_MAX_BYTES`); a photo, a voice note and a picture sent as a file stay at 10 MB (`TELEGRAM_SMALL_FILE_MAX_BYTES`), told apart in ONE function in `wire.ts`, `telegramFileMax(kind)`, which the plan (`planTelegramFile`), the keeper, the bridge's download and the door's ceiling (the larger of the two) all ask. One shared 50 MB was turned down: a picture is read whole to be drawn and a voice note is a few minutes, so 50 MB would only have let an oversized picture through, and the extra memory it costs is paid only by a video.
- **Every limit on the path was checked at 50 MB.** The bridge holds a video whole in memory while it copies it, which the guest's unit did not allow at its old ceilings: they are raised (the figures and the reasoning are written once, with the bridge's own deployment notes), and the download and upload timeouts are five minutes each (they were two). Cloudflare's request body limit is 100 MB on the Free and Pro plans; the Worker is 128 MB; **a Durable Object's RPC carries 32 MiB at most** (Cloudflare's documented limit on a serialised call). That last one decides the design: the door no longer reads the body, it hands the request's body stream on to the keeper as the stream it arrived as, with the `Content-Length` it carries (a request with none is read in memory up to 10 MB, as a photo is, and refused with 411 past that; 413 past 50 MB before a byte is read), and the keeper writes the stream to the bucket through a `FixedLengthStream` (R2 writes a stream only of a known length), which also fails the write if the file is not the length it said; nothing of a file is held whole in the space.
- **Read in parts, never whole.** `Mailroom.openTelegramFile` reads a Telegram file by a ranged `bucket.get`, so a request reads only its bytes: a file of at most 10 MiB comes whole as it always did; a voice note or a video asked for with a `Range` (`a-b`, `a-`, `-n`) comes as that part, cut to 8 MiB (`bytes=0-` over a 50 MB video answers its first part, as a server may; the browser asks again); a request with no range, several ranges or a save, for a file over 10 MiB, comes whole, **joined by the space from successive parts** (`fileResponse`: one part is held at a time, a part that does not follow on ends the stream with an error and never a file with a hole in it). A range past the end is 416; the object in the bucket must be the length the store holds, or the read is refused. The first 32 bytes are read by a ranged get of their own for the type, because the type is what THEY say whichever part is asked for. A part no longer waits on the one-at-a-time chain of mail reads (`keptReads`): it parses nothing and is at most 8 MiB, so a playing video no longer holds up the thumbnails behind it. A WHOLE read (a picture or voice note of up to 10 MiB, or a video up to that) still takes its turn on the chain, because a conversation of pictures asks for a dozen at once and each holds up to 10 MiB in a 128 MB heap. The range grammar is written once, in the company layer (`packages/workspace`: `parseByteRange`, `resolveByteRange`); the space parses the header, the inbox resolves it against the length it holds.
- **Videos left for being too large are copied once.** The space already holds each file's length, so nothing is read again from Telegram: the first `work` request of a bridge that takes files runs a pass (`oweVideosWithinLimit`, store/telegram.ts) that turns every video held as `left` / `too-large` with a stated length of at most 50 MB into `owed`, with its row, and writes the `setting` row `telegram-video-cap:<the limit in bytes>`. The pass is idempotent (it touches only what is still left for being too large), runs once per limit (the limit is in the key, so changing it runs it again), and does not touch a video left for another reason, one in a channel or a bot's chat, one with no stated length or one deleted for everyone.
- **A bridge says it takes the larger videos, and the order of the two deploys does not matter.** The work request carries `videoMax` (the bridge sends `TELEGRAM_VIDEO_MAX_BYTES`). A bridge from before this change sends none and would answer "too large" for any video over 10 MB, for good, so the space (1) starts the pass above only for a work request that says `videoMax` at 50 MB or more, and so does not spend its one run on the old bridge, and (2) offers a video over 10 MB (an owed row marked `big`, written when the message is planned) only to such a bridge, leaving it out in the query so twelve of them cannot crowd the old bridge's small files out of its ten places. With the space applied first, the old bridge keeps copying what it always did and the big videos wait owed (for the week a row is kept) until the new bridge is applied; with the bridge first, the space from before refuses a 10 to 50 MB upload at its door, but the new bridge's `videoMax` is unknown to it and it offers none that large. The bridge's unit file changes (the memory ceilings), so its apply restarts the service.

**33. A mailbox on the install's own mail server, read and written over JMAP, two-way. Migration 18 (store version 19), one-way.** The inbox reads and sends through a mailbox on a Stalwart server (a JMAP mail server): the same mail in a mail app and in the space, read and closed the same both ways, replies from the space in the mailbox's Sent. A fourth mailbox kind, `jmap`, beside Gmail, Cloudflare and Telegram.

- **The server is the mailbox of record, as Gmail is (decisions 4 and 5).** Read and Close are made on the server first and the inbox stores what it then says; the other shape (the inbox's own stamps copied to the server afterwards) was turned down because a phone and the space could disagree and the inbox would hold state no mail app shows. Open marks the thread seen, Close moves the thread's messages in play from the Inbox to Archive, Reopen moves them back; a refusal changes nothing and says why. Bulk and the spam call stay the inbox's own and are never written to the server. Mail the server files as junk stays in its Junk and is not brought in (j); a flag is kept with the message and not shown (k).
- **Its code is a JMAP module beside the Mailroom, not a generalisation of it.** What the Mailroom holds is Gmail's (history, watch, grant, the calendar pass) and changing it would risk the owner's live Gmail for nothing. What is shared is the store's label-shaped path: at its edge `src/jmap/mapping.ts` turns a message's server state into the label words the store derives read and closed from. Each place the store asked "is this Gmail" now asks one named question of the kind (`src/store/kinds.ts`), so the fifth kind is one file to read.
- **Migration 18** (17 was taken by decision 29): the `mailbox` table made again for the fourth kind (as 8 and 16 did), `message.jmap_id` and `conversation.jmap_thread_id` (each unique per mailbox, as Gmail's ids are; Gmail's columns keep meaning Gmail), and the push subscription kept as a `setting` row. Bookmark before the deploy; an undo build keeps migrations 12 to 18.
- **Declared in code** (`INBOX_JMAP_MAILBOXES`, as the Cloudflare mailboxes are): address, name (the pill), the server's JMAP door, and the NAME of the Worker secret that holds the mailbox's own key. An invalid declaration declares none and says why once; a mailbox taken out keeps its row and mail and says it is not synced.
- **Push, not polling.** A first probe found that Stalwart 1.0.3 pushes to an https address (RFC 8620 push subscriptions: a verification code the client completes, then a state-change POST within about two seconds). So the inbox holds a subscription per mailbox, the server calls the existing public push hostname on a path that names the mailbox and carries a random token kept in the store, and the door answers at once and only owes a sync. A push is a hint: the cursor (the server's Email and Mailbox state strings, written with the page it follows) is the truth, and what is asked is always "what changed since the cursor". The subscription lapses in 7 days: the daily pass renews it, makes it again if the server dropped it, and syncs once from the cursor, so a dropped push costs a day at worst. A stream held open from the Durable Object was turned down (it would keep the whole inbox awake and be opened again after every deploy); polling every two minutes is the fallback if push fails and was not needed.
- **A refused key is a state and never retried.** The server bans an address permanently after 100 failed sign-ins in a day, and the space reaches it from the same loopback address as the ingest Worker, so a revoked key retried would have stopped incoming mail. Loopback is exempt now (`AllowedIp`), and the inbox never retries a refused credential anyway: `refused`, ended by a deploy that changes the key, the door or its token (compared by a fingerprint), shown amber with the reason.
- **Two credentials, neither the superuser's.** The door is a hostname of its own on the existing tunnel that serves the JMAP paths only, behind an Access application and service token of their own (the ingest's are untouched, and either can be withdrawn alone). Behind it the inbox signs in with an API key on that one mailbox limited to the JMAP calls the allow-list names (`src/jmap/allow.ts`, pinned by a test and by `packages/infra/test/jmap-door.test.ts` against the key's permissions), so a leak of the token alone opens nothing. What the module may ask of the server is that short list: nothing creates or removes a folder, copies a message or deletes one but the one unsent copy of a refused submission, so the space can never delete mail on the server.
- **Sending is the third transport** (decisions 13, 15 and 18) through the same plan, claim, single attempt and filing: one call makes the message (From the mailbox's address and declared name, a Message-ID the inbox chose and recorded at the claim) and submits it, with the server asked to file it in Sent. Refused puts the draft back and removes the unsent copy; anything else stays `sending` and says "check the mailbox's Sent"; "it did not go out" is refused when the server's copy with the claimed Message-ID is already held. No files go from it; the draft chat is refused on its conversations. **The owner's instruction rule is unchanged and entirely the store's**: nothing in the JMAP module knows who is sending.
- **How the walks and the send are kept safe** (the review of the phase): the fill and a resync page by the time of the last message read (the time is inclusive, so the last one is read again), not by position, because a position over a live query skips a message when one ahead of it moves out of the folders meanwhile; a push subscription whose verification has not come in ten minutes is removed and made again; a send is refused only on an explicit refusal (a method that did not run, or a `notCreated`), and every other shape of answer, a missing or unnamed one included, is an unknown outcome that keeps the draft `sending` and removes nothing; the allow-list knows the folders from the server's own list and lets a move go only between the Inbox and the Archive, a message be made only in Drafts, a submission file it only in Sent, and only the refused submission's unsent copy be destroyed; and the mail server's pass runs in the object's alarm even when Gmail's run failed (Gmail's failure is still thrown afterwards, so the runtime retries as before).
- **Not in this decision:** the calendar pass for its mail (a newer update of an event does not close the older ones), files, and the spam check's Clef score. The Cloudflare account's 200 sends a day are shared with the first and second business mailboxes' replies.

**34. A picture goes out through the bridge, which fetches it through its own door. No migration, no new binding or secret.** The owner attaches or pastes ONE picture into a Telegram reply box (a person's chat or a group; a channel has no box) and it goes out as a Telegram photo as the owner, the words typed under it as its caption. This **amends three sentences**: decision 27's "it cannot read anything out" (the bridge's token now reads the picture of the send it is being offered, below), decision 31's "no files" (a Telegram send may carry one picture) and decision 32's "nothing is ever sent to Telegram as a file" (one picture is uploaded, for the picture send alone). The send rules of decision 31 all hold for a picture; part two below the line extends decision 15 to a draft that carries one (Claude's instructed send names the picture).

*The limits, in the contract.* One picture per message, a PNG or a JPEG **decided by its first bytes** (`sniffPicture`, `wire.ts`), by the space's page before the press, by the inbox at the send and again by the bridge before the upload, never by a name or the type a browser gave; at most **10 MB** (`TELEGRAM_PICTURE_MAX_BYTES`, a constant of its own beside the copy limit of decision 32 so a change to what is copied in never changes what may be sent: the space's page holds the same number in the company layer (`packages/workspace`), `INBOX_PICTURE_MAX_BYTES`, and a test holds the two equal); at most **1,024 characters** of words under it (`TELEGRAM_CAPTION_MAX`, Telegram's caption limit without Premium; words alone stay at 4,096; longer words with a picture are refused in the plan and never split into a second message), none at all being fine (the picture alone). The em dash rule is the caption's too. The thread says it takes a picture (`InboxThread.canPicture`, additive, true for a Telegram chat that has a reply box; `canAttach` stays the business mailboxes').

*How a picture travels.* (1) the owner presses Send with a picture: the space posts the form it posts for a business mailbox's files (`/api/email/files`), the words may be empty when a file is there; the inbox's `Mailroom.sendViaTelegram` checks the bytes (one picture, within the limit, PNG or JPEG), plans the send (every check of decision 31, with the caption's limit in place of 4,096), writes the picture to the raw bucket under **the draft's own key, `tg/<draft id>`** (the object first, `putPicture`) and claims the draft, which in the same transaction **names the picture on the draft** (its file entry: a kept photo, its true length and its SHA-256 `sha256`, its fingerprint; `raw_key`), and owes the bridge the send under its random id (the owed row now also holds the picture's type, size and fingerprint). A claim that fails deletes the object again. (2) The bridge's work request says `pictures: true`; only then is a send that carries a picture offered, with `picture: { type, size }` beside the words (an older bridge would send the caption alone; a send never offered goes back to a draft with its picture after five minutes, as in decision 31). (3) The bridge **fetches the bytes on the door's seventh call**, `POST /api/picture?account&message&random` (the send's id and the random id it was offered under; the answer is the bytes, `no-store`). The store answers only while that very send is owed, holds a picture, has been offered and may still be offered (**five minutes from its first offer plus the fetch's limit**), for a chat the bridge may write to, and the keeper reads the object only when it is the length, the kind and the fingerprint the draft names (`TelegramMediaKeeper.readTelegramPicture`; anything else is a 4xx and the bridge sends nothing). The call changes nothing (an unfollowed call) and Claude's door has no such route. (4) The bridge uploads the picture and sends it as a photo under the random id (below); (5) the outcome settles the draft as for words, and the draft becomes the sent message, **keeping the picture it already names**. If the copy in the bridge's own stream arrives first, `adoptStreamCopy` matches it as a photo whose words are the draft's caption (a picture with NO caption is never matched from the stream, since its copy's only words are Telegram's own label "[Photo]", which any captionless photo the owner sent from the owner's phone in that chat meanwhile also has: it waits for the bridge's outcome, which names the message, or for the owner), the held message takes the draft's key and file entry, its own owed download is dropped, and the draft row goes: one object, no second download, shown once. A copy that had already kept an object of its own keeps it, and the draft's is deleted.

*Every way a draft goes away deletes its picture.* `releasePicture` (store/telegram.ts) is the one place a row that names a `tg/` key is removed from: it writes the drop row of decision 32 (`telegram-media-drop:<key>`) unless some message still names that key, in the same transaction as the removal, and the Durable Object sweeps after the call (`SWEEPS_FILES_AFTER`, durable.ts). It covers a draft discarded or deleted (`deleteDraft`, which a composer's draft whose send was refused also goes through), a stuck send the owner says went out, and a send settled by its stream copy. The sweep also forgets a drop row whose key a message names again.

*The time a send may still land* grows by the picture's fetch and upload limits (a minute each, `SEND_FETCH_TIMEOUT_MS`, `SEND_UPLOAD_TIMEOUT_MS`), for every send, one rule: **13.5 minutes** from its first offer in place of 11.5 (decision 31's arithmetic, plus the two).

*The bridge.* `doSend` keeps its locks in order (a carried chat, the words' length, now the caption's when a picture is named, the key's shape, not a channel) and adds two for a picture (the offer's type and size within the limit before anything is fetched, and the fetched bytes' own first bytes and length). Then, in the heartbeat, one send at a time in the order offered (each carries `leftMs`, how much of its five minutes is left when the space answers, and the bridge starts none once that long has passed since it asked, so a slow picture ahead of a send cannot carry it past the window the 13.5-minute bound counts on): **fetch** (a refusal means the send is no longer offered: nothing sent, nothing reported; a space that does not answer is a passing trouble for the next offer), **upload** (`client.uploadFile`, one worker; **an upload that does not finish in its minute ends the process**, as an unanswered send does, because GramJS retries a part by itself when the connection drops with no end of its own: nothing outward-facing has happened, since uploaded parts are visible to no one until a send names them, and the space offers the send again under the same random id), **send** (`messages.SendMedia` with `InputMediaUploadedPhoto`, the caption as plain words, the stored random id and nothing else, built once in `sendPhotoRaw`; its 30 seconds, and its answer read by `sentOf` as for words). The allow-list grows by exactly the request `messages.SendMedia`, the type `InputMediaUploadedPhoto` and the client method `uploadFile`; `test/boundary.test.ts` pins that each is built or called once, from where, in what order, after which locks, and that nothing sends an album (`SendMultiMedia`), a file as a document or any request of the `upload` namespace. The codes Telegram answers a photo with that mean nothing went out (a group that forbids pictures, a picture it will not take for its dimensions, a caption too long) are reported failed with their code and said in words on the draft, which keeps its picture; the codes are Telegram's documented ones and which a real send meets is the hand test. The probe gains `send-picture` to Saved Messages (its alone).

*Consequences.* **The door gives something out for the first time**: a leaked bridge token can read the picture of a send while that send is offered (five minutes plus the fetch's limit), named by a draft id and a 62-bit random id it was told. That is the narrowest way to hand the bridge a file and the one the owner took (a second key on the bridge's machine was considered and rejected). A slow picture holds the bridge's other sends for its seconds (each step bounded, so the heartbeat never shows the amber line). Telegram shrinks a photo it is sent, so small print in a large screenshot may soften (the owner chose the photo, like the owner's phone). **Deploy order is not strict**: re-apply `bridge-app` (its flag says it sends pictures), then apply `space`; either order loses nothing, since an older bridge is never offered a picture send, and a new bridge on an older space sends a flag the older parser ignores (it keeps only the fields it knows) and is offered no picture send. Its hand tests are in `docs/runbooks/inbox-telegram-bridge.md`.

*Part two: pictures on drafts, and Claude's instructed send of one. No migration.* A Telegram draft can carry one picture (the same limits and the same key, `tg/<draft id>`). **Claude puts one on** with `PUT /api/drafts/:id/picture?rev=` (the picture as the body, read with the 10 MB ceiling) and its script's `picture <draft> <rev> <file|none>` (one command for both; `none` is `DELETE …/picture?rev=`, which is `updateDraft` with `takeOutPicture`); **The owner puts one on** from the draft card's Edit through the part's one new method `putDraftPicture` (the draft, the revision shown, the file) and takes it out with the new field of the existing edit, `InboxDraftEdit.takeOutPicture`, which may be saved together with new words. Both are **edits of the draft like an edit of its words, named by the revision**: the revision rises, `updated_by` says who, the audit holds a `draft.update` entry by the actor, and so decision 15's "nobody but Claude changed the draft after the instruction" sees a picture the owner put on, replaced or took out after the owner spoke (and does not hold Claude's own against it). Refused for a draft that is not a draft, is on no Telegram chat or on a channel or the service chat or Saved Messages, and at another revision; the bytes are checked by their first bytes and the limit. The object is written first (`Mailroom.putDraftPicture`, on the Mailroom's chain so a put never interleaves with a send of the same draft) and the store names it after, in one transaction (`commitDraftPicture`); a put over another replaces it under the same key (the old bytes are read first and put back if the commit fails, so the object always matches the fingerprint the row names), a take-out deletes the object through `releasePicture` and the sweep. The words of a draft that carries a picture may be none; they are needed again once it is taken out.

*What Claude is told.* `drafts` (and every answer that is a message) prints `picture: { type, size, fingerprint }` on a message that carries one of the inbox's own pictures, derived from the file entry; **the bytes are never in any answer Claude can read** (the kept-file read refuses its token; the draft's picture is readable by the owner through the same route as any kept file, for a draft in `draft` or `sending` status). **Claude's send of a draft that carries a picture names it**: `send` takes `picture`, the fingerprint `drafts` printed (`SEND_FIELDS` grew by this one field, a check and not content: the send still carries no words of its own). None named for a draft that has one is 422, another fingerprint 409, a fingerprint named for a draft that has none 409, and nothing is claimed or offered in any of them; the match is by the SHA-256 of the stored bytes, case-insensitive. Everything else of decision 15 holds (the switch, the recorded instruction, nothing arrived after it, nobody but Claude changed the draft after it). The picture that went out on the owner's word is kept with the send in the audit entries of the claim and of the send (`picture`: the fingerprint) and on the sent message's own file entry, which `sent-by-claude` reads (its messages carry `picture`); no column. The script's help says to tell the owner which picture it is (the file attached) and that the owner can see it on the draft's card before asking the owner to send it. The owner's own press on a draft's card sends it as it stands, at the revision shown, with no fingerprint to name (the owner sees the picture).

*The space.* The draft card shows the picture (the owner's own attachment route for the draft's id, at the draft's revision so a replaced picture is not shown from the browser's memory) and says whose it is ("Claude attached this picture"); Edit attaches, pastes or takes out the picture as the reply box does, saved as one call for the picture and one edit for the words, the picture first. The page keeps the reply box's rules (one picture, PNG or JPEG by its first bytes, 10 MB, 1,024 characters under it, said before Save). `InboxDraft.picture` (type and length) is additive. Its hand tests are in the same runbook.

**35. A draft can carry a Cc the author typed, beside "copy everyone". No migration, no new binding or secret.** Until now a draft's Cc could come only from `copyEveryone` (everyone on the message it answers), so the owner could not put a new person on a reply, and a second email had to be sent. A draft of kind `email` now takes `cc`, a list of addresses, when it is made (`DraftInput.cc`) and by an edit (`DraftPatch.cc`, which REPLACES the whole Cc; `[]` takes every address off): Claude's draft route and its script's `draft` and `edit`, and the owner's card through the space's draft edit (`InboxDraftEdit.cc`, a Cc box on the card's Edit). The Cc itself stays where a draft's Cc always was, the message row's `cc_header`, written as `a@x.example, b@y.example`.

*What is refused, and what is dropped.* A `cc` that is not a list (null included), a list with any entry that is not an email address (printable ASCII, one `@`, a dot after it: the shape a send needs, so a bad one is told now and not at the send), a Cc that with the recipient comes to more than 20 people (`INBOX_MOST_RECIPIENTS`, the send's own limit, said at the draft or the edit and not only at the send) and any non-empty `cc` on a draft that is not an email (a text, a direct message, a Telegram message) are refused, 422, with nothing drafted or changed (the list's format is also checked by the router in front of the store, `parseCcList`, because after a deploy the store's object can answer with the code before it for some minutes, and a store that was never told about `cc` would otherwise make the draft and drop the list): no contact is made for the address of a refused draft, and a refused edit leaves the row and its revision as they were. What is dropped without a word, because a send would refuse it or deliver it twice: duplicates, the draft's own recipient, and the sending mailbox's own address by the one rule the send applies (`isOurs`, `src/store/kinds.ts`: for a business mailbox or a mailbox on the install's own mail server its address and every address on its domain; for the owner's Gmail every address of the owner's). The owner's Gmail address copied on a reply that goes out from a business mailbox stays, as the send has always allowed. Names around an address are dropped (`Ann <ann@x.example>` is `ann@x.example`), as the copy-everyone list has always been bare addresses.

*How it combines with `copyEveryone`: the draft keeps which addresses were typed.* A draft's Cc state is two facts: the addresses the author TYPED, and whether "copy everyone" is ON. They are kept in one `setting` row beside the draft (`draft-cc:<message id>`, JSON, present only while there is something to say, deleted with the draft: no table, no migration, as the compose keys are kept). What "copy everyone" added is the rest of the Cc. `MessageRow` carries them as `copiesEveryone` and `ccTyped` (and the space's `InboxDraft.copiesEveryone`, which is the card's tick box). A draft from before the state was kept reads as copying everyone when it has a Cc and as having typed none of it, which is what it was, since a Cc could then come from nowhere else. The rules: a draft made with both has the typed addresses first and the thread's people after them, each once. In an edit, `copyEveryone: true` adds the thread's people as they are NOW after the typed addresses; `copyEveryone: false` leaves exactly the typed list, whoever the thread names by then (a person copied earlier who has since dropped off the thread does not stay); a `cc` replaces the whole Cc. An edit that names a `cc` and a tick together takes all of that list as typed. An edit that names only a `cc` while "copy everyone" is on keeps the people already copied as copied for as long as the list still holds all of them, and the addresses beyond them are typed; take one of them off and what is left is the author's own list, with the tick off. A typed address that is also one of the people is typed, so it survives an untick. A Cc nobody changed (an edit of the words alone) is left as stored. The space's tick box shows the inbox's `copiesEveryone` and not "the Cc is not empty"; the page changes one of the two per save (the Cc box is read-only while the tick is changed).

*It is an edit like any other, and a change of recipients voids the instruction.* A change of the Cc is `updateDraft`: it raises the revision and is audited as `draft.update` by the actor, so decision 15 reads it as it reads a change of the words: a send at the old revision is 409, and a draft whose Cc anyone but Claude changed after the owner's instruction is refused to Claude's send (409, "go back to the owner"). Beyond that, **a change of who the draft goes to (its To or Cc) after the instruction voids it whoever made it, Claude's own included** (409 with words of their own, `recipientsChangedAfterInstruction`): the owner agreed to a message for those people, and a Cc Claude added after "send it" is not one they agreed to. **So does a draft created after the instruction, by Claude too** (`draftCreatedAfterInstruction`): an instruction covers a draft that existed when it was given, so Claude cannot make a new draft with a stranger on its Cc and send it under an older "send it". **And so does anything else the audit records on the draft after it** (`draftRecordChangedAfterInstruction`): the Outbox import (`outbox.carry`, whose `created` time is Claude's own input and is never trusted: the audit's time is the inbox's), a draft marked sent and put back (`message.unmark-sent`), a stuck send resolved, a chat with the assistant. The check is an allow-list: after the instruction ANY audit entry on the draft voids it, except Claude's own `draft.update` that changes neither the recipient nor the Cc (the body-only edit decision 15 allows) and the send path's own `message.sending` and `message.send-refused` (a refused send is put back as it was, and Claude may try again on the same word). It is found in the audit by comparing the people before and after each `draft.update` after the instruction (the set of Cc addresses and the recipient; order, case and names do not matter), so an edit of Claude's that leaves the same people does not void it, and neither does one of its edits of the words. `test/draft-cc.test.ts` holds each case.

*Delivery.* Every transport reads the stored Cc through the one `checkRecipients` and hands it on (Gmail's raw message, the `send_email` binding's `cc`, the mail server's submission), which refuses an address that is ours, one that appears twice, one that is not bare and more than `INBOX_MOST_RECIPIENTS` people; the tests send a typed Cc through each. "Copy everyone" as decision 13 words it (choice (b)) is unchanged: it copies the people on the message being answered.
